# Stars, their priors, and the bodies constrained against them

`src/exozippy/components/star/`, `components/galacticmodel/`, `components/mann/`,
`components/torres/` and the planet-side mass coordinate in `components/planet/`: the mass
priors (IMF, the FFP mass function), the distance prior, evolutionary-track readiness, the
empirical stellar and planetary relations, the `linear` vs `log_q` planet mass
parameterization, and the planet radius prior (uniform in R_P/R_*).

Read this before changing a prior on a stellar quantity, adding an empirical relation, or
touching the planet mass coordinate or the planet radius prior. Related: `src/exozippy/components/parameter.md` (how a
component declares the prior it adds, and the element roles the mode tables use),
`src/exozippy/components/mulensing/mulensing.md` (the lens-side consumer).

## Stellar mass priors: IMF and the FFP mass function (`components/galacticmodel/`)

`galacticmodel.build_likelihood` adds one `imf_prior` potential, a `pt.sum` over the **whole** `stars.logmass` vector -- lens and source alike, no mask. (By default -- `present_day: true`, review 1.7.2 -- the stars on the stellar IMF take a present-day mass function INSIDE the kinematic mixture instead and contribute 0 here; only `present_day: false`, or an `ffp` star, puts a nonzero term in it. See "The present-day mass function" below.) Two independent choices feed it: the galacticmodel block's `IMF:` (which stellar IMF, system-wide) and each star block's `mass_function:` (whether that star draws the stellar IMF at all).

**The IMF is a density in the BIRTH mass, and `star.logmass` is the PRESENT-DAY mass.** For most stars the two are the same number. A star on a MIST track is not: the evolutionary model samples its present-day mass and reads the track at the initial mass that implies (`components/evolutionarymodel/evolutionarymodel.md`, 3-9% heavier for a clump star below 1.5 solMass). So the potential -- the IMF, and the present-day mass function (`present_day:`) inside the population mixture -- reads each star's birth mass through `Component.initial_logmass` (`galacticmodel._birth_logmass`, `set_subtensor` over the claimed stars; two components claiming one star raise), and the component that supplies it adds the Jacobian `log |d log M_ini / d log M|` itself. With no such component the vector is `star.logmass` unchanged.

**`IMF: chabrier | Salpeter`**, default `chabrier`; anything else **raises**. It has to raise: the key selected `KROUPA_IMF_SLOPE` / `SALPETER_IMF_SLOPE` for a power law that had not been applied since the Chabrier lognormal replaced it, so every `IMF: Salpeter` in a config was a silent no-op. `Kroupa` is still unsupported on purpose -- it is a **broken** power law (1.3 below 0.5 solMass, 2.3 above) needing a piecewise, continuity-matched density, and `KROUPA_IMF_SLOPE` is only its low-mass segment.

- **The change of variables is the trap.** The potential applies to `stars.logmass`, i.e. it is a density in `x = log10(M)`, while Salpeter is quoted as `dN/dM ~ M^-alpha`. So `dN/dx = M^-alpha * M ln10 = ln10 * 10^((1-alpha)x)`: the slope in the sampled coordinate is `(1 - 2.35) = -1.35` per dex (`-3.108` nats/dex), **not** `-2.35`. `SALPETER_IMF_SLOPE = -2.35` is the **signed mass-space exponent**, not an already-converted log-space slope -- hence the `+ 1.0` in the code. Do not "simplify" it away.
- **Both branches are normalized over the same support**, read through the one `_sampled_bounds` helper (`star.logmass`'s hard bounds), so switching IMF moves logp by a meaningful amount rather than an arbitrary offset. Two archived-baseline moves have come out of that constant: **-0.3568 nats per star** when the lognormal was first normalized, and **+0.0029 per star** when the high-mass tail landed. Unreadable/non-finite bounds fall back to unnormalized with a warning rather than failing a fit.

**The `chabrier` branch is the PIECEWISE Chabrier IMF, tail included** (`chabrier_logmass_logp`, the one place the form lives -- for both backends, `xp=pt` for the potential and `xp=np` for its own normalizer). Until 2026-08 it was the lognormal **segment** alone applied over the whole `[-9, 2.5]` support, under a comment saying one "would smoothly match it to a Salpeter tail" -- in the conditional, describing what one could do. Nobody had (review 3.7.1). The lognormal steepens without limit above its peak: its slope in log mass is `-(x - log Mc)/sigma^2`, i.e. **-2.02 nats/dex at 1 solMass and -5.10 at 10**, against the real tail's constant `-1.3*ln10 = -2.99`. So massive stars were over-penalized and the error *grew with mass* -- and it is not only a massive-lens concern, because the potential is one `pt.sum` over the whole `stars.logmass` vector, sources included, and bulge source stars sit near 1 solMass. Measured: a 10 solMass star was charged 4.03 nats relative to a 0.5 solMass one where it should be 3.46.

- **The tail's amplitude is fixed by CONTINUITY at 1 solMass**, not by Chabrier's separately quoted normalization. The two are equivalent up to the additive constant the normalizer removes anyway, and stating it as continuity makes the C0 property manifest rather than a coincidence of two rounded numbers.
- **The join is SMOOTHED, and that is a deliberate departure from the published form.** The true IMF is only C0 there -- the slope jumps by 0.97 nats/dex, a logp **kink** of the same class as the SHO kernel's `Q = 1/2` switch -- and the sampler has to cross it. Blending over a width `D` deviates from the exact piecewise form by roughly `D * 0.97 / 8`, so `CHABRIER_BLEND_WIDTH_DEX = 0.2` costs **0.014 nats** (measured; predicted 0.024). Against that, Chabrier's own high-mass exponent is uncertain at `x = 1.3 +/- 0.3`, i.e. `+/-0.69` nats/dex of slope, ~0.7 nats across a decade. The smoothing is ~50x smaller than the IMF's own uncertainty and cannot be the limiting error. A third of a dex from the join the deviation is already below 1e-3 nats.
- **The blend is a WEIGHTED SUM of the two densities**, `w(x)*lognormal + (1-w(x))*powerlaw`, never a `pt.where` over the two branch log-densities. Both branches are finite everywhere here, so a `where` would not actually produce the documented NaN -- which is exactly the point of not using one: the weighted sum has no branch selection at all, so the trap is unreachable by construction rather than by argument. It is evaluated as `logaddexp(log w + L, log(1-w) + P)` -- that sum exactly, in the form that does not underflow (at the -9 dex floor the lognormal is `exp(-107)` while the unweighted tail is ~1e11).
- **`softplus`, not a hand-rolled `maximum(t,0) + log1p(exp(-|t|))`.** The hand-rolled form is stable but its derivative at exactly `t = 0` comes out of `maximum`'s tie-break instead of the true 0.5 -- a gradient artifact planted at the one point the smoothing exists for. `pt.softplus` gives 0.5 there; numpy's stable spelling is `np.logaddexp(0, t)`.
- **The normalizer is a numerical QUADRATURE now**, because the blended density has no closed form. One trapezoid on a dense uniform grid per element, computed **once, in numpy, at build time** -- legitimate only because `star.logmass`'s bounds are **static**; a dynamic (linked) bound there would break it. `_CHABRIER_QUAD_POINTS = 100001` puts `dx ~ 1.2e-4` dex on the default support, 380x finer than the 0.045 dex blend scale, and 4x the points moves `log Z` by < 1e-12.
- The analytic `_lognormal_log_norm` it replaced is **deleted**, but its lesson is not: it built the truncated mass from **erf/erfc with a per-side branch** and never as a difference of `Phi` CDFs, because this support straddles the Chabrier mean at -14.6 and +5.5 sigma, where `1-eps` minus `1-eps'` discards nearly every significant digit. Quadrature sums positive numbers and has no such subtraction, so the cancellation cannot recur -- but anyone tempted to restore a closed form for speed needs the warning. The analytic form survives in `tests/test_galactic_model.py`, where it validates the quadrature on a support entirely below the join (agreement 1.7e-9).
- **The reported label and the prose name both segments.** The table used to read "Chabrier (2003) IMF" and the draft "\citet{Chabrier:2003} lognormal" while the code applied only the low-mass half -- so neither would have caught this. See "Reporting component-added priors" in `parameter.md`.
- **`IMF: Salpeter` auto-raises `star.logmass`'s lower bound** to the hydrogen-burning limit, 0.075 solMass = -1.1249 dex (Chabrier & Baraffe 2000), per star, with a warning. Under chabrier the defaults.yaml floor is inert (density there is ~exp(-107)); under a power law rising 3.11 nats/dex without limit it becomes the answer instead of a safety rail. 0.075 is also `components/mann`'s low-mass boundary, so one constant serves both. This is not a claim that Salpeter is accurate down there -- Salpeter (1955) fit ~0.4-10 solMass -- but truncating at 0.4 would exclude the M dwarfs that dominate real microlensing lenses.

**`mass_function: imf | ffp`** on the **star** block, default `imf` (byte-for-byte the pre-2026-08 model). `ffp` swaps that star, and only that star, onto the free-floating-planet mass function. The choice is per star because the realistic system is a stellar **source** plus an FFP **lens**, and one `pt.sum` covers both; it lives on the star block rather than as a galacticmodel key listing star names because that puts it next to the mass it describes and next to the other per-star switches (`mist:`, `parsec:`), resolvable by the star's own name through machinery `Component` already has. Both spellings work: `mass_function: ffp`, or `mass_function: {kind: ffp, alpha: 1.25}`. Unknown kinds and unknown sub-keys raise -- a silently ignored mass-function key is exactly the bug above.

- **Sumi et al. 2023** (AJ 166, 108; arXiv:2303.08280), MOA-II 9-year survey: `dN/dlog M = 2.18(+0.52/-1.40) * (M / 8 M_Earth)^-alpha dex^-1 star^-1`, `alpha = 0.96(+0.47/-0.27)`, fit over `0.33 < M/M_Earth < 6660`. The often-quoted **`21(+23/-13)` is `f`, the integrated number of FFPs per star over that range -- not the power law's amplitude**, which is 2.18. Do not put 21 in as `Z`.
- **No Jacobian, and that is the point.** This form is already a density in log mass and `star.logmass` is already the sampled coordinate, so `log p(x) = -alpha*ln10*x - log Z` drops straight in. Contrast Salpeter above, which needs `|dM/dx|`. Applying `(1 - alpha)` here would be wrong by a whole factor of M; `tests/test_ffp_mass_function.py` pins the per-dex slope at exactly `-alpha*ln10`, which is normalization-independent and so isolates precisely that error.
- **Only `alpha` is exposed**, because only `alpha` can do anything. Normalizing over the sampled support (the same `_power_law_log_norm` the Salpeter branch uses, generalized to a per-star `k`) makes the pivot mass `M_norm` a pure additive constant the normalizer cancels exactly -- exposing it would be a knob that provably does nothing. The amplitude (equivalently `f`) is an **abundance**, a rate per star: it could only matter to a model weighing the FFP and stellar lens populations against each other (a mixture, or a population-weighted event rate -- and note the event-rate factor lives in `lens.py`, not here). Deliberately **not** implemented. `alpha` is exposed because the measurement is uncertain and Roman will revise it, and tracking a new fit must not require a source edit.
- **The support is advised, not clamped.** Selecting `ffp` changes no bound; it warns, once per star and **only where `lower` is still the default** (a user who set it -- numerically or by link -- has already decided, and warning them anyway teaches people to ignore warnings). The warning carries what is needed to choose: the density rises at `alpha*ln10 = 2.21` nats/dex, so 90% of the prior mass sits within `1/alpha = 1.04` dex of whatever the bound is (exact, since `1 - 10^-1 = 0.9`); -9 dex is ~2 Ceres masses; set `star.<name>.logmass: {lower: ...}`, e.g. Sumi's own -6.0039 dex (0.33 M_Earth); bounds may only be tightened. An `ffp` star is also **exempt from the Salpeter floor** -- opting out of the stellar IMF means opting out of its floor.
- **The asymmetry is deliberate; do not "fix" it.** Salpeter auto-raises the floor because a *stellar* IMF below the hydrogen-burning limit is being applied outside its **domain of validity** -- a correctness fix. The FFP function does not, because sub-stellar masses **are** its domain, so where to cut its support off is an ordinary prior choice belonging to the user. Clamping it would gate exactly the models the mass function exists to express. Give users rope; do not gate viable models.
- **The seam for a custom parameterization is one function**, `galacticmodel.ffp_logmass_logp`. Its docstring gives the functional form, the (absent) change of variables and when you would need to put one back, where the normalization comes from, and which two lines to replace. Nothing else in the codebase touches the form. It is deliberately a marked seam, not a plugin system.
- Worth knowing before comparing numbers: with the default -9 dex support the FFP prior is **diffuse** -- its normalizer is dominated by the floor -- so its absolute logp at 3 Mjup is actually *lower* than Chabrier's there. The meaningful statement is relative: Chabrier charges 5.25 nats for a 3 Mjup lens versus a 0.5 solMass star, while the FFP function prefers it by 4.95, a 10.2-nat swing. That is also the sharpest argument for advising rather than clamping.

**`star.logmass` bounds.** The `-9.0` floor in `star/defaults.yaml` is deliberate and load-bearing, not a sloppy wide default -- the reasoning is in the comment on the key itself; read it before tightening it. `star.mass`'s `lower`/`upper` were **deleted**: `mass` is derived (`10**logmass`), so `logmass`'s bounds are the real hard support enforced exactly by the logit transform, and bounds on `mass` could only act as soft barriers layered on top, disagreeing with it by eight orders of magnitude. To change the allowed mass range, change `logmass`.

Both potentials **declare themselves to the reporting layer** (`_declare_mass_prior`, `_declare_kinematic_prior`), so the tables name the IMF actually applied, the FFP function with the slope actually used, and the galactic model on `distance`/`pm_ra`/`pm_dec`/`rv` -- instead of "Uniform" on all of them. See "Reporting component-added priors" in `src/exozippy/components/parameter.md`. The per-element split is the point: one `pt.sum` carries two different densities when a star opts into `ffp`, and the Prior column has to say which is which.

Tests: `tests/test_galactic_model.py`, `tests/test_ffp_mass_function.py`, `tests/test_prior_reporting.py`.

## The present-day mass function: `present_day: true` (`components/galacticmodel/pdmf.py`, review 1.7.2)

**The IMF is a mass function at BIRTH; a microlensing lens is whatever object is there TODAY.** Applied as the present-day mass function, the IMF keeps every massive star alive and luminous forever, and the event rate's `theta_E ~ sqrt(M)` weight amplifies that tail: toward Baade's window the prior put 33% of rate-weighted lenses above 1 Msun (q90 4.2 Msun, up to the 316 Msun bound) and its t_E median ran 52% long against OGLE (review 7.16.3, `tests/test_prior_predictive_tE.py`). An old bulge has no main-sequence stars above ~1 Msun; what it has instead is ~20% dark remnants.

`galacticmodel: [{present_day: true}]` (a boolean, **the default since review 1.7.2**, JDE ruling 2026-10-30; `present_day: false` keeps the IMF as the present-day mass function, the pre-1.7.2 model bit for bit, for regression comparison) evolves the selected IMF (`IMF:` chabrier or Salpeter, unchanged and orthogonal) to a present-day mass function per Galactic population, with genulens's prescriptions (Koshimoto, Baba & Bennett 2021, ApJ 917, 78; its `mass_function.cpp`), which are PopSyCLE's (Lam et al. 2020, ApJ 889, 31):

    phi_b(x) = IMF(x) P_b(alive | x)  +  sum_type [remnants of the dead, mapped through the IFMR]
    P_b(alive | M_ini) = int g_b(T) [T < tau(M_ini, [Fe/H]_b)] dT

- **Ages, marginalized at build time**: thin disk star formation `exp(-(10 Gyr - T)/7 Gyr)` over 0-10 Gyr (genulens's weighting; it collapses K21's 7 age bins, as our one-layer thin disk already does), thick disk 12 Gyr, bulge 9 +/- 1 Gyr. Marginalizing is exact for the mass prior and leaves a fixed 1-D table per population.
- **Turnoff from OUR MIST v2.5 grid**: `tau(M_ini, [Fe/H])` = the age at the grid's last EEP (807, one point before the TP-AGB; the rest of a life is ~1%), committed as the 21 kB `galacticmodel/mist_lifetimes.csv` by `scripts/make_mist_lifetimes.py` so a microlensing fit does not need the 123 MB grid. One [Fe/H] per population (`pdmf.POPULATION_FEH`: thin 0, thick -0.5, bulge 0), as genulens does with PARSEC; M_die(9 Gyr, 0) = 1.08, M_die(12 Gyr, -0.5) = 0.89 Msun (genulens: ~1.0, 0.86).
- **Remnants**: white dwarfs for `M_ini < 9` with Kalirai et al. 2008, `M_WD = 0.109 M_ini + 0.394` (0.49-1.375 Msun); neutron stars and black holes above with Raithel, Sukhbold & Ozel 2018's probabilistic `P(NS | M_ini)` and mass fits, coded exactly as genulens does (`pdmf.R18_P_NS`, `_ns_mass`, `_bh_mass`). The last R18 bin (60-120 Msun) is extended to the top of our logmass support.
- **It moves INSIDE the population mixture.** The PDMF depends on the population, so `log p = log IMF(M) + log sum_b n_b f_b` becomes `log sum_b n_b f_b phi_b(M)`: one more additive term per branch inside the existing logsumexp. A 1.5 Msun lens is then a young thin-disk star or a remnant, which changes its distance prior. PDMF stars leave the `imf_prior` potential (it is 0 for them). In a linked group (below) each member carries its own mass term inside the group's shared branch.
- **Object densities.** The branch density anchors are genulens's LUMINOUS (`n0MS`) counts, so with remnants in the mass function a branch has `n_b / F_lum,b` objects (`F_lum,b` = the luminous fraction of `phi_b` over the star's support, 0.86-0.91). The model adds `-log F_lum,b` to a PDMF star's branch density, and `evidence_log_norm`'s position normalizer integrates the same densities, so the joint prior over (position, velocity, mass) stays normalized: switching `present_day` moves the normalizer by the matching amount (-0.23 nats for the Baade lens + source).
- **Smooth by construction.** The tables are LINEAR densities on a 1e-3 dex grid, convolved with a 0.02 dex Gaussian (the thick disk's single-age turnoff, the 1.375 Msun WD ceiling and the R18 bin edges become erf ramps, not cliffs), then logged with a floor of 1e-20 of the population's peak (finiteness only: a 3 Msun object has no place in the 12 Gyr thick disk between its neutron stars and black holes, but the mixture always has the young thin disk). The graph only interpolates the tables linearly (`pdmf.interp_table`): the gradient is a finite difference of adjacent entries everywhere, with no branch selection, no `pt.where` and no radicand -- pinned on the C and JAX backends through the turnoffs and the R18 gaps. Nothing is excluded: heavy masses stay reachable as remnants and young-disk stars (rope, not gates).
- **`galacticmodel.p_remnant`** (a Deterministic, one entry per PDMF star in star order): the probability the star is a dark remnant given its mass, position and velocity -- branch responsibilities times each branch's dark fraction at that mass. For a linked member it uses the star's own branch terms, an approximation to the group's shared population. It is in the trace, not yet a table row.
- **Applies to every star on the stellar IMF**, sources included (an `ffp` star keeps the FFP function). A source is selected by brightness, not by `sqrt(M)`, so neither the IMF nor the PDMF is its right prior (design Q6, open); its mass is normally pinned by its light through a relation anyway.

**What it does NOT do (phase 2, review 8.10.7):** remnants are dark in the MASS PRIOR only. A lens's light -- the blend tie, the SED -- still comes from its stellar relation whatever its mass, so a fit with a lens-light constraint still charges a 0.6 Msun white-dwarf lens a 0.6 Msun dwarf's light. Also out: remnant natal kicks (NS 350, BH 100 km/s in genulens; the dark branch shares its population's kinematics), binaries, giant-branch mass loss, a metallicity spread within a population, and a free (sampled) age.

**Validation (2026-10-30, the four sight lines of `examples/validation/prior_predictive_tE.py`).** A numpy Monte Carlo of this prior (as `tests/test_galactic_genulens.py` draws it, the lens's population picking its PDMF) against genulens 2.0 with `REMNANT 1 BINARY 0 gammaDs 2`, rate-weighted, Baade:

Through the real System and NUTS (`prior_predictive_tE.py sample --present-day`, Baade, 4 x 2500 draws): t_E q10/q50/q90 = 8.2 / 22.4 / 57.7 d against OGLE's 4.2 / 18.9 +/- 1.6 / 51.6 (genulens 18.1 median), down from 9.0 / 28.7 / 106; M_L 0.091 / 0.422 / 0.887 Msun; mean `p_remnant` of the lens 17.2%. Tests: `tests/test_pdmf.py` (normalization per population per support; a single-age 10 Gyr population's remnant number and mass fractions against an independent quadrature; the bulge white-dwarf mass range; the bulge rate-weighted quantiles and type fractions; finite C and JAX gradients through the turnoffs and R18 gaps; a System build with it on and off).

## Linked stars: one position, one density term (`components/galacticmodel/`)

`galacticmodel.kinematic_prior` is, per star, a joint density over `(distance, pm_ra, pm_dec, rv)`: a three-branch mixture (thin disk, thick disk, bulge) of number density times a velocity Gaussian, `log sum_b n_b(d) f_b(v)`, plus the `d^2` volume element and the `(K d)^2` velocity Jacobian. The branch densities live in one function, `branch_log_number_densities` (`xp=pt` for the potential, `xp=np` for the normalizer), the same two-backend pattern as `chabrier_logmass_logp`. Positions and velocities are in ONE frame -- astropy's `Galactocentric`, x mirrored into genulens's orientation (Sun at x ~ +R0) -- read off one transform; the frame, why, and the tests that pin it against astropy and genulens are in `components/galacticmodel/galacticmodel.md`.

**A star whose distance is hard-linked to another star's has no position of its own, and contributes no density term (review: evidence across 1L1S/1L2S/2L1S, 2026-10).** A binary source (1L2S) is the case that found it: `Source2`'s `distance` is `initval: star.Source.distance, sigma: 0` (`examples/DC2018/dc18_arch_config.py`), so until this fix the prior carried `log n(D_s) D_s^2 f(v)` TWICE -- a second density factor on `D_s` that is not a constant (it reweights the source distance by `n(D_s) D_s^2` once more) and so biased `ln Z(1L2S)` against 1L1S and 2L1S, by ~+9 nats at a typical bulge start. The physical system has ONE position along the line of sight, so it gets one density and one volume element.

- **Detected through the link machinery, not a microlensing special case.** `linked_star_structure` reads `Parameter.element_links["hard"]` (an `initval` link with `sigma: 0`, `Component._wire_user_links`, which now also records each link's `dep_paths`). A star whose distance link reads **exactly one other star's distance and nothing else** joins that star's GROUP; chains are followed to the root. Any other hard-linked distance (an expression of two stars, or of a non-star parameter) has no single partner, keeps its own full term, and **warns** -- its term is then a factor on the link's sources, which the user should know. Soft (`sigma > 0`) links are not links here: that star has a sampled distance of its own and is a separate position, with the soft link an extra (data-like) term.
- **One group, one population.** Per group, `log p = logsumexp_b [log n_b(d_root) + sum_k log f_b(x_free,k | x_linked,k)] + 2 log d_root + Jacobians`: the branch is shared by the whole system, because a binary's members are one population (a bulge star's companion is a bulge star). Two independent mixtures would let the members sit in different populations at the same position.
- **What a member's velocity contributes depends on which velocity coordinates are ALSO hard-linked**, and every case is an exactly normalized density over the member's free coordinates (the constants it needs are in the model, not left to `evidence_log_norm`), so a member never moves `ln Z` by a constant:
  - **all three of `pm_ra`, `pm_dec`, `rv` linked** -> nothing (its velocity IS its partner's);
  - **some linked** -> the conditional `f_b(x_free | x_linked)` = full Gaussian minus the marginal of the linked sub-block, in the sampled coordinates (Jacobian `K d` per proper motion, `1e-3` for rv). The DC2018 1L2S config links `pm_ra`/`pm_dec` but not `rv`: Source2's free rv then gets the conditional line-of-sight velocity density given the shared position, proper motion and population, which integrates to exactly 1 (the test asserts it). Nothing observes a microlensing source's rv, so linking it too is equivalent and cheaper;
  - **none linked (distance only)** -> the member's full velocity Gaussian, an independent draw from the SAME population at the SAME position. **This is the ruling for "distance linked, proper motions independent"**: a link on distance alone states only that the two stars are at one place, so the prior adds nothing the user did not say. A real binary's members also share a space velocity to within the orbital velocity (~1-30 km/s, well inside the 20-120 km/s dispersions), but that is a binary-orbit prior nobody wrote; a user who means it links the proper motions (and rv), and gets the rule above. Imposing it silently would be the code choosing a statement for the user.
- **A star in an orbit group gets these links automatically** (`Star._link_orbit_members`, `components/orbit/orbit.md` "Hierarchical orbits and body groups"): distance, `pm_ra`, `pm_dec`, `rv`, `initfeh`, `age` and `av` tie to the orbit's anchor, so the member is fully linked (all three velocity coordinates) and contributes nothing to the kinematic prior: the bound system has one position and one systemic space velocity. A Keplerian orbit adds the orbital motion on top wherever a consumer models it. A distance-only hand link to a star OUTSIDE any orbit stays distance-only: the rule below is unchanged for it.
- **The rule keys on the DISTANCE link only.** A star with a free distance and linked proper motions (a common-proper-motion pair at two distances) is its own position -- its density term stays -- and contributes the conditional velocity density of its free coordinates. A pinned (`sigma: 0` numeric) coordinate is NOT a link: pinned is a statement of a value, and its density term stays, as it always has.
- **Not a pt.where anywhere.** The per-star vectors are assembled with `pt.set_subtensor` and the groups with a constant 0/1 membership matrix (`pt.dot`), per CLAUDE.md. With no linked star the potential is the exact expression it always was: **bit-identical start logp on every shipped example that links no star's distance** (measured on all 12 shipped galacticmodel configs -- DC2018_128, KMT-2019-BLG-1806, galactic_model, kb180087_obj3, ob07224, ob08092, ob09020, ob140939, ob161003, ob161045, ogle0383, ob170114 -- and two without one, kelt4 and hat3: identical to the last printed digit). The one that does, `examples/KMT-2021-BLG-1122L` (a triple lens whose companions LensB/LensC are hard-linked to Lens on distance, pm and rv -- its params file says why), loses two spurious copies of the lens's density term; its data are not shipped, so no test builds it.
- A linked member is also outside `Star`'s own volume prior: `Star.build_likelihood` stands down wherever a galacticmodel exists, and a hard-linked distance is not a coordinate a volume element could be the density of.

**Normalization: stated, computed once, reported outside the model.** The density of one independent star (a group ROOT) in the sampled coordinates is

    p(d, pm_ra, pm_dec, rv) = sum_b n_b(d) N_b(v) * d^2 * (K d)^2 * 1e-3 / Z_pos,
    Z_pos = int_lower^upper sum_b n_b(d) d^2 dd      (d in pc, n_b in stars/pc^3)

`Z_pos` is the number of stars per steradian in the cone out to the distance bound; with each `N_b` a normalized Gaussian and the velocity Jacobian, the velocity integral is 1 and the distance integral is 1. The potential in the model is this density plus, per root, `log Z_pos + (3/2) log(2 pi) - log(1e-3)` -- the `(2 pi)^(3/2)` (identical in every branch) and the m/s -> km/s factor were always dropped. **`GalacticModel.evidence_log_norm()`** returns minus the sum of those constants (the sign convention of `outputs/evidence`: ADD it to a lnZ taken from the model logp). It is NOT added to the potential, for the reason `parameter.md` gives for the parameter-level constants (PR #465): it moves nothing a sampler sees, and putting it in would shift every start logp and every pinned-logp test. Its size is ~-36.9 nats per independent star toward the bulge (`examples/galactic_model`: -36.85 for one star; the two-star microlensing examples -72.9 to -74.8). The two configs of an evidence comparison with the same number of independent positions therefore carry the same constant exactly; a different number differs by exactly that many copies of it.

- `Z_pos` is a 1-D quadrature (`log_position_norm`, trapezoid in `ln d`, 2e5 points; 4x the points moves it by < 1e-9, and it matches `scipy.integrate.quad` to 1e-7), legitimate as a build-time constant because the sight line is frozen at the anchor (above) and the bounds are static. A dynamic distance bound warns, as `Star`'s volume normalizer does.
- A root whose distance is PINNED owes `log(sum_b n_b(d0) d0^2)` at the pin instead of `Z_pos`, which leaves its velocity mixture normalized. A DERIVED root distance (`fitpirel`'s lens) uses `Z_pos` over the static bounds: the `fitpirel_jacobian` makes the prior a density in `log_pi_rel`, and the `source_behind_lens` barrier, not this term, truncates it.
- Approximations, both below 1e-4 nats: the velocity bounds are taken as infinite (`pm +/- 1e5 mas/yr` is > 400 km/s beyond 1 pc, where `n d^2` has no weight), and the root's velocity term omits `log |det Q|` of the polar map, which the smoothed radius `sqrt(x^2+y^2+1e-6)` makes `1 - 1e-4` only within 0.1 kpc of the Galactic center. A MEMBER's term carries it exactly, because a conditional has to integrate to 1.

Tests: `tests/test_galactic_linked_stars.py` (the rule, the fully linked member adding exactly nothing, the conditional integrating to 1 over one and two free coordinates, the distance-only case against an independent scipy implementation, the normalizer against `quad`, and a 1L1S vs 1L2S `System` of one event with the identical galactic contribution and `evidence_log_norm`).

## The stellar distance prior (`components/star/star.py`)

`star.distance` carries finite `lower`/`upper` (1 mpc to 100 kpc) and no sigma, so `parameter.py`'s logit transform gave it a **uniform-in-distance** prior -- a default that fell out of the machinery, not a choice anyone made. `Star.build_likelihood` now adds the constant-space-density (volume) prior instead: `+2*log(d)`, normalized over the parameter's own hard support (`log Z = log((upper^3 - lower^3)/3)`), i.e. `p(d) ~ d^2`, `p(plx) ~ plx^-4`. Uniform-in-d is `p(plx) ~ plx^-2`; the two disagree by exactly `d^2`, negligible for a well-measured parallax and dominant below `plx/sigma ~ 10` (the Lutz-Kelker regime). One or the other always applies -- there is no config key, because there is a right answer.

**Where a `galacticmodel` block exists the star component stands down entirely.** Its `kinematic_prior` already carries `volume_element = 2*log(d)`, and it applies to `system.star.distance.value` -- the **whole** vector, no mask, lens and source and every other star alike (a star whose distance is hard-linked to another's shares that star's ONE volume element, "Linked stars" above, and has no distance coordinate a volume element could be the density of) -- so the two coverage sets are identical and a second copy would make the prior `d^4`. A galactic model is also strictly stronger than constant space density (real disk/bulge profiles along the actual sight line), so where one exists it wins outright. If galacticmodel's coverage ever narrows to a subset, star must cover the **complement** rather than stand down; `tests/test_distance_volume_prior.py` pins the coverage claim so that change cannot pass silently. Topology detection is `Star._galactic_imf` (component attr, then raw config), the same helper the Salpeter floor uses.

Details worth not rediscovering:

- **Normalized, deliberately**, even though the choice here is topology-driven rather than user-selected and so does not need PR #82/#86's "make two user-selectable IMFs comparable" argument. Three other reasons do: the prior it replaces *is* a normalized density (the logit reparameterization implies exactly `U(lower, upper)`), so dropping the constant would quietly demote `star.distance`'s prior to an unnormalized reweighting; the bounds are user-settable even though the prior is not, and today tightening `upper:` moves logp by exactly the `-log(span)` it is worth rather than by an arbitrary offset; and it is one closed-form constant with no gradient and no runtime cost. A **dynamic (linked)** `lower`/`upper` is normalized over the STATIC bounds and warns -- `build_pymc` re-maps that element's real support, so the constant does not normalize it.
- The term covers **every** element, pinned and hard-linked ones included, exactly as the galacticmodel priors do. For a pinned element it is a constant and cannot move a posterior.
- It applies **on top of** a user's Gaussian `sigma` on distance, and that is right: such an entry is a parallax *measurement*, and multiplying it by the volume prior is the standard treatment. The shift is `~2*(sigma/d)^2` in fractional distance -- 2e-4 for a 1% parallax, 18% for a 30% one, i.e. it only bites exactly where the volume prior is what you want.
- **Nothing else carries a distance Jacobian or volume term.** SED (`calc_appmag`'s distance modulus), mann (`calc_absmag`), and astrometry (parallax scaling) use distance only in forward models; `mulensevent.event_rate_prior`'s `log(mu_rel * theta_E)` is a rate *selection* factor, not a volume element, and it is applied whether or not a galacticmodel exists (its normalization: `mulensing/mulensing.md`, "The event-rate prior").
- Reading a Parameter's hard support has one owner, `parameter.sampled_bounds` (galacticmodel's IMF normalizers call it too). Unreadable/non-finite bounds fall back to unnormalized with a warning rather than failing a fit.
- `Star.build_likelihood` also **declares** the term to the reporting layer (`self.distance.add_prior_contribution(...)`), so the tables read `p(d) propto d^2 on [lower, upper]` rather than "Uniform"; where a galacticmodel exists the star component declares nothing and galacticmodel declares its own. See "Reporting component-added priors" in `src/exozippy/components/parameter.md`.

Every pinned logp for a topology with a star and no galacticmodel moves by exactly `2*sum(log d) - n_star*log Z` (`log Z = 33.44017` at the defaults.yaml bounds, so `-28.835` nats per star at the 10 pc default start). `tests/test_runaway_logp_regression.py` has a galacticmodel and is therefore the unchanged control.

Tests: `tests/test_distance_volume_prior.py`.

## Proper-motion frames: absolute `pm_ra`/`pm_dec`, Sgr-A*-relative `pm_ra_sgra`/`pm_dec_sgra`

`star.pm_ra` (`mu_alpha cos(dec)`) and `star.pm_dec` are **absolute** ICRS proper motions -- what Gaia and Roman measure, and what the Galactic kinematic prior and `mu_rel` read. On a microlensing or Galactic-model topology the star also carries the derived, reported pair `pm_ra_sgra`/`pm_dec_sgra`: the same motion **relative to Sgr A\***, i.e. minus Sgr A\*'s apparent motion (Reid & Brunthaler 2020) resolved on the local axes at that star's own position. A proper motion quoted from OGLE/KMT relative astrometry (relative to the field or red-clump mean; "heliocentric" there means only not-geocentric) is a prior on the `_sgra` pair, never on the absolute one; and since the field mean is not Sgr A\* (0.5 - 1 mas/yr, 0.7 measured at OGLE-2014-BLG-0939), add that zero point to sigma in quadrature. A `mu` on the `_sgra` pair also starts the sampled absolute pm at the converted value (`Star._seed_pm_from_sgra_prior`). The normative statement, the numbers, the Jacobian argument and the tests are `src/exozippy/components/mulensing/conventions.md` **C31**; tests: `tests/test_star_pm_sgra.py`.

**A modeled neighbor's pm is pinned only when nothing constrains it** (review 8.9.7(a), 8.10.3; `Star._pin_unmeasured_neighbor_pm`). On a microlensing sightline a star that is neither a lens nor a source body nor in any orbit group is a resolved neighbor. The rule (JDE 2026-10-26): "if nothing constrains it, pin it. but if something constrains it (like galactic model, distance, pm) don't." Per neighbor, in order: (1) **tied** -- the params file links its pm to another star's (`star.<name>.pm_ra: {initval: star.<host>.pm_ra, sigma: 0}`, and `pm_dec`); the link is the value, no start is hinted; (2) **free under the galactic kinematic prior** wherever a `galacticmodel` is in the topology -- its `kinematic_prior` covers the whole star vector, each star at its own sampled distance ("The stellar distance prior" above), so nothing new was needed, only not pinning; (3) **free** when a gaia/abs/catalog dataset targets it or names it in `psf_stars:` (a catalog proper motion is the flux-weighted motion of the whole PSF); (4) otherwise **pinned** -- `sigma: 0` through the opt-in `"overrides"` pin, the value through `add_hint` -- at the bulge field mean on this sightline (`background.bulge_field_pm_prior`). Off the bulge there is no field mean to pin at: free, with a warning. A params entry overrides any of these. PR #497 pinned in case (2) too, which the ruling corrected. **A bound companion is spelled as an orbit group** (`type: static` if it does not move): that ties its distance and proper motion to its host's automatically ("Linked stars" above) and makes it no neighbor at all. A neighbor hard-linked to another star on distance alone gets case (4) (or (2)) and, in case (4), a warning naming the orbit group: a distance link states a shared position only, so the code does not tie the pm on its own. Tests: `tests/test_catalog_astrometry.py` (`test_neighbor_pm_is_pinned_unless_astrometry_reads_it`, `test_bound_companion_pm_is_tied_by_its_link`; the orbit-group links: `tests/test_orbit_star_links.py`, `test_neighbor_pm_is_free_under_the_galactic_prior_at_its_distance`).

## Which stellar structure parameters a topology actually has

`star.radius`, `star.teff` and `star.feh` are declared for every star unconditionally, because the reporting chain every star carries (`logg`, `density`, `luminosity`, `fbol`) names them in its own `deps`. Whether anything in the **likelihood** reads them is a property of the topology, and in a point-source microlensing fit with no SED, no evolutionary model and no empirical relation the answer is *nothing at all*. Left free they refill their prior; pinned they change nothing. So they are neither: `Star._apply_structure_activity` declares them **inactive** per star (no raw coordinate, no potential, no table row) wherever nothing reads them, driven by the one predicate `Star.structure_consumers`.

The rule the three tiers state, and the reason this is not a pin:

1. **inactive** -- the quantity is not a parameter of this configuration. It supplies no value anything reads, so nothing about it is arbitrary. Not user-overridable, and should not be.
2. **`pin_unselected` via `"overrides"`** -- pinned by default but layered *under* the params file, for a parameter that exists for every instance and is wanted only on some (Band's unread limb darkening; `star.logmass`/`ra`/`dec` on a microlensing-only source star, all three of which *are* read).
3. **free** -- anything the model reads. **Never given a value by the code.**

**The defect (review 3.8.1) was a tier confusion, and it was live.** `_microlensing_only_star_indices`' docstring claimed mulensing "never reads a source star's mass/teff/feh/radius"; under `finite_source` that is false -- `rho`'s deps are `star.radius[source_map]` -- so a tier-2 blanket pin fixed the very radius the finite-source signal is measured against, at an untouched 1.0 solRad default. Being a *microlensing* fact it could also only ever answer for microlensing: it pinned the SOURCE star's radius and left the LENS star's, equally unread, free. Measured on `examples/ob161003` (2S2L, `finite_source: true`), whose params file already carried a comment insisting the source radii must be free: **before, 56 free RVs and start logp 79356.588 with no `star.radius` among them; after, 58 and 79339.520 with it sampled.** The file's claim had been false since it was written.

**A microlensing-only source's `logmass` is freed by any component that reads the mass -- the empirical relations AND the evolutionary models** (`_mass_reader_star_indices`). An evolutionary model is not an empirical relation, but it reads the mass all the same: mann and torres predict the mass, mamajek predicts teff/radius from it, and a MIST track is indexed by it (`star.logmass` is the track's mass coordinate). All four share the `StellarRelation` wiring mixin and name their stars in `star_indices`, so they are asked through it, not by name. The pin exists because such a star's mass is otherwise read only by the IMF. The hard-coded `("mann", "torres", "mamajek")` this replaced held only the empirical relations, so a source star with MIST in place of torres -- constrained through its microlensing distance and its SED radius/teff, via the track -- had its mass pinned at the seed-0 start: every seed's track then had to fit the star at seed 0's mass (a three-seed source-flux ridge fit lost 99-177 nats per seed > 0 to MIST's age term). Only stars an instance NAMES are freed, as in `structure_consumers`; a source nothing reads the mass of keeps the pin (whether it should is a separate question). Tests: `tests/test_star_structure_activity.py` (the four `logmass` cases).

Details worth not rediscovering:

- **The complete consumer list is in `structure_consumers`' docstring**, one entry per reader naming the code that reads it: `sed` (the stars its `seen_star_mask` names -- its teffsed/fbolsed floors are masked to them since 2026-09, `fbol = calc_fbol(luminosity(radius, teff), distance)`, and `_predicted_appmag_node` reads `feh` for the stars it predicts; a bare `sed:` config with no instance to ask counts every star), `evolutionarymodel` (the stars an instance NAMES, via `star_indices` -- **not** the `mist:`/`parsec:` switches, which default to opted-in and so counted every star in any config carrying the block; the switches survive only as the fallback for a premature block no component backs), `lens` under `finite_source` (radius, source bodies only), `planet` when an orbit exists (`p`, `ar` read the hosts' radius), `mann` (radius, feh), `torres` (radius, teff, feh), `mamajek` (teff, radius), and a band with the Claret limb-darkening tie on (`star_constrains_ld`: teff, feh, and radius through logg, for the tied band's star -- `components/sed/sed.md`). Add a consumer there and the mask, the INFO line and the degeneracy warning all follow.
- **What counts is a term in the likelihood, not a mention.** `star.luminosity` reads radius and teff for every star always -- but nothing reads *it*, so counting it would make the predicate trivially true everywhere. That residual -- an inactive star still reporting a `luminosity`/`logg`/`density`/`fbol` computed from its bookkeeping pin -- closed on 2026-10-20: a derived element still cannot be masked OUT (`manifest.py` refuses that combination; the mask means "not built"), so `Component._inherit_inactivity` clears the derived element's `is_active` after the build wherever an element-aligned local dependency is inactive (`components/parameter.md`, "Reporting is per element"). The Deterministic is built as before; the row is gone.
- **A user's own constraint makes the parameter read**, and this is load-bearing rather than a courtesy. A `mu`/`sigma` is a term in the logp -- the user asserting a spectroscopic measurement -- and deactivating it would DROP that prior *and* stop reporting a quantity they measured. Five shipped examples depend on it (kelt17's 7454 +/- 75 K and [Fe/H] 0.21 +/- 0.08, hd80606, GaiaBH1, HIP1349, kelt4). It is also why readership cannot be answered from the component topology alone. The field list is `Parameter._user_constraint_fields`': **`mu`/`sigma`/`lower`/`upper`, and not `initval`**.
  - **`lower`/`upper` count**, which is arguable on physics -- a bound on a quantity nothing reads restrains nothing -- and settled by a concrete cost: `solve_api._bounds_diagnostics` skips inactive elements, so a bound excluding its own initval stopped being reported to the GUI at all. `test_solve_api.py::test_bounds_excluding_initval_yields_diagnostic` caught it. Do not narrow this back.
  - **`initval` does not**, because it is a start value and cannot move a posterior: it says where the number is, not that anything should fit it. Load-bearing in the other direction -- the shipped microlensing params files keep their arbitrary ~M dwarf values as initvals precisely so they document the assumption *and* seed the fit if an `sed:` block is ever added.
  - **`sigma: 0` is the one exception and overrides the rest of the entry**: that is a pin, which the inactive role subsumes exactly, and honoring it would have made this fix a no-op on the very files it exists for -- `examples/ob08092` and `examples/DC2018_128` pinned all six by hand.
- **Under `finite_source` the radius is read but not separately identifiable** -- `rho = theta_star/theta_E` with `theta_star = R_S/d_S`, so `theta_E` and `d_S` absorb any rescaling. It is freed anyway, with a warning naming the degeneracy and the three ways to break it (an `sed:` block, `mann`/`torres`, an explicit prior). A finite-source non-detection genuinely bounds `rho` from **above**, so the fit reports an upper limit -- real information -- whereas pinning it is the code choosing a radius for the user. The warning is suppressed for a star the user pinned themselves: they have decided.
- **A parameter no star uses is still DECLARED**, wholly inactive, unlike `mode_manifest`'s rule of omitting it -- `logg`/`density`/`luminosity` name `radius` and `teff` in their `deps`, so dropping either is a build-graph dependency error rather than a saving.
- Blast radius, measured over every shipped config: only the microlensing examples and `examples/galactic_model` deactivate anything. Every RV/transit/astrometry example is untouched, because each puts a spectroscopic prior on teff/feh and each has an orbit (which is what reads the radius). `examples/ob08092` and `examples/DC2018_128` keep a **bit-identical** free-RV set and start logp (14 free / 6187.7111017152465 and 18 / 3364.6825381918998) -- their hand pins simply became unnecessary and were deleted from their params files.
- One trap already paid for: `planet.star_map` is a numpy array, and `stars or []` on a 1-element array reads the **element**, so `array([0])` tests False. That silently deactivated the star radius `planet.p` reads in every single-planet fit. `tests/test_star_structure_activity.py` pins it.

Tests: `tests/test_star_structure_activity.py`.

## Photometrically unseen stars (`Star.register_parameters`, `Star.structure_consumers`, `SED.seen_star_mask`)

**The SED reads only the stars it predicts a flux for, and a star it never sees has its SED-side parameters pinned.** With an `sed:` block in the topology, `SED.seen_star_mask` (derived from `SED.photometric_readers`, see `components/sed/sed.md`) says which stars some likelihood term actually reads through the SED: the stars named in an SED filter row, a microlensing source whose light curve TIES its flux to f_source through a user-stated zeropoint (`mu` and `sigma`; an unstated zeropoint is only reported, so it reads nothing -- review 2.9.11), every star under a light curve with `sed_constrains_blend: true` (the blend must contain at least the lens's light), and -- conservatively, every star -- when a transit dilution or an absolute-astrometry `band:` reads the SED. A star the SED's no-grid fallback SEVERED (review 2.9.15) is read by nothing and takes exactly this treatment. `structure_consumers` marks the SED as reading `radius`/`teff`/`feh` on exactly those stars (a bare `sed:` config with no instance to ask keeps the historical all-stars answer), and the SED's own `teffsed`/`fbolsed` floors are masked to them -- until 2026-09 those floors were an unmasked `pt.sum`, which is what made the SED count as reading every star. So an unseen star's `radius`/`teff`/`feh` fall into the existing inactive tier of `_apply_structure_activity` unless something else reads them: an empirical relation (`mann` reads radius/feh, `torres` radius/teff/feh, `mamajek` teff/radius) or a user prior. Its `av`, `teffsed` and `radiussed` are read by nothing at all on such a star, so `Star` pins them through the opt-in `overrides` channel (`pin_unselected`; a params entry carrying a prior still frees one) -- the list is the SED's `STAR_KNOBS`, not a copy of it -- and, wherever the user wrote no field for one, also marks it INACTIVE (2026-10-20): a pinned knob was still a table row ("A_V = 0.01", "R_SED = 1 solRad" on a lens nobody photometers), and so was everything derived from it; `loggsed`, `luminositysed` and `fbolsed` now inherit the role (`Component._inherit_inactivity`). A knob the user named keeps the pin path their entry outranks. It also lifts the BC grid's `loggsed` barrier on such a star (`_unread_loggsed_bounds`, infinite bounds as a manifest option, NaN -- keep -- on read stars and wherever the user wrote the bound): the grid bound reaches every star, and with radiussed pinned at an M-dwarf lens's radius the barrier pushed the lens MASS whenever logg passed NextGen's 5.0 ceiling (below ~0.2 Msun). Mass (IMF, `theta_E`), distance (galactic prior, `pi_rel`) and proper motion stay.

The relations are NOT skipped on an unseen star, and the first cut of this rule (PR #337) was wrong to do so. Mann on an unseen microlensing lens is a chain of overlapping constraints, not a circle: the event constrains the mass through `theta_E` and `pi_rel`, Mann ties radius and absolute Ks to that mass, and the blend (with `sed_constrains_blend`) bounds Ks from above. Measured on DC2018 sweep2, Mann's mass and radius penalties were satisfied to 1.00 +/- 0.02 across the whole posterior. What the relation cannot say is the star's Teff, and that is where the pull came from: left free, the lens's Teff/feh are held only by the Ks tie, Ks is a weak thermometer for a hotter star (dM_Ks/dTeff falls from -0.95 to -0.22 mag/1000 K between 3300 and 5500 K), so their conditional widths grow with the lens MASS -- 2-5x more posterior room when the lens was heavy (047 57 -> 11 raw units from the lowest to the highest pi_rel quintile, 223 15 -> 4, 194 34 -> 9). Marginalizing over that volume moved pi_rel 0.3-0.6 dex low and the lens mass 2-5x high on events whose profile maximum sat at the true pi_rel; the marginal-minus-profile volume term on 194 was -4, -7, -11 nats at log pi_rel -2.2, -1.6, -1.2 (notes 2026-09-25/27, "THE LENS-DISTANCE PULL"). The remedy is to give the Teff a reader that knows the answer: the `mamajek` relation (`components/mamajek/`, Pecaut & Mamajek's mean dwarf sequence, Teff(M) at a 4% floor, radius optional) -- see "Empirical relations" below. Without it, an unseen lens's Teff is simply inactive. The rule applies only when an `sed:` block exists -- without one there is no photometric machinery for a star to be excluded from, and `teff`/`feh`/`radius` keep their historical always-registered status. Tests: `tests/test_unseen_star.py`.

## The star side of the `evolutionarymodel` component

That component now ships (`src/exozippy/components/evolutionarymodel/evolutionarymodel.md`), and the star side needed **no edit** when it landed. `star/defaults.yaml` carries the two coordinates an evolutionary track is indexed by -- `initfeh` (initial metallicity) and `eep` -- alongside the present-day `age` a track returns, and the `in_system("evolutionarymodel")` branch of `Star.register_parameters` declares all three, masked per star by the `mist:` / `parsec:` switches. **That mask is a real per-element role now** (it was declared and unread until the roles landed): a star with no track has no EEP -- inactive, so nothing samples it, no potential touches it, and no table reports it -- and if NO star opts in, the three are not declared at all. `examples/hat3/hat3_mist.yaml` is the one shipped example with an `evolutionarymodel:` block; every other example is byte-identical either way (start logp, free-RV set, resolved parameter state).

The branch is driven by the config **key**, so it also fires for a premature `evolutionarymodel:` block that no component backs -- review 3.8.2, and the reason the old "the branch never fires" claim here was false. With no component to read them, an opted-in star's track coordinates are sampled with nothing constraining them, so `Star.register_parameters` **warns**, naming those stars; the unrecognized-key warning `System` already emits does not say that. Setting `mist: False` on them (or removing the block) silences it. The warning is gated on `hasattr(system, "evolutionarymodel")`, so it falls silent now that the component exists -- but note the mask is `mist or parsec`, which defaults to opted-IN, and is therefore a weaker test than "an evolutionarymodel instance actually names this star". `EvolutionaryModel._pin_unmodeled_stars` covers the gap for the track coordinates, and `structure_consumers` asks the component directly for radius/teff/feh. Three mechanisms, three questions: the mask asks "did this star ask for a track", the pin asks "did a block give it one", and the consumer predicate asks "does anything read this quantity".

`mist:`'s historical default is `True` (absent -> opted in) and `parsec:`'s is `False`; every shipped example spells the opt-out `mist: False` outright. Setting either with no `evolutionarymodel:` block still does nothing and **does not raise** -- inert-but-ready by design, so a config written before the component landed keeps working unchanged. This deliberately reverts the star half of review 5.10 (`Star._reject_wip_keys`, PR #158); the orbit half (`Orbit._reject_wip_parameterizations`, review 5.11) stands.

`eep`'s spelling is **Equivalent Evolutionary Point**, per MIST's own `README_tables.pdf`. MIST is inconsistent -- Dotter (2016) and `models/MIST/generate_interpolated_missing_tracks.py` say "phase" -- but "point" is the spelling that does not collide with the separate `phase` column in the MIST track format (the FSPS phase type: -1 PMS, 0 MS, 2 RGB, ...), which an evolutionarymodel component may well surface. Its bounds are MIST's: `upper: 1710` is Table II's terminal primary EEP (end of the white dwarf cooling sequence) and the `354.1661` start sits just past the intermediate-age main sequence (IAMS, EEP 353).

The third key of review 5.10, **`sedfile:`, is deleted outright** rather than gated: `Star.sedfile` was read by nothing, and the implemented broadband-photometry path is the separate `sed:` block, whose own `SED.sedfile` (from that block's `file:` key) is the attribute everything consumes. ## Empirical stellar and planetary relations

Relations that constrain `star.mass`/`star.radius` from other stellar properties are **one component per relation**, one instance per constrained star, each naming its target with a `star:` key (`mann: [{star: "B", constrain: [mass, radius]}]`; see `examples/kelt4`). They add Gaussian potentials on the star's existing nodes -- they never make mass/radius derived, so other data (transit, RV) still constrain them jointly. `star.py` adds `distance` to its manifest when such a component is in the topology.

`mann` (`components/mann/`) implements Mann+2015 (radius) and Mann+2019 (mass) from absolute Ks, ported from EXOFASTv2's `massradius_mann.pro`. Two Ks pathways, matching EXOFASTv2's mannrad/mannmass vs mannsynrad/mannsynmass: `ks: synthetic` takes the star's individual Ks from `sed.predict_star_appmag(star_idx, "2MASS/2MASS.Ks")` (so it works when the observed Ks is a blend of several modeled stars), and `ks: <number>` + `ks_err:` takes a direct observation. Either way the relation's input is **non-centered** -- `mann.appks = ks_source + ks_err * ks_offset` with `ks_offset ~ N(0,1)` -- which is the same posterior as EXOFASTv2's free `appks` plus a `((appks - synk)/0.02)^2` penalty, without the rotated stiff pair that a diagonal mass matrix cannot whiten. sigma on each penalty is the relation's published **fractional** scatter times its own *prediction*, so unlike the fixed-sigma priors elsewhere the `-log(sigma)` normalization is kept (EXOFASTv2 accumulates chi2 only and drops it). Applies to 0.075-0.7 solMass.

In both relations the `[Fe/H]` term is **multiplicative** -- `(a + b*Mks + c*Mks^2) * (1 + f*[Fe/H])` for the 2015 radius, `(1 + f*[Fe/H]) * 10**poly` for the 2019 mass -- and the radius `f` is **+0.04458**. Do not "restore" either detail from `massradius_mann.pro`: the radius term was additive there until 2026-08 and its sign is negative there now. The negative comes from the f column of Table 1 in the 2015 ApJ print run, which is precisely what the 2016 erratum (ApJ 819, 87, "due to a press error ... Tables 1, 2, and 3 were published with incorrect values") reissued; the erratum and arXiv:1501.01635 both give +0.04458, and refitting eq 5 to the paper's own 177-star data (Table 5 + Table 6, `M_Ks` recovered via `Fbol = sigma Teff^4 (R/d)^2`) returns `a,b,c,f = 1.9321, -0.3471, 0.01650, +0.0454` and the published 2.70% scatter, vs 3.32% with the negative sign (review 1.6).

`torres` (`components/torres/`) implements Torres+2010 from `star.teff`/`star.logg`/`star.feh`, ported from `massradius_torres.pro`, and applies above ~0.6 solMass -- the complement of `mann`. It needs no new parameters at all (empty manifest, potentials only). Its relations predict **log10(M)** and **log10(R)** and its scatter is in **dex** (0.027/0.014, overridable per instance as `logm_floor`/`logr_floor` -- deliberately named differently from mann's *fractional* `mstar_floor`/`rstar_floor`), so the mass penalty acts directly on `star.logmass` with no exponentiation round trip, and the constant `-log(sigma)` is dropped exactly as EXOFASTv2 does. These two structural differences (log vs linear, dex vs fractional, latent vs no latent) are why the relations are separate components rather than sharing a base class.

`mamajek` (`components/mamajek/`) is the third relation and the only one that runs the other way: it predicts **Teff** (and, on request, radius) from the star's **mass**, along Pecaut & Mamajek's mean dwarf sequence -- the same shipped table `components/star/mamajek.py` reads for start values (`EEM_dwarf_UBVIJHK_colors_Teff.txt`, cite `Pecaut:2013`). The table is resampled at import onto a uniform log10(M) grid and evaluated in the graph by clamped piecewise-linear interpolation (`physics.calc_mamajek_logteff/logradius`); a polynomial in log M misses the sequence by 3-9% because Teff(M) is nearly flat below 0.4 solMass and steep above 0.6. It exists for the star nothing photometric sees: a microlensing lens has its mass from the event and, with `mann`, its radius and Ks from that mass, but nothing holds its Teff -- and a free Teff whose width grows with the mass is what tilted the DC2018 lens masses high ("Photometrically unseen stars", above). Default `constrain: [teff]` at `teff_floor: 0.04` (JDE 2026-09-27; the sequence's own spread at fixed mass is ~100-150 K); `radius` is opt-in (`radius_floor: 0.05`) because below 0.7 solMass Mann's Ks-based radius is the better one and two radius priors on one star double-count. Consistent with Mann by construction: evaluated at the table's own M_Ks, Mann returns the table's mass and radius to ~2% over 0.1-0.6 solMass. Like `mann` its sigma is a fraction of the prediction, so the `-log(sigma)` normalization is kept (`normalize=True`). Outside 0.078-1.5 solMass the prediction is clamped and a warning names the star at the start. `examples/DC2018`'s generator puts it on every Lens. Tests: `tests/test_mamajek_relation.py`, `tests/test_unseen_star.py`.

Their **scaffolding**, as opposed to their statistics, is shared: `components/relations.py` holds the `StellarRelation` mixin (instance naming after the target star, `star:` resolution, `constrain:` parsing, `build_maps`, the masked penalty, the calibration-range warning loop, the no-op plotters) plus the two `config_schema` entry builders. It is a **mixin, not a base class**, and deliberately defines no `load_data`, `register_parameters` or `build_likelihood` and holds no relation coefficients and no sigma convention -- there is no `_predict()` hook, because that is exactly the design that would put the two sigma conventions one inheritance level apart. The one choice they disagree about is a keyword-only `normalize` on `_add_penalty` with **no default**: mann passes `normalize=True` with `sigma = prediction * fractional_floor`, torres passes `normalize=False` with a constant dex sigma, and each call site says so in a comment naming what the other one does. Being a plain mixin (not a `Component` subclass) is also what keeps `factory.discover_components`' recursive sweep from picking it up; use it as `class Mann(StellarRelation, Component)`. Tests: `tests/test_stellar_relations.py`.

For both, calibration-range violations are **startup warnings only** -- EXOFASTv2 re-checks every likelihood call and hard-rejects on out-of-range [Fe/H], but a `-inf` wall has no gradient for NUTS to follow, so nothing here bounds the posterior. The shared `_warn_outside_range` takes the message as a format string, because the advice is relation-specific (which other relation to prefer, and whether the range is one- or two-sided).

The **Chen & Kipping 2017 planet mass-radius relation** (ported from EXOFASTv2's `massradius_chen.pro`) is NOT a separate component -- components only instantiate from YAML keys present in the config, and this relation must default on automatically, so it lives on the planet component (`planet/physics.py` `calc_chen_radius`/`calc_chen_radius_sigma`, potential added in `Planet.build_likelihood`). Per-planet default (EXOFASTv2 mkss.pro's `chen = fittran xor fitrv`, extended to astrometry): on when exactly one side is data-constrained -- any `transit:` block puts every planet on the radius side; RV/astrometry put a planet on the mass side when it is a body of an observed orbit (`star_membership` of the RV/gaia-abs target star -- primary role only for gaia/abs -- or the rel-mode `orbit:`); off when both or neither. A per-planet `chen: true/false` key overrides. Microlensing does not count as a mass constraint. The penalty is Gaussian in linear Earth radii on the radius predicted from the (clipped-at-1e-10-Mearth, since planet.mass may go negative) mass; sigma is per-segment fractional (0.0403/0.1460/0.0737/0.0443 -- the stellar segment was absolute 0.043 in EXOFASTv2 until a 2026-08 fix there) and the `-log(sigma)` normalization is kept (mann's reasoning: sigma scales with the prediction). Whatever the relation (not data) determines -- the mass in a transit-only fit, the radius in an RV/astrometry-only fit (`Planet.chen_derives`) -- gets a "Derived from \citet{Chen:2017}" `table_note`, propagated at stage 7 to every Parameter whose value node has that quantity among its tensor-graph ancestors (`Planet._annotate_chen_table_notes`). Tests: `tests/test_chen.py`.

## The orbit-crossing / Hill-sphere barrier

`Planet._add_crossing_potential`, descended from EXOFASTv2's `exofast_chi2v2.pro`:445-468 (review 8.8.9). Two planets whose orbits approach within a Hill radius are unstable on timescales far shorter than the age of any system we fit, so the posterior is bounded by requiring the radial gap between the inner planet's apastron and the outer's periastron to be at least `k` **mutual** Hill radii:

```
a_out (1 - e_out) - a_in (1 + e_in)  >=  k R_H,
R_H = ((m_in + m_out) / 3 M_*)^(1/3) (a_in + a_out) / 2
```

`k` is `min_hill_separation`, per planet, default **1.0** -- EXOFASTv2's one-Hill pad, so the formulation differs from the port and the default strictness does not. A pair takes the larger of its two members' `k`. What was here before the port was the bare apsidal test with **no Hill pad**, on **adjacent pairs only** (before that, a never-executed `-inf` block that walked attributes deleted in the vectorized refactor, so any two-planet system raised `AttributeError`); the port itself padded each side by that planet's **own** Hill radius `h_i = (1-e_i) a_i (m_i / 3 M_*)^(1/3)`.

- **Soft, not `+infinity` chi2.** EXOFASTv2 rejects outright; a gradient sampler cannot be handed a wall with no gradient to follow out of, and the JAX backward pass NaNs on one. This is the one half of the port that is deliberately not faithful. The barrier is the shared clipped log-sigmoid with a transition width of 1% of the inner planet's **starting** semi-major axis, so it is scaled to the orbit it guards.
- **All pairs**, as EXOFASTv2 does. Adjacent-only had nothing to recommend it: three planets can have a widely eccentric outer one crossing the innermost while missing its neighbour.
- **Planets are ordered once**, by their starting semi-major axes -- a topology choice, not a sampled quantity. Re-deriving it per draw would make the potential discontinuous wherever two orbits swap places.
- **The Hill radius is the MUTUAL one, not each planet's own.** It is the length the dynamics literature states separations in, so `min_hill_separation` is a number a user can look up rather than one only this file's algebra defines; it is symmetric in the pair by construction; and it depends on the two masses only through their sum, so a marginal secondary mass no longer moves the threshold on its own. Everything it needs is a node that already exists. `M_*` is the **inner** planet's host: for two planets round one star that is simply the star, and where the hosts differ the criterion is not a stability criterion anyway (see the nested-orbit limitation below). Sequencing, for the record: the faithful per-planet-pad port landed 2026-08-18 under "EXOFASTv2 parity -- finish the port rather than inventing a variant", deliberately parity-first; the 2026-08-19 ruling then took the review item's own plan and replaced the pads with the mutual radius, keeping `k = 1` so only the formulation moved. At the same `k = 1` the mutual radius is the **looser** pad -- for an equal-mass pair at `e = 0` the summed own pads are `(a1+a2)(m/3M)^(1/3)` while the mutual radius is `2^(1/3)/2 = 0.63` of that -- which is the one sense in which the strictness is not literally unchanged; measured, a packed 10 d / 11 d pair went from -33.35 to -10.40 nats at the start.
- **1 R_H is a crossing criterion, NOT a stability claim, and the default is deliberately permissive.** The criterion is crude in **both** directions and EXOFASTv2's own comment says so. *Too strict*: **resonant protection makes crossing orbits stable** -- Neptune and Pluto cross in radius and are protected by their 3:2 mean-motion resonance, and co-orbital / Trojan pairs live *inside* their mutual Hill sphere, i.e. at a separation of zero by this measure. Mutual inclination also separates orbits that overlap in radius and is not modeled. A fit of such a system must set `allow_orbit_crossing: true` on a member of the pair, or lower `min_hill_separation`; otherwise the barrier quietly penalizes the very configuration being measured. *Too loose*: the empirical long-term threshold is ~8-12 **mutual** Hill radii (Chambers, Wetherill & Boss 1996; Pu & Wu 2015; Obertas, Van Laerhoven & Tamayo 2017), so a user who wants a real stability prior sets `min_hill_separation` in that range. Real stability is an N-body / SPOCK-style analysis, a follow-up rather than a per-step penalty.
- **`allow_orbit_crossing: true`** on a planet exempts every pair it belongs to -- EXOFASTv2's `alloworbitcrossing`, spelled per planet because the exception exists for a *pair* (a resonant or co-orbital one) and naming a member is how a user says which. Logged per skipped pair.
- **The cube root's argument is floored, never its result.** `planet.mass` may be negative in `linear` mass mode and `x**(1/3)` is NaN there; flooring after the root would multiply its infinite derivative at zero by `pt.maximum`'s zero gradient and give NaN again -- the `calc_theta_E` / `calc_jitter` idiom. `1e-30` leaves `h ~ 1e-10 a`, i.e. no pad, which is the right answer for a planet the data say has no mass.
- Only pairs on **distinct orbits** are constrained, unchanged: two planets sharing an orbit index are co-orbital by construction, and for nested hierarchical orbits this compares semi-major axes about different centers -- the right first-order condition, not a stability criterion.

Tests: `tests/test_orbit_crossing.py`.

## Planet mass parametrization (`linear`, `log_q`, `msini`)

The sampled mass coordinate is per-planet and topology-chosen (`Planet._resolve_mass_parameterization`), overridable on the planet with two exclusive booleans, **`fitlogq:`** and **`fitmsini:`** -- one per alternative, at most one true, raising (naming both) when both are; neither set is the signed linear mass. Each is tri-state like the orbit's `fitvcve`: unset takes the topology default, `true` selects that coordinate, `false` rules it out of the default. `linear` samples `planet.mass` itself over a range including negatives -- RV and astrometric amplitudes flip phase through zero, so crossing zero is what avoids the Lucy-Sweeney positive-definite bias at a marginal detection. `log_q` samples `log10(m_planet / m_host)` and derives `mass = 10**log_q * star.mass[star_map]`. The host (`star_map`) is derived from the orbit blocks' groups -- the one star in the primary group of the planet's orbit, or the lens star for a static lens companion with no orbit block -- never a key on the planet (`components/orbit/orbit.md`, "Planet membership has one spelling"). `msini` samples the minimum mass and derives `mass = msini / sin i` (below).

**`mass_parameterization:` is retired and RAISES** (review 2.14.9). It was the one per-instance coordinate choice in the tree spelled as an enum, against the 2026-09-12 booleans-not-enums ruling (`components.md`, "Config flag vocabulary"; review 4.2.7), and adding the third coordinate is what forced the rename. `planet.REMOVED_KEYS` refuses it at the planet's construction with the migration for the value written -- `log_q` -> `fitlogq: true`; `linear` -> `fitlogq: false` and `fitmsini: false` -- the pattern `mulensing/bodies.py` uses for the removed MMEXOFAST keys. `parse_mass_flags` is the one reader of the booleans; the star's `fitthetae` inverse and the event's `fitthetae` gate, which must know at THEIR stage 3 whether a lens companion samples `log_q`, ask it through `lens_companion_samples_log_q` rather than reading the config themselves.

Default, in order: `log_q` for a microlensing lens body, or where no RV or astrometry measures the orbit; otherwise `msini` where nothing measures the orbit's INCLINATION (below); otherwise `linear`. The mass side is **the same `mass_side` predicate Chen uses** -- `Planet._mass_constrained`, shared by both. Lens membership outranks everything because the magnification model clips `q` to `[1e-9, 100]` (`lens.py`, `op.py`), so `q <= 0` is meaningless there; and where Chen determines the mass, its `pt.maximum(mass, 1e-10)` clip makes the whole negative half-axis a flat plateau with **no restoring force**. `log_q` rather than `log(mass)` because `star.logmass` is already the sampled stellar coordinate, so the microlensing constraint (which measures `q`, not the masses) becomes a **shear** of the sampled pair rather than a diagonal ridge.

**Validated on `examples/DC2018_128`** (two 50000-draw fits, identical code, `n_temps: auto` -> 17 rungs), so nobody has to re-derive it:

- **The two coordinates give the same posterior** -- every parameter within **0.41 sigma** (`star.Lens.mass` 0.48 vs 0.491, `planet.mass` 0.43 vs 0.438, `lens.q` 0.000854 vs 0.00085). The reparametrization is answer-preserving; do not expect it to move results.
- **The gain is mixing.** Under `linear`, `planet.mass` is the worst-mixing parameter in the whole model (ESS 337, Rhat 1.067); under `log_q` the sampled coordinate reaches ESS 3230 / Rhat 1.01 and `star.logmass` improves 363 -> 2865 (the shear decorrelates the pair). The mass stops being the convergence bottleneck. An earlier "condition number ~48 -> ~11" figure (a local 2x2 curvature block at the start point) is superseded by this -- it measured the wrong thing at the wrong scale.
- **Do not read this event's mode structure at the committed `n_temps: 8`.** There, `linear` reports 2 modes and `log_q` 1, which looks exactly like `log_q` failing to explore a high-mass branch. At 17 rungs the direction **reverses** (`log_q` reaches M_Lens > 1 Msun in 38/38 chains, `linear` in 0/38) and both are unimodal. It is a ladder artifact -- at 8 rungs the ladder is communication-limited (measured Lambda ~ 6-7, wants ~13+). A feature that flips sign when the ladder is fixed is not a feature.
- **`lp` is not comparable between the two coordinates**: the prior/Jacobian terms differ by construction, so the two logp values are with respect to different measures. Compare the light-curve sector instead -- and note that for a non-finite-source binary lens the lens mass never enters the light curve at all (only t_0/u_0/t_E/s/alpha/q/pi_E and the fluxes), so any mass difference is prior or sampling, never fit quality.
- The analytic tilt argument (flat-in-`m_p` carries an implicit `p(M*,q) ~ p(M*)*M*` from `|dm_p/dq| = M*`, which flat-in-`log_q` lacks) is correct algebra but proved **subdominant** here -- no visible posterior difference once well sampled. Do not cite it as a benefit.

The unambiguous structural wins stand: negative `q` becomes unreachable rather than likelihood-flat, and the Chen plateau disappears.

- **The prior changes in `log_q` mode**: uniform in log q, not uniform in linear mass. No Jacobian potential is added. This matches the house convention (`star.logmass` is sampled uniform-in-log with no compensating term) and the standard microlensing mass-ratio prior; the old uniform-over-`[-1000, 260000]` Mjup was a default nobody chose and was dominated by its largest values.
- **Planets may differ**, through the `Planet.MASS_MODE_TABLE` mode table: `log_q` is not a parameter of a `linear` planet at all and vice versa, so a microlensing lens body can take the ratio coordinate it requires while an RV-measured planet in the same system keeps its signed linear mass. This used to be impossible in both directions -- an explicit per-planet disagreement **raised**, and a mixed *default* silently fell back to all-`linear` (giving the unconstrained planet a uniform prior over a vast mass range instead of the coordinate its topology asks for) -- because `Parameter.build_pymc` set `is_derived` for the whole vector. `Planet.mass_parameterizations` is the per-planet list; `mass_parameterization` remains as the whole-component answer ("log_q" only when every planet agrees) for readers that just want to know whether the ratio coordinate is in play. `_reconcile_mass_user_params` is per planet too: a stale `planet.<i>.log_q` entry is only stale for the planets that sample a linear mass.
- A user `planet.b.mass` reaches a `log_q` start, and `Eq(mass, 10**log_q * star_mass)` is inert in linear mode, because of the ROLES (review 3.14.22, `src/exozippy/config.md`): in log_q mode the mass is DERIVED, so a user mass is kept in order and the sampled `log_q` -- a mere `defaults.yaml` seed -- is solved from it; in linear mode `log_q` is no parameter, so its `defaults.yaml` value is a fill that moves nothing.  (Until the KS rule this rested on `log_q`'s `rank: 5` steering the old Condition B.) The symbol is mapped unconditionally because `get_symbol_map` runs at `ConfigManager` construction, before components exist, so it cannot see a stage-2 topology decision.
- User `lower`/`upper` on `planet.mass` are **not** translated onto `log_q` (they are absolute masses; the host mass is unknown at that point) -- they become soft barriers on the derived mass, which is the right semantics. A `sigma: 0` pin **is** moved onto `log_q`, since `sigma: 0` on a derived parameter is a silent no-op. `mkparam` restart files carry `planet.log_q`; reusing one after the topology flips to linear raises.
- `calc_theta_E` now guards `mass_lens` with `pt.maximum(..., 1e-12)` the same way it guards `pi_rel`: a linear-mode lens body can drag `mlens_total` negative, and `sqrt` of that fed `log(theta_E)` in `Lens.build_likelihood`, poisoning the logp and its gradient over a whole region instead of letting the `theta_E_singularity` soft bound penalize it. The floor goes on the **radicand**, not on `theta_E` afterwards, and that is not cosmetic: `sqrt'(0) = inf`, and `pt.maximum`'s zero gradient on the clamped side then makes `0 * inf = NaN`, so a floor applied downstream still left `log(theta_E)` with a NaN gradient across the whole `pi_rel <= 0` region. Flooring the argument keeps `sqrt'` finite, the gradient is a clean zero there, and `source_behind_lens` supplies the restoring force.

Tests: `tests/test_planet_log_q.py`.

### `msini`: sampling what an RV curve measures (review 2.14.9)

An RV-only orbit measures `m sin i` and nothing measures `i`. Under the default `U(0,1)`-in-`|cos i|` x uniform-in-mass prior, `p(cos i | data) ~ 1/sin i` and `P(M > X) ~ (2/pi) msini / X`: the (mass, cos i) posterior is a banana with a long tail to high mass at `|cos i| -> 1`, and a sampler in those coordinates mixes along it badly. Measured on `examples/kelt4/kelt4_rvonly.yaml` before the change: `planet.mass`/`orbit.cosi` Rhat 1.02, ESS_tail ~50 while every other coordinate exceeded 2000; `mass_raw` wandered 200-900 whitening units, so each chain's windowed metric jumped ~10x between windows and its step size and divergences followed whichever excursion the last window caught. In a toy with `msini ~ N(0.85, 0.06)` (3 seeds) the (mass, cos i) coordinates gave 0/134/199 divergences, Rhat 1.03-1.16 and P(M > 50) 0.35-0.67% (the predicted tail is ~1.1%, so it was under-sampled); (msini, cos i) gave 0 divergences, Rhat 1.009-1.017, ESS_tail 400-570, 45-61 leapfrog steps instead of 114-173, and P(M > 50) 1.1-1.55%. The PR that landed this records the kelt4_rvonly runs after the change.

- **Answer-preserving, by construction and by test.** At fixed cos i, `|d mass / d msini| = 1/sin i`, so the uniform-in-(mass, cos i) prior is `1/sin i` in (msini, cos i): `Planet.build_likelihood` adds `planet.msini_jacobian = -sum log sin i`, declared on `planet.msini` as a component-added prior (`$\propto 1/\sin i$`, "uniform in mass and cos i"). Without it the prior would be uniform in m sin i, i.e. `p(mass | i) ~ sin i`, down-weighting exactly the tail the change exists to sample. `tests/test_fitmsini.py` checks it as a PROPERTY, as the chord Jacobian is checked: carried from the raw coordinates to (mass, cos i) through the model's own transform, the no-likelihood density is flat to 1e-6 in both coordinates, and with the term removed it is off by exactly `+log sin i`; a finite difference of the model's own mass node gives `d mass / d msini = 1/sin i` and the potential its log.
- **The radicand is floored, strictly positive.** `mass = msini / sqrt(max(1 - cos^2 i, 1e-30))` and the Jacobian takes the same floored radicand (`planet/physics.py` `SINI_RADICAND_FLOOR`, the `ECC_FLOOR` / `CHORD_RADICAND_FLOOR` family). cos i is logit-bounded and never reaches +/-1, but `1 - cos^2 i` rounds to 0.0 once `|cos i| > 1 - 1.1e-16`, which the logit tail does reach; never divide by an unfloored sin i.
- **The default** is `msini` for a planet that `_mass_constrained` puts on the mass side, that is not a lens body, whose orbit `inclination_constrained_orbits` (orbit.py, beside `amplitude_constrained_orbits`) does not name, when no orbit in the system samples the transit chord, and whose `planet.mass` the user did not pin. What measures an inclination: any transit light curve (every one models every planet, so it names every orbit with a planet; the phase-curve terms live there too), an `rm:` or `dopptom` dataset naming the orbit, astrometry of any mode, microlensing keplerian lens or source orbital motion, and the USER -- `sigma: 0` on the orbit's `cosi` or `inc` states the inclination outright (the pin `fitchord`'s default already defers to). `beam_constrains_mass` ties beaming to `K`, i.e. to `m sin i`, so it measures nothing about `i` by itself and is not on the list. In the shipped examples two configs take the default, `kelt4/kelt4_rvonly.yaml` and `hd80606/hd80606_rvonly.yaml` (the high-eccentricity validation's RV-only leg; its siblings `hd80606.yaml`, with the TESS transit, keeps the linear mass, and `hd80606_transitonly.yaml` samples log_q).
- **A user pin on `planet.mass` blocks the default** (`_user_pinned_mass`), the orbit's `_pin_blocks_default` rule: in msini coordinates the mass is derived and `sigma: 0` on a derived element is dropped. An explicit `fitmsini: true` still wins, with the generic per-element warning.
- **msini needs real bounds without giving every other planet a barrier.** defaults.yaml gives `msini` an `initval` and an `init_scale` (0.1 Mjup, mass's), but NO `lower`/`upper`: a derived or reported element with a numeric bound gets a soft barrier, which would add a potential to every linear and log_q planet. The sampled elements take `planet.mass`'s bounds through the manifest `"overrides"` channel instead (`Planet._msini_bounds_override`, NaN elsewhere), so `|m sin i| <= |m|` gets the mass range as hard logit support and the mass keeps its bounds as soft barriers on the derived value, as in log_q mode.
- **`Eq(msini, mass * sini)`** (planet/symbolic_physics.py) follows the log_q precedent through the roles: in msini mode the mass is derived, so a user `planet.mass` initval -- or the `K -> mass` solver's result -- back-solves into an msini start; where msini is only reported its `defaults.yaml` value is a fill and the relation cannot move mass or cos i. (Its `rank: 5`, which Condition B used to rely on, is gone with the KS rule; both directions are pinned in `tests/test_fitmsini.py`.)
- **REPORTED elsewhere, not derived.** On a `linear` or `log_q` planet msini is `{"output_expr_key": "default"}` (built late, consumed by nothing), the V_c/V_e pattern: an msini planet's mass reads msini while a linear planet's msini reads mass, which as two DERIVED selections would be a per-parameter cycle in `graph.py`. Both `msini` and `mass` are reported in every mode (review 8.8.17's "report both, always").
- **The seed polish lands on a corner, and that is a MAP artifact, not an answer.** The polish maximizes the density in the SAMPLED coordinates, and in (msini, cos i) the flat inclination direction carries the 1/sin i factor; together with the Chen & Kipping normalization, which favors the ~80 Mjup end of its Jovian regime, it walks `examples/kelt4/kelt4_rvonly.yaml` to cos i = 0.99994 (mass ~84 Mjup), where one ulp of arithmetic moves the polished lp by ~0.5 nats. The posterior is untouched and numpyro tunes out of it (two 4 x (1000 + 1000) seeds: 0 divergences, against 0 and 7 in the linear coordinate), but a golden start there would be a platform coin flip, so `tests/test_integration_kelt4.py` and the ulp harness in `tests/test_polish.py` pin `fitmsini: false` and keep the linear optimum they were calibrated on. The same corner was seen under log_q when 2.14.9 was investigated.
- **Exclusive with `fitlogq`, incompatible with `fitchord`, independent of `fitvcve`.** On a chord orbit cos i is derived from the chord and a/R*, which depends on the mass, while msini derives the mass from cos i -- a dependency cycle, so an explicit `fitmsini: true` RAISES at stage 3 (the default simply steps down to `linear`). The cycle crosses orbits: the build graph orders parameter VECTORS, so a chord on ANY orbit makes `orbit.cosi` as a whole depend on `planet.mass`, and an msini planet on a different, plain-cos i orbit still closes the loop (measured: two RV orbits, one `fitvcve: true` -- whose `fitchord` follows it -- recursed in `Component.add_parameter`). So any chord orbit rules msini out for every planet. Because stage 3 follows the user's config key order, the planet may register before its orbit; it asks `Orbit.inclination_modes(system)`, a side-effect-free run of the very functions the orbit's own stage 3 calls, so the two cannot disagree. `fitmsini: true` with no orbit raises.

Tests: `tests/test_fitmsini.py`.


## Planet radius prior: uniform in R_P/R_* (`Planet._add_radius_prior`)

`planet.radius` is the sampled coordinate, and its logit transform alone is exactly uniform in R_P over `[lower, upper]` (`Parameter.build_pymc`'s section-C correction, `components/parameter.md`). EXOFASTv2 instead steps in p = R_P/R_* with a flat prior. The two are not the same prior: at fixed R_*, `|dp/dR_P| = 1/R_*`, so uniform in R_P is `R_*` times uniform in p on the joint (p, R_*) density, which tilts a transit fit toward a larger, less dense star -- hence a larger impact parameter and a larger p. Measured on HAT-3b against EXOFASTv2 (2026-10-16): p 0.109655 here vs 0.109200 there (+0.15 sigma); importance-reweighting the EXOZIPPy posterior by 1/R_* moved it to 0.10941, about half the gap.

- **The term.** `Planet.build_likelihood` adds `planet.radius_jacobian = -sum log R_*` over the planets below, R_* being each planet's host through `star_map`, so the implied prior is uniform in (p, R_*). JDE 2026-10-16: match EXOFASTv2 for now; whether a prior uniform in log R_P would be more physical is open. The choice lives in ONE place, `planet/physics.py` `radius_base_prior_logp` (the log-density of the base prior relative to uniform in R_P), plus the Prior-column and prose strings declared beside the call; swapping the base prior is a change there.
- **Which planets.** Every planet whose `radius` element is SAMPLED (`Parameter.element_is_sampled`), and only when an orbit exists (p and everything that reads R_P/R_* exist only then). A pinned radius (`sigma: 0`, including a hard link) gets nothing: the term would be a function of R_* alone, a prior on the star.
- **Other radius priors multiply it.** A user `mu`/`sigma` on R_P and the Chen & Kipping relation are separate potentials on R_P; they compose with the flat-in-p base prior exactly as EXOFASTv2 applies both as penalties on its derived rp, so the Jacobian stays. The Prior column reports the user's Gaussian and the `$\propto 1/R_*$` term together.
- **The bounds are still on R_P, not on p.** Where nothing constrains R_P (no transit, chen off, no user prior), the 1/R_* factor integrated over the fixed R_P interval is a 1/R_* prior on the host, which EXOFASTv2's p-bounded stepping does not have. Such a radius measures nothing. With chen on (an RV- or astrometry-only fit) EXOFASTv2 carries the same 1/R_* marginal, because the relation fixes R_P = p R_* at a mass-given value.
- **R_* is floored strictly positive inside the log** (`physics.STAR_RADIUS_LOG_FLOOR`, the CLAUDE.md where-trap rule): `star.radius` defaults to `[0.1, 2000]` solRad, but a user may widen it to 0 or drop the bound for a Gaussian.

Tests: `tests/test_radius_prior.py` -- carried from the raw coordinates to (p, R_*) through the model's own transform, the no-likelihood density is flat to 1e-6, and with the term removed it is off by exactly `+log R_*`.
