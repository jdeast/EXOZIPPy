`src/exozippy/components/mulensing/`: the flux-space observation likelihood, the built-in
PSPL seeder, and the rules about which bodies may be a lens or a source.

Read this before touching the microlensing likelihood, the seeding (the peak finder, and
why the MMEXOFAST hand-off is gone), or the lens/source body maps. Related: `src/exozippy/components/mulensing/conventions.md` (the

## The microlensing likelihood is Gaussian in FLUX (`components/mulensing/mulensinstrument.py`)

There is one observation likelihood and it lives in flux; the magnitude branch is gone. Photon-counting noise is (approximately) Gaussian in flux, a magnitude is a logarithm of it, and the model is *linear* in flux (`F = f_s*A + f_b`) -- the old code already computed `F` and then took `-2.5*log10` of it purely to hand the result to a `pm.Normal`. `data_format:` is now only a statement about the FILE: `flux` is used as given (non-positive values are **first class** -- nothing is clamped, which is the whole point for difference imaging), `magnitude` (the default) is converted at load to `F = 10**(-0.4 m)` with `sigma_F = ln(10)/2.5 * F_obs * sigma_m` evaluated at the **observed** flux, so sigma stays a data constant rather than a function of the model. `self.flux` is the observable array; there is deliberately no `self.mag`.

- A magnitude-format fit keeps its answer to **O(sigma_mag)**: the two log-likelihoods differ by a constant change-of-variable Jacobian plus a `sum_i x_i^3/(K sigma^2)` term (`K = 2.5/ln10`) that shifts an estimate by `1.5*sigma_mag^2/K` in magnitude, i.e. `1.5*sigma_rel*sqrt(N)` posterior sigmas -- ~0.3 sigma for 0.01 mag photometry over ~500 epochs, and it is the *magnitude* fit that carried that bias. Tests measure it and pin its convergence: `tests/test_mulens_flux_likelihood.py`.
- **What the clamp was costing.** `np.maximum(flux, 1e-30)` mapped every non-positive difference-imaging epoch to ~75 mag; those are precisely the *baseline* epochs, and the baseline is what pins `f_blend`. The old code therefore did not degrade uniformly — it silently deleted the data constraining the blend, in exactly the events (heavy negative blending, faint sources) where the blend matters most.
- `err_scale` needed no unit change: it is dimensionless in flux for the same reason it was dimensionless in magnitudes — it multiplies whatever error the file reports. It now scales the flux error, which for a magnitude file is the propagated `F*sigma_m/K`, so an existing prior still says the same thing to first order. `mulensinstrument` has no `jitter_variance` (`noise_model = "err_scale"`), so there is no additive term to re-unit.
- **`err_scale` is bounded `0.01..100`, not `1e-6..1e6`** (review 8.2.2, JDE 2026-09-15). A six-decade error scale lets the optimizer prefer "the data are noise" to "the model is wrong": on DC2018-226 a point-lens fit to a wide binary inflated both bands 300-460x, walked `u_0` to zero under the flattened likelihood, and paid 181k nats of misfit it could no longer feel (review 2.4.14). The entry also declares a `near_bound_remedy` -- rescale the supplied errors, or seed a better starting model -- that the start-time and wrap-up wall warnings append (`components/parameter.md`). The DC2018 sweep tightens further to `0.5..2` in its params file, because simulated curves carry honest errors and there `err_scale` is a check, not a fit. A user with genuinely mis-stated errors can widen the bound in their params file; the point is that doing so is a decision, not a default.
- **The PSPL peak finder discards a degenerate refinement.** `u_0 -> 0` with `t_E -> inf` keeps a peak's shape while the linear flux solve absorbs the amplitude, and on a low-amplitude or anomaly-dominated event the chi2 surface slopes gently that way, so an unbounded Nelder-Mead walks off to a source parked on the lens for the whole light curve. DC2018-001 and -226 came back with `u_0 = 5e-10`, `t_E = 1e7` and `4e8` days -- start logps of `-2.5e12` / `-1.3e9`, 15 s per evaluation, and a polish that spent ten hours in a point-lens basin. `peakfind.refinement_is_degenerate` (`u_0 < 1e-4`, `u_0 > 10`, `t_E` beyond twice the data span, or -- since 2026-09-21 -- `t_E` shorter than the cadence) discards such a result with a warning that names the degeneracy. What replaces it is no longer the raw grid point: `(t_0, t_E)` are refit at each grid `u_0`, and among the refits within 4 chi2 of the best the LARGEST `u_0` is reported. The case that forced this is a peak that fell in a season gap: the observed wing fixes `t_0` and `t_E` for any `u_0` but leaves `u_0` itself free, so the free refinement walks it to zero while every fixed-`u_0` refit lands within 2.5 d of the truth (DC2018-226) -- and the grid point the old fallback kept sat at the season's first epoch, 30 d off. Tests: `tests/test_peakfind.py`.
- **The PSPL peak finder looks for the PRIMARY first** (review 2.4.14, experiment A). A point-lens fit locks onto the biggest feature in the curve, and on a wide binary that is the planetary-caustic anomaly, not the event: on DC2018-226 the primary's peak fell in a season gap, its wing was a 4-sigma-per-day excess, the anomaly 68 d later was a 12-sigma spike over 179 epochs, and eleven of the twelve `t_0` candidates sat in the anomaly's window. The blind fit seeded there modelled the anomaly as its own one-day event with a `q ~ 0.03` companion and never visited the truth's basin in 50k draws; MMEXOFAST's EventFinder has the same blind spot on the same event (measured, 2026-09-17); seeded with the primary's PSPL and generic `s`, `q`, `alpha`, the same sampler found `s ~ 4.2`, `q ~ 7e-4`. So `find_pspl_seed` fits once, measures the found feature's width FROM THE DATA (`feature_window`: the FWHM of the binned flux excess -- not from the model, which may be the degenerate fallback), masks three half-widths either side, fits the rest of the curve, and returns the second fit when it beats a flat baseline by 100 chi2 AND lights up the observed epochs (`_observed_width`, again not the model FWHM, which tracks the unconstrained `u_0`) over at least three times the first feature's width. Otherwise the first fit stands. On the 44 DC2018 events this changes exactly one seed, 226's, from 3.8 `t_E` off to 0.14 `t_E` off; the anomaly's window is reported under `"anomaly"` and logged, and nothing is derived from it -- no `s`, `alpha` or `q` (JDE 2026-09-17: no estimator anywhere; the sampler finds the companion). `examples/DC2018/dc18_seed.py` now delegates to this function instead of carrying its own copy of the fit. Tests: `tests/test_peakfind.py` (`test_anomaly_dominated_curve_seeds_the_primary`, `test_wing_only_peak_in_a_gap_is_placed_from_the_wing` and the two guards that a plain event and a lone short event are left alone).
- `f_source`/`f_blend`/`log_f_total` are unchanged -- they always lived in the file's own flux system, which for a magnitude file is the system in which `F = 10**(-0.4 m)`. The SED `zeropoint` hook therefore still reads `zp = m_SED + 2.5*log10(f_source)` and is provably insensitive to the file's format.
- The additive noise amplitudes **are** affected, because a light curve's flux zeropoint is arbitrary (`~1e-8` for a magnitude file, `O(1)` or `O(1e4)` counts for difference imaging). `gp_rot_sigma`/`gp_sho_sigma`/`out_scale` are now bare fluxes with no unit string, and `MulensInstrument._scale_flux_amplitudes` installs per-light-curve `upper`/`initval` from the bootstrapped baseline flux through the manifest `"overrides"` channel (multipliers are the flux-space images of the old 5 mag / 10 mag caps). The defaults.yaml numbers are ceilings only. A user bound looser than the computed cap is clipped with a warning.
- **Plots stay in magnitudes** (the convention these curves are read in), but the alignment onto the reference instrument's flux system is now **affine** -- `F_aln = (fs_ref/fs_i)*(F_i - fb_i) + fb_ref` -- so errors propagate by one factor and the GP mean, additive in flux, is added to the model flux in the instrument's own system before the map. Any point whose (aligned) flux is not positive becomes **NaN** and is not drawn, replacing the old `1e-30` clamp that put ~75 mag spikes on the chart *and* in the fit.

## Priors that must not depend on the seed or the topology (evidence across architectures)

Comparing `ln Z` between a point lens, a binary source and a binary lens of one event (`examples/DC2018/dc18_arch_config.py`, whose README section is the canonical DC2018 prior set) is meaningful only when every shared prior is the same function in every model. Two component defaults were not, and are fixed in the component rather than in the generator:

- **`neighbor_flux`'s upper bound is 2x the MEDIAN OBSERVED flux** of its light curve (`MulensInstrument._neighbor_flux_scale`, from `flux_median_init`, recorded in `load_data` after the magnitude->flux conversion), and its start and preliminary scale follow it. It was 2x `fs_init`, the flux bootstrap's baseline -- which solves `F = f_s A + f_b` at the SEEDED geometry, so a second source, a companion or a different grid seed changed the uniform's width, i.e. its normalization, by an arbitrary `log(span ratio)`. The bound arrives through the clipping `"overrides"` channel, so no params-file entry could have restored identity: a looser user bound is clipped to the seed-dependent one. A light curve whose median is not positive (difference imaging with a negative baseline) falls back to `fs_init`, with a warning naming it. Tests: `tests/test_neighbor_flux.py`, `tests/test_dc18_arch_config.py`.
- **`q_flux` (binary source) can be sampled as `log_q_flux`**, per light curve, with `fitlogqflux: true` on the `mulensinstrument` entry: `log_q_flux` uniform on `[-3, 3]` and `q_flux = 10**log_q_flux` derived (`calc_q_flux_from_log`; the `log_s`/`s`, `log_rho`/`rho` shape, through `mode_manifest`, so a mixed vector works and every reader of `q_flux` is unchanged). The default stays linear `q_flux` on `[0, 1000]`, which puts 99.9% of the prior mass on the second source being the brighter one. The bootstrap's flux ratio seeds whichever coordinate is sampled. A user `q_flux` start under `fitlogqflux` is a start on a DERIVED element with no engine relation, so seed `log_q_flux` instead. With one source the flag warns and does nothing.

What a config cannot equalize is recorded with the generator: the galactic model's per-star prior is unnormalized (`galacticmodel.py`), so a binary source's extra star body offsets `ln Z` by a term with no fixed normalization.

## The SED zeropoint: the user's statement, in a stated magnitude system

`mulensinstrument.zeropoint` is the DERIVED offset `zp = m_SED + 2.5*log10(f_source)` between a light curve's instrumental source magnitude and the SED's prediction (its Parameter mechanics -- derived, `force_node`, the context node -- are in `src/exozippy/components/sed/sed.md`). Two rulings decide what it means.

- **There is no default tie** (review 2.2.21, JDE 2026-09-30: "the user should specify a zero point and how much they trust it, we shouldn't decide that for them"). defaults.yaml carries no `mu`/`sigma`; the Gaussian on a light curve's zeropoint, and with it the whole f_source -> SED tie, exists only where the params file states BOTH (`mulensinstrument.<name>.zeropoint: {mu: 22.0, sigma: 0.02}`). `mu` without `sigma` WARNS that it does nothing; `sigma: 0` RAISES (an exact zeropoint would make f_source deterministic given the SED); an `initval` -- including the bare spelling `zeropoint: 22.0`, which the params boundary translates to `{initval: 22.0}` -- RAISES, because a start value on a derived quantity is a number nothing reads and the user means a prior (`MulensInstrument._check_zeropoint_entries`, stage 3, and `_zeropoint_context`). An untied zeropoint is still built and REPORTED (the calibration the fit implies). The predicate is `MulensInstrument.sed_tie_mask` (prediction AND `mu` AND `sigma`; stage-order-safe, since `SED.photometric_readers` asks it while the STAR registers): an untied light curve reads nothing of the source's SED flux, so with no tie anywhere the source's SED-side parameters are pinned like any unseen star's (review 2.9.11, `components/sed/sed.md`). `sed_prediction_mask` is also where the SED's no-grid fallback lands (review 2.9.15): a source the SED severed has no prediction on any light curve, so its zeropoint element is inactive, the user's prior on it is reported as unmatched, and the blend tie -- which reads the zeropoint -- goes with it; a severed NON-source drops the blend tie alone. Until this ruling defaults.yaml supplied `0 +/- 0.2 mag` -- "the file's flux is 10**(-0.4 m) in calibrated magnitudes" -- for every mulens+SED fit whether anyone had said so or not; the only shipped configs that relied on it were the three `examples/KMT-2019-BLG-1806` configs, whose params file now states the published calibration instead (JDE 2026-10-01: "the params file should have the zero point"): Zang et al. 2023 (AJ 165, 103) calibrate the KMT I magnitudes to the standard I band with the OGLE-III catalog, so each light curve carries `zeropoint: {mu: 0.0, sigma: 0.07}` (sigma = their I_S uncertainty) and `magsys: Vega`; the files themselves give m_S = 21.28-21.31 at the shipped start against the published I_S = 21.35 +/- 0.07. The source-flux SEEDING (`_seed_source_star_from_flux`) reads the zeropoint mu, so it too runs only on tied light curves: with no calibration statement the baseline flux says nothing about the source's magnitude.
- **The zeropoint has a magnitude system** (issue #313). The BC grid predicts VEGA magnitudes, while a zeropoint is a statement in whatever system the photometry was calibrated in -- the DC2018 challenge's 22.0 is AB, which read as Vega made every sweep source 1.04 mag too faint in W149 and 0.54 mag too red. Each light curve takes `magsys:` on its `mulensinstrument` entry, parsed at `__init__` by the SED rows' own boundary parser (`components/sed/magsys.py:parse_magsys`): exactly `"Vega"` or `"AB"`, case-SENSITIVE (a case variant raises with a "did you mean"). Unstated means the band filter's NATIVE system, the same rule as an SED row (JDE 2026-10-01) -- Roman WFI is AB-native, Cousins/Bessell/2MASS Vega -- and a filter with no recorded native system raises naming the light curve. Resolution goes through `SED.filter_magsys` -> `SED._column_magsys`, the one helper `_convert_to_bc_system` also uses, so the column record's drift check and its `ab_minus_vega` are the 1.9.1 ones (`NextGen.magsys.csv`; nothing re-reads the CSV). The offset is applied in ONE place, `MulensInstrument._predicted_mag_on_zp_system`: the SED's Vega prediction plus m_AB - m_Vega on an AB light curve, read by both the derived zeropoint and the opt-in blend tie (which compares a prediction against that zeropoint, so the two must agree). The seeder subtracts the same offset from its measured magnitude to compare it with the Vega locus. A Vega light curve adds an exact 0, so its graph value is unchanged. The startup table prints the system in the Units column (`mag (AB)`, via the Parameter's display-only `unit_qualifier`), stage 3 logs one line per light curve naming the system and the offset, and the draft's microlensing paragraph says which light curves are tied and, when any is AB, that their prediction is converted (citing Oke & Gunn 1983).
- **The DC2018 configs.** Every DC2018 mulens+SED config states its zeropoint, so none lost a tie. With Roman AB-native an unstated system already reads their `22.0` as AB; each such light curve nonetheless carries an explicit `magsys: AB` with a one-line provenance comment (`dc18_photometric_leg.py`: the challenge's photometry is AB). The `ab194` arms whose mu was already hand-converted to Vega (`zpvega`, `anchorfix`, `truthsrc_vega`, `blendtie`, `zplink`, and the partial-offset `zpf025/050/075`) carry `magsys: Vega`, which keeps their meaning (and start) unchanged; without it they would now be converted twice. On the 22.0 configs the change is the fix: the source reads 1.04 mag brighter in W149 (0.50 in Z087).
- A light curve with no SED prediction (no `band:`, or a band filter missing from the BC grid) has no zeropoint: its element is INACTIVE (manifest `mask`, `inactive_value: 0.0`) instead of reporting the prior center through a `pt.switch`, which with no default center would have been a number nobody stated. A system in which no light curve has a prediction declares no zeropoint at all.

Tests: `tests/test_zeropoint_magsys.py` (AB converted by exactly the record's offset, AB and the equivalent Vega calibration building the same fit, unstated -> native, case-variant / unknown / no-native raises, the startup unit and log line, no default tie, mu-only warns, initval and bare value raise), `tests/test_sed_flux_constraints.py`, `tests/test_source_flux_seeding.py`.

## The plotted curve is the curve the likelihood fits

Not an aspiration -- structural rules, each of which was violated (review 1.6.1, 1.6.4) and each of which is now pinned by evaluating the compiled plot function rather than by reading the source (`tests/test_mulens_plot_parity.py`).

- **One resolver for (u1, u2, bandpass)**: `MulensInstrument._finite_source_limb_darkening`, called by `build_likelihood` AND by `compile_plotters`. That resolution used to live inline in `build_likelihood` only, so the plotters called `get_magnification_op` with neither argument, `MulensEvent.get_magnification_op` computed `effective_bandpass = None`, and every plotted and GUI model curve was the **uniform-source** magnification while the fit scored the limb-darkened one -- a discrepancy of up to several percent, largest exactly at the caustic crossings and the finite-source peak these plots exist to show. The resolver takes a LIGHT CURVE (`_finite_source_limb_darkening(system, i)`): see the next section.
- **The compiled plotter stops at the model FLUX**, and the conversion to the plotted delta-magnitude happens in numpy through the very `_flux_to_mag` the data traces go through (`mag - baseline_ref`). The graph used to end in `-2.5*log10(maximum(A_eff, 1e-30))`, i.e. the plot kept the `1e-30` clamp the previous section describes removing from the data path: a draw with `f_blend < -f_source*A` drew a ~75 mag spike where the data path had already switched to a gap. Keeping ONE conversion is what makes "the model curve and the points it is drawn over agree about what a non-positive flux means" true by construction. The GP curve was already built on the flux node for the same reason.
- **One flux-model builder, evaluated per instrument** (reviews 1.6.7, 1.6.11): `MulensInstrument._model_flux` is the detrend-free flux expression both `build_likelihood` and `compile_plotters` call, and every plotted curve is SOME instrument's own expression mapped by the same `_flux_alignment` as that instrument's data -- for a binary source, whose `q_flux` is per instrument, that is one curve per instrument (`_plot_model_layout`). The detrend factor `10**(-0.4 X.c)` is applied by the Instrument base (`_detrended_model`) and divided out of the plotted fluxes and errors by its inverse (`detrend_corrected`). Both are described, with their tests, in `src/exozippy/components/instrument.md`.

## Limb darkening is per band, not per instrument

The source profile belongs to the band a light curve was taken in, so `_finite_source_limb_darkening(system, i)` answers for light curve `i`: its own band's `(u1, u2, bandpass)`, or `(None, None, None)` for a point source, for no Band component, or for a light curve with no `band:` (a uniform source; `register_parameters` warns once when such a light curve sits beside ones that do name a band). Until review 8.6.10 it was ONE answer for the whole instrument -- the first band's, with a warning -- so a V light curve crossed the caustic with band I's profile, on exactly the epochs that carry the finite-source information: a 17 mmag I-vs-V systematic, and +40-55 chi2 per solution at the start of a real OGLE-2013-BLG-0341 fit (band I's u = 0.649 on every band).

- **One magnification call per (source, band), each on that band's rows only.** `_ld_groups` partitions the light curves (one group per band, plus one for the band-less), `_model_flux` takes the per-row light curve as NUMPY (`row_lc`: `inst_map` from the likelihood) so each band's rows are a static index array, and the per-band results are reassembled with `pt.set_subtensor` into one vector. Never `pt.where`: its gradient multiplies the unselected branch by zero, and a NaN there poisons the whole gradient (CLAUDE.md). The rows are disjoint and cover the data, so the cost stays one VBM evaluation per epoch per source however many bands there are.
- **A single group is the old graph.** With one band present (every single-band event; every point-source or LD-free one) the call is the one call on the whole vector, so those fits are bit-identical -- the mulens acceptance fixtures pin it.
- **The plotter compiles one curve per group** and `_compiled_model_flux(t, obs_pos, i, ...)` dispatches on light curve `i`'s group. A grid point has no band of its own (it takes the band of the curve being drawn); evaluating every band on the grid inside one graph and indexing by `i` would multiply the plotting VBM cost by the number of bands. `_model_flux_node` stacks every group's curve, so a GUI slider on any band's `u1` counts as a dependency.
- **The astrometric centroid shift** of a lensed dataset takes its `photometry:` light curve's band (`AstrometryInstrument._apply_lens`, `_lensrel_model`).
- **Per-source profiles (8.6.11) are the next step, not this one.** `rho` is per source but the profile is still per band: two sources of different Teff cross the caustic with one band profile. The loop structure (source outer, band inner) is where a per-(source, band) `u1` would go.

Tests: `tests/test_mulens_per_band_ld.py` -- a two-band caustic-crossing binary whose per-row magnification matches a direct per-band VBM call (and measurably not the other band's), one VBM Op per band partitioning the epochs, and per-band plotted curves.

## The zooms and the lens-plane figure (`components/mulensing/mmplot.py`)

`MulensInstrument.plot` writes, besides the full light curve (`_mulens.pdf`): a PEAK zoom (`_mulens_zoom.pdf`), one zoom per ANOMALY (`_mulens_anomaly<k>.pdf`), and the caustic + trajectory figure (`_mulens_caustics.pdf`). The windows are computed ONCE, at the reference point (`zoom_windows`), and passed to every draw's `plot_data` through `plotrender.plot_via_specs(..., zoom_windows=...)`, so the spaghetti of one zoom shares one x range; the GUI, calling `plot_data` with no windows, recomputes them at each point.

- **The peak zoom is `t_0 +/- 3 u_0 t_E`** (3 rho t_E for a resolved source; floored at 0.02 t_E, capped at 1.5 t_E). It replaced `t_0 +/- 3 t_E`, which was barely narrower than the +/-5 t_E chart.
- **Each zoom evaluates the model on its OWN grid** (`ZOOM_GRID_POINTS`, 500) and carries a `y_range` framed on the model and data inside the window. The full chart's 2000-point grid over 10 t_E is one point per 2 h on a 20 d event, too coarse for a crossing; and a renderer autoscaling y spans the whole light curve whatever the x range. 500 and not 2000 because the zoom grids are evaluated at every posterior draw: on `examples/ob09020/ob09020_linear.yaml` (finite-source binary) seven 2000-point zooms made one plot call 19 s, and a fit draws 50.
- **An anomaly is where the data can see the companion.** At every data epoch, with its own observer, the binary magnification `A` is compared with the binary's EFFECTIVE SINGLE LENS `A_1` along the same trajectory (`single_lens_magnification`): `z = f_source,i |A - A_1|/sigma > 3`. The single lens is NOT the bare primary: a close companion (s < 1) lenses with the whole mass from the center of mass; a wide one leaves the primary's own theta_E/sqrt(1+q), shifted q/((1+q) s) toward the companion by the companion's deflection (Chang-Refsdal). One expression covers both (center `x_1 + q/(1+q) min(s, 1/s)`). The bare primary is off by 3e-3 to 1e-2 over the WHOLE peak at q = 1e-3, which Roman-grade errors flag as an anomaly; the effective lens by 1e-4 to 6e-4 (measured, `tests/test_mulens_lens_plane.py`).
- **Windows are a tree, descended on the model's departure, not on z.** Epochs with z > 3 are grouped (split at two unflagged epochs or a 0.25 t_E gap), padded by half their width and merged where the padding overlaps -- interleaved instruments otherwise cut one crossing into pieces. A window is replaced by the windows found inside it at the next RELATIVE departure `|A - A_1|/A_1` (1%, 3%, 10%, ... 300%) when it spans 80% of the peak zoom (it would repeat it) or holds two or more features there. A stellar binary departs from any single lens over the whole event (ob09020_linear: 1316 of 2837 epochs at z > 3), and the descent is what finds its caustic crossings. A z ladder was tried first and split that light curve on its ERROR BARS: z jumps between instruments where the model is smooth. A window with one feature keeps its context: DC2018_128's planetary dip-and-bump is one window.
- **The lens-plane figure is drawn by MulensModel** (`Model.plot_caustics`, `Model.plot_trajectory`), one model per source from `MulensEvent.mm_param_list` -- the ONE builder of the `mulensmodel` backend's parameter vector -- through op.py's own `_build_binary_model`/`_build_pspl_model`, so the drawn lens is the parameters' lens with the backend's parallax convention (Skowron deviations on the satellite channel; one trajectory per observer location). Each instrument's epochs are marked on the trajectory in its color, and each anomaly gets a panel framed on the source's closest approach to the caustic, with the source disk drawn for a finite source. The frame is MulensModel's and VBM's: center of mass at the origin, companion on +x at `s/(1+q)`, primary at `-s q/(1+q)` (MulensModel's trajectory reproduces VBM's `BinaryMag0` there to 4e-15). The axis limits are set BEFORE MulensModel draws, because it sizes the trajectory arrow from the current limits. A linearly moving companion's caustic is drawn at `t0_par`.
- **What MulensModel cannot carry gets no figure and no anomaly zooms**, logged once: a 3+ body lens, a companion on a keplerian orbit, xallarap (`MulensEvent.mulensmodel_unsupported`); anomaly zooms also need a single source. The peak zoom is always drawn.

Fixed alongside: `resolve_auto_vbbl` emitted `VBM` for a POINT-source binary too, which MulensModel refuses without rho, so every point-source binary fit on `backend: mulensmodel` scored logp = NaN at every proposal (DC2018_128: NaN, against vbm_direct's 3373.88; now identical). It emits `point_source` there.

## The limb-darkening LAW reaches the magnification, not just its first coefficient

`Band`'s default law is **quadratic** (`band.py`, `c.get("ld_law", "quadratic")`), and until 2026-08 only `u1` reached the magnification -- `vbm.a1` on the VBM path, `set_limb_coeff_u` on the MulensModel one. On a band whose limb darkening ONLY microlensing reads that had two costs, and the second is the one that makes it a defect rather than an approximation: the source profile was a linear law standing in for a quadratic one, **and** the magnification was a function of `u1` alone, so exactly one combination of the sampled Kipping pair `(q1, q2)` was likelihood-free -- sampled, reported, and constrained by nothing but its prior. It is not caught by the unread-LD autopin, which pins a band *nothing* reads (`components/sed/sed.md`); mulensinstrument is a registered consumer whenever `finite_source` is on. Every shipped example hid it by writing `ld_law: "linear"` by hand, which is the correct workaround and is exactly why the default was a trap for the next user.

- **The measured cost is NOT in the light curve, it is in `u1`.** On `examples/ob09020`, refitting `u1` absorbs the quadratic profile to 0.1-1.3 mmag rms against 3-26 mmag errors -- so it does not measurably bias `rho` there. What it does is bias the fitted `u1` by **+0.14 (22-30%), constant across a factor of 33 in `rho`**. Harmless while `u1` is a free nuisance; a real bias the moment any physical LD prior is imposed on it, which is why this had to land before the atmosphere-model LD prior (`ld_atm_prior`, designed, not implemented). The FSPL case is much larger than the binary central-caustic case -- 14-21 mmag at `u_0 <~ rho`, where the profile shapes the peak directly.
- **Turning the quadratic profile on is a no-op at `u2 = 0`**, measured: VBM's `LDquadratic` reproduces `LDlinear` to 2.2e-16 fractional. That is what makes it safe to key on the band's declared law with no config flag of its own -- an existing linear-band fit cannot move.
- **Guard on the manifest, not the law.** `"u2" in band.manifest` is the test, because with every band linear the parameter does not exist at all (`Band.LD_MODE_TABLE` via `parameterization.mode_manifest` omits a parameter no instance uses). Same test `transit.py` and `rm.py` use.
- **Index the param vector FORWARD.** `_compute` read `u1` as `p[-1]`; with `u2` optionally following, that index silently means a different parameter depending on the band's law. It now counts forward from the companion block, and `_param_labels` names `band.u2` so the non-finite guard can too.
- **MulensModel cannot follow, and says so.** `set_limb_coeff_u` takes one coefficient and the Yoo04 B0/B1 factorization is a linear-law formalism, so `backend: mulensmodel` warns once (`MulensEvent._resolve_quadratic_ld`) naming both fixes -- the default `vbm_direct`, or `ld_law: linear` to make the linear law deliberate and drop the unconstrained coordinate. Warn rather than raise: it describes the topology, and raising would break the A/B reference over a default law the user never chose.
- **Under `backend: vbm_direct` every finite-source single lens goes through VBM's ESPL, whatever the band's law.** It used to keep the MulensModel Op on a linear band, so that existing answers would not move: the two differed by up to ~5 mmag, blamed on Yoo04's table interpolation. Measured against a direct integration of a linear-LD disk (`u1 = 0.55`; the point-lens A integrated analytically in r from the lens, numerically in angle, then over annuli), that attribution was wrong. Yoo04 itself is excellent; the ~5 mmag was the MulensModel backend's own `auto_vbbl` window (`op._build_pspl_model`), which uses a POINT source outside `t_0 +/- 5 rho t_E`, where a finite source still raises A by ~`rho^2/(8 u^2)` (0.5% at 5 rho). Max / rms deviation from the integral, mmag, on trajectories through the peak (81 epochs over `+/- 8 rho t_E`; `u_0 << rho` is `u_0` = 0.05 and 0.1 rho, `u_0 ~ rho` is 0.7, 1.0 and 1.3 rho, `u_0 >> rho` is 3 and 10 rho):

## No gradient through the Op path, loudly (`components/mulensing/op.py`)

`MulensEvent.sampler_requirements` already declares that the Op path is not differentiable, so a gradient request through one of these Ops is a configuration error. All three now say so with a `NotImplementedError` naming the fix (`VBMDirectMagOp` always did; `_MagOpBase` -- the MulensModel A/B reference backend -- and `_MagGradOp` were brought into line in review 2.6.5 / 1.6.3).

- `_MagOpBase.pullback` used to hand back a `_MagGradOp`, silently wiring **N_params+1 full MulensModel light curves per gradient evaluation** for a forward difference carrying O(eps) error. Nothing warned, so the fit ran, burned the CPU, and returned a posterior nobody had reason to distrust -- a worse failure than `VBMDirectMagOp`'s outright refusal precisely because it "worked". `_MagGradOp` is kept as the recipe if this is ever revisited; it is otherwise unreachable.
- **RAISE, not `DisconnectedType()`, and the distinction is the whole point.** `DisconnectedType` asserts the output does not depend on that input. For `_MagGradOp`'s incoming cotangent `g` that assertion is FALSE -- the Op computes `out[i] = sum_t g[t] * D[i,t]`, exactly linear in `g` -- so declaring it disconnected returns a silently ABSENT gradient where a real one exists, which is the same class of bug, only quieter. Nothing is lost by refusing: a second derivative built on a first-order finite difference carries O(eps) error on a quantity that is itself only O(eps) accurate, so the Hessian would be numerically meaningless even if implemented correctly. There is no future in which second-order support is added inside this Op rather than by rewriting the magnification in pytensor.
- `_MagGradOp.connection_pattern` therefore keeps declaring both the parameter vector and `g` **connected**, and that is what makes the raise reachable: declaring `g` disconnected -- the obvious way to "fix" the wrongly shaped VJP it used to return -- would let pytensor skip `pullback` entirely and hand back the absent gradient instead of the error.
- **The symbolic PSPL path stays differentiable.** `MulensEvent.get_magnification` is pytensor all the way down and is what "NUTS-compatible microlensing" means here; only `get_magnification_op`'s Op branch refuses.

## Seeding: the built-in peak finder (`components/mulensing/peakfind.py`)

**Do not seed `t_E`/`pi_E` on top of a physical chain; seed the leaves** (review 8.6.15). With masses, distances and proper motions in the model, `mulensevent.t_E` (= theta_E/mu_rel_geo) and `pi_E_N`/`pi_E_E` (= pi_rel/theta_E along mu_rel_geo) are DERIVED, so a seed on them reaches the model only by back-solving into whichever leaves were left unseeded. Measured on `examples/ob170114`: with the source pm seeds removed and t_E/pi_E re-seeded at the published 173 d/(0.167, 0.127), the engine moved the source pm and the model was built at t_E = 202.65 d, phi_pi = 1.1 deg against 37.25 -- and phi_pi is load-bearing in the C24/C25 modes (`bigomega = phi_pi + xi_Omega + 180`). The recipe: seed `pm_ra`/`pm_dec` of the lens host AND the source (all four leaves; a binary source's companion too) with the masses and distances, and DROP the t_E/pi_E seeds -- they then derive to the published values (`examples/ob170114/ob170114.params.yaml` shows the conversion). |pi_E| is not an independent datum here (pi_rel/theta_E, fixed by the masses and distances); only its direction is. The relaxation engine's own contradiction detector cannot see this case (it needs every symbol of ONE violated relation at user rank), so the report is `ModelAuditor.check_user_starts`'s, and the recipe reaches it as the `seed_remedy` declared on those three parameters in `defaults.yaml` (see `parameter.md`). With every leaf seeded, re-adding a CONSISTENT t_E seed is silent: the derived value already is 173 (172.9987). Tests: `tests/test_mulens_seed_overdetermination.py`.

**The engine's t_E and pi_E relations are GEOCENTRIC, as the model's are** (review 8.6.23). They read `mu_rel_geo = mu_rel_helio - pi_rel * v_earth_perp(t0_par)` (Gould 2004), with Earth's projected velocity supplied as two constants by `MulensEvent.register_frame_constants` (`ConfigManager.add_constant`; zero without microlensing data, where the two frames coincide). The heliocentric form they replaced made a pinned t_E come out 1-9% off in the built model (ob140939 8.6%, ob09020 3.3%, ob08092 1.3%); the geocentric one is exact (`tests/test_ks_engine_examples.py`). It needed the precedence rule of review 3.14.22: under the old alphabetical engine the solvable geocentric form handed a pinned t_E to the lens mass. The heliocentric `mu_rel_mag` stays a reported relation. With both in place the 8.6.15 case above changes: ob170114 with its source pm seeds removed and the published t_E/pi_E re-seeded is now BUILT at t_E = 173 d and phi_pi within 2 deg of 37.25 (the unseeded source pm absorbs them), and nothing is reported. The recipe still holds for a seed the pinned masses and distances cannot give -- |pi_E| = pi_rel/theta_E is theirs, so a pi_E_E seeded at 4x the published value is dropped, reported by `check_user_starts` with the `seed_remedy` (`tests/test_mulens_seed_overdetermination.py`).

**The flux bootstrap reads the event geometry with `ConfigManager.probe_start`** (`MulensInstrument._probe_bootstrap_geometry`, one probe per `load_data`, after the peak finder; review 2.6.30(b)). A t_E derived from masses/distances/proper motions, a q from body masses, and the seed-0 peak-finder hints are all seen; only INFORMED starts count (a defaults.yaml t_0 describes no event). An uninformed t_0/u_0/t_E takes the no-geometry branch (median flux, q_source at its declared start) with a log line naming what is missing -- the invented 30 d is gone -- and an uninformed companion s/q/alpha logs and degrades to point-lens columns. See `src/exozippy/config.md`, "Reading a start before stage 4".

**The flux bootstrap allows a negative blend** (`MulensInstrument._estimate_flux_components`, reviews 2.6.10 and 2.6.23). It solves `F = sum_j f_s,j A_j + f_b` with every source flux `>= 0` and the blend FREE (`_nnls_free_blend` splits `f_b` into two non-negative parts, so when the best blend is positive the solve is exactly the old NNLS); a user `f_source` takes the median residual as the blend, negative or not. `q_source` is clipped only to its DECLARED bounds, read through `resolve` (`_q_source_declared` -- so a user's tighter bound counts), with a warning when the clip moves it; never to a hand-copied range. Do not restore the old shape: NNLS on a ones column (`f_b >= 0`), `max(median, 0)` and a `[0.05, 0.95]` clip forbade exactly the heavy negative blending the flux likelihood was rebuilt for (above), and the clip also turned a ratio computed purely from the user's own `f_source`/`f_blend` entries into a different start (a stated 0.99 became 0.95, and no engine relation back-solves instrument fluxes to restore it). When the free-blend solve finds source light but a non-positive TOTAL -- what a light curve with no baseline coverage does (ob09020's and ob07224's peak-only follow-up files, `A >= 14` throughout, extrapolate the blend to a large negative number) -- the bootstrap WARNS naming the file and re-solves with the blend held `>= 0`; it does NOT take the data median there, which for peak-only data is the magnified flux, an order of magnitude above the baseline. When no positive source flux survives, the bootstrap WARNS naming the file and takes the data's baseline flux and `q_source`'s configured start (the user's entry, else defaults.yaml): the light curve is user input, not an internal invariant, so this is a warning rather than a raise -- but never a silent substitute (it used to return 0.95 without a word, discarding the strongest available sign that the seeded geometry does not describe that band).

**The built-in peak finder is THE microlensing seeder** (review 8.6.25, JDE 2026-09-30: "we should make the peak finder the default (when insufficient information is passed)"; the only one since the MMEXOFAST hand-off was removed, below). `peak_find:` follows the exclusive-booleans rule (an n-way choice is one boolean per alternative, not an enum): absent (the default) -> run when no other seeder registered sets and `peakfind.plan_peak_find` finds a start missing; `true` -> always, on all three; `false` -> never; `peak_find: auto` RAISES (the default is the absent key). `plan_peak_find` asks `ConfigManager.probe_start` (seed 0 included) which of t_0/u_0/t_E has an INFORMED start: none -> the full three-coordinate search and all three pushed (8.4.9); any -> `find_pspl_seed(..., fixed=...)` HOLDS every informed value, fits only the rest (with t_0 held the primary-first pass is skipped, the primary's epoch being known; the degenerate-refinement guards always see the full geometry) and pushes only the rest (8.6.25 part 2; an informed t_0 used to skip the finder and leave u_0/t_E at defaults.yaml, so the flux bootstrap took its no-geometry branch; a user t_E with no t_0 used to be refit -- JDE 2026-10-01: "when the user supplies [t_E], it should respect it"). **Never refit an informed u_0 or t_E**: a t_E the galactic kinematics derive (theta_E/mu_rel from masses, distances and proper motions) is informed through a relation, and replacing it with a PSPL t_E is exactly what `tests/test_seed_quality.py`'s multi-source case caught (it was the reason for the old t_0-only gate, which `plan_peak_find` replaces). The finder runs in `load_data` after pass 1 and BEFORE `_probe_bootstrap_geometry`, so the flux bootstrap sees its result -- keep that order. One seeder per fit: `ConfigManager.add_seed_hints` raises on a second registration (review 2.1.25). **"Missing" is decided by DERIVABILITY, not by presence** -- the informed test is provenance > `PRECEDENCE_DEFAULT`, and the engine gives nearly every mapped path a start from defaults.yaml (a seed on a sampled element, a fill on a derived one), so presence is nearly always true and means nothing. Do not revert this to a literal-key scan: `mulensevent.0.t_E` (from theta_E/mu_rel) and `lens.1.q` (from body masses) are derived, so **no mkparam restart file ever names them**, and a scan would call every complete restart file insufficient and re-seed the trajectory on every second-iteration fit -- the regression that crashed DC2018_128 inside the old MMEXOFAST trigger (review 8.6.22) and that `tests/test_restart_sufficiency.py` pins against the peak finder, on a REAL mkparam restart file, in both topologies.

**The `mmexofast:` and `mmexofast_options:` keys are REMOVED and RAISE** (JDE 2026-10-01: "We're stripping mmexofast from the repo, let's remove the key"). The package, its pyproject group (with sfit-minimizer and emcee, which nothing else used), `mmexofast_support.py` and the `mmexofast_to_params` utility are gone. A config that names either key -- with any value, `false` included -- raises at the boundary (`bodies.REMOVED_KEYS`, checked by `reject_unknown_keys` before the generic unknown-key refusal) with the migration, rather than being silently ignored: it was written around a JSON that would no longer reach the fit. Everything the JSON supplied is ordinary user input now, and every one of those inputs already outranked the JSON (seeds sat at `PRECEDENCE_DERIVED_DATA`, below every user entry): its fits -> per-seed `initval: [...]` lists in the params file (`t_0` minus the JSON's `jd_offset`; `s` as `log_s`; `alpha` by the identity convention); its `excluded_points` -> the instrument's `mask:` (0-based on-disk row indices, skipped for a file with a robust `likelihood:` exactly as before -- see `src/exozippy/components/instrument.md`); its `errfacs` -> `err_scale` initvals. `examples/DC2018/convert_mmexofast_json.py` does that conversion for an existing config and JSON, and on the converted DC2018 configs the start logp is bit-identical to the JSON path. Two consequences to know: (1) a multi-solution set given as user lists needs `seed_polish: true` -- `auto` polishes component seed sets but reads a multi-seed USER set as posterior-draw restarts and leaves it alone (`polish.resolve_polish_steps`), so the converter writes it; (2) the JSON's `sigmas` (init-scale hints for the whitening probe) have no user spelling and need none -- the probe measures the scales (`src/exozippy/whitening.md`). What went with MMEXOFAST and is deliberately NOT replaced: its rho estimate (JDE 2026-10-01: "yes, drop the rho seed" -- no replacement; the peak finder seeds only t_0/u_0/t_E, and rho starts at defaults.yaml or from the source radius the params file implies), and its automatic outlier rejection (use `likelihood: hogg`, or a `mask:`). Its BINARY-LENS SEARCH was not replaced for a year and now is, by `gridsearch.py` (next section) -- which is a different thing in an important way: MMEXOFAST's search ran inside the fit, and the grid runs BEFORE it and hands its result over as ordinary user `initval:` lists. Tests: `tests/test_peakfind_default.py` (the boundary raise and the default seeding), `tests/test_peakfind.py`, `tests/test_restart_sufficiency.py`.

## Lens orbital motion (the companion's `orbit:` and that orbit's `type:`)

**How a companion moves is the TYPE of the orbit it moves on** (review 8.8.14, JDE 2026-10-07: "they should all be the same thing ... orbit: type = linear, quadratic, keplerian, nbody, default to keplerian").  A companion's lens entry names an orbit block with `orbit:`; a `type: keplerian` orbit (the default) derives `s(t)`/`alpha(t)` from its elements, a `type: linear` one supplies the rates as ITS parameters (`orbit.<name>.ds_dt`, `orbit.<name>.dalpha_dt`), anchored at `t0_par`, which is that orbit's epoch by construction (`Orbit._taylor_epochs`).  No `orbit:` is a static geometry.  The per-lens `orbital_motion:` key that used to say this separately -- and could disagree with the orbit -- is REMOVED and raises with the migration (`bodies.REMOVED_KEYS`); so is the event's `source_orbital_motion:` (below).  A `type: linear` orbit is the same Taylor orbit that carries an RV trend (`orbit.<name>.gammadot`, `src/exozippy/components/orbit/orbit.md` "Taylor orbits"): each coefficient is active only where a consumer reads it, so an orbit read by both a lens and the RVs of its primary would carry both sets, independently.  `type: quadratic` on a lens orbit raises (the backends take first derivatives only), as does `type: nbody` anywhere.

Measured on the migration, start logp against master: `examples/ob09020/ob09020.yaml` (keplerian) 36499.80133577527 and `examples/ob170114` (xallarap) 5939.361661315224, both bit-identical; `ob09020_linear.yaml` 33249.53397460201 -> 33249.533974602, one ulp, because moving the rates from the lens to the orbit moves their prior terms to another place in the summed logp -- the same model.

The conventions -- the definitional linear mode, the `gamma_perp = -dalpha_dt` identity, the A16 four-sign mirror, and the keplerian projection's load-bearing minus -- are conventions.md **C24**; read that first. What lives where in the code:

- **The per-epoch geometry is built in ONE place**, `MulensEvent._companion_geometry_series(times)`, from the TIMES ARGUMENT (never `self.time`), so `compile_plotters`' model grids get the same moving geometry the likelihood fits -- the "plotted curve is the fitted curve" rule extends to s(t)/alpha(t) by construction. `get_magnification_op` threads the series to the backend; nothing else may re-derive it.
- **A `type: linear` orbit** carries `ds_dt` [r_E/yr] and `dalpha_dt` [deg/yr user, rad/yr internal] (orbit parameters, read by `_companion_geometry_series` through `lens.motion_orbit_idx`), both anchored at `t0_par` (one anchor for the orbital and parallax terms, 5d), and the lens adds the derived `period_min` (a reported LOWER bound on the period from the projected separation, `orbit.md` "Taylor orbits"; no warning, deliberately) and the derived `beta` -- Skowron A19's sky-plane kinetic/potential ratio -- reported per source and softly bounded below 1 in `build_likelihood` (`soft_upper_bound`, rope not gates: beta_sky is a LOWER bound on the true ratio, so beta < 1 is a necessary bound-orbit condition, and typical binaries sit at 0.25-0.6). This is what makes a full Keplerian unnecessary for a short arc: the bound-orbit physics arrives as a potential, not as 4+ prior-dominated sampled directions.
- **`VBMDirectMagOp(orbital_motion=True)`** takes two extra dmatrix inputs, `S` and `ALPHA` (DEGREES), shape `(n_companions, n_epochs)` -- row j is companion j, a static companion's row its constant (review 8.6.33); the param vector keeps its scalar `s_0`/`alpha_0` entries (the t0_par values), so `_param_labels` and the layout are unchanged. The `n_companions == 1` branch builds its geometry per epoch whether or not the series is present (review 8.6.8 5c's layout merge; `broadcast_to` is a view, so the static case pays nothing). The N-lens branch keeps its single `SetLensGeometry` call for a static lens and, when anything moves, rebuilds the C15 layout and calls `SetLensGeometry` at every epoch (`_magnify_moving`); `tests/test_nbody_lens_motion.py` pins it epoch by epoch against the static N-lens path.
- **`BinaryLensMagOp(orbital_motion=True, t_0_kep=...)`** passes the rates to MulensModel's native LINEAR branch -- the A/B reference `tests/test_lens_orbital_motion.py` pins the per-epoch construction against (that parity test is the linear-mode SIGN test). MulensModel's KEPLERIAN branch is never used for anything: it contradicts its own linear mode by a sign (conventions.md section 6).
- **A companion on a `type: keplerian` orbit requires `backend: vbm_direct`**; the orbit drives the same series from `Orbit.state_vectors` with NO new sampled parameters.

**A fresh defect found while pinning the A/B reference (2026-08-27): MulensModel silently ignores a 2-element magnification-methods list.** `set_magnification_methods([0.0, "VBM"])` -- op.py's spelling since its first version -- applies VBM to NOTHING (the list format is `[t_1, method, t_2, ...]`, and a lone leading epoch closes no bracket), so every finite-source epoch fell back to point source. Measured at a stellar-binary caustic approach: A = 18.55 (point source) against 18.74 (BinaryMag2 and contour agree; a 1% error at the epochs that carry the finite-source information). The existing A/B parity tests never saw it because their planetary-geometry draws have finite-source corrections below the 1e-8 tolerance everywhere. Fixed with `[-np.inf, method, np.inf]` in both builders; only `backend: mulensmodel` finite-source fits (and explicit single-lens `mag_method:` strings, which hit the same else-branch) were affected -- the default `vbm_direct` path never enters MulensModel. Worth reporting upstream: MulensModel accepts the malformed list without complaint.

Also measured here, and why several orbital-motion tests use rtol rather than bitwise equality: **VBMicrolensing's `BinaryMag2` is call-history dependent at the ~1e-14 level** -- two consecutive identical calls on one instance differ in the last bits near a caustic.

## Source orbital motion -- xallarap (`orbit:` on each source entry)

**Every source moves on its own orbit, or not at all** (review 8.6.9, the source twin of 8.6.33's per-companion lens motion). A source moves when its entry in the `source:` block names an orbit with `orbit:`, which must be `type: keplerian` (a linear or quadratic source orbit raises -- the degeneracy below). The source must be ALONE on one side of that orbit: on the primary side its offset is the primary track, `a_1 = a * m_companion / m_total`; on the companion side the opposite track, `a_2 = -a * m_primary / m_total` (`source_offset_from_orbit` is linear in its amplitude). Two sources on ONE orbit, one per side, are the linked binary source -- their shifts are exactly opposite and in the mass ratio -- and the other side of a source's orbit may only be another source if that source names the orbit too. A source inside a group of bodies is a hierarchical system and raises (8.6.33 phase 2), and a lens companion and a source may not share an orbit. `MulensEvent` keeps `source_orbit_idx[j]` / `source_orbit_side[j]` per source slot and `xal_orbit_idxs` (every orbit a source moves on). The event-level `mulensevent.source_orbit:` key that used to move one luminous source is removed and raises with the migration, as does the older `source_orbital_motion:`.

The conventions -- the source's offset entering at exactly the parallax slot, C7's minus, the t0_par anchor, and why the linear mode is refused -- are conventions.md **C25**. Code layout mirrors the lens side: `MulensEvent._source_offset_series(times, system, index)` is the ONE builder, per source slot (likelihood and plotters alike), `physics.source_offset_from_orbit` / `xallarap_trajectory_shift` are the primitives (same kernel and Thiele-Innes owner as everything else), the symbolic PSPL path adds the shift directly (so 1L1S xallarap stays NUTS-differentiable), and `VBMDirectMagOp(source_motion=True)` takes two more per-epoch dvector inputs `(dtau_t, du_t)` -- a finite-source single lens with xallarap routes through the ESPL branch, since the MulensModel single-lens Op has no slot for a per-epoch trajectory shift. NO new sampled parameters: the source orbit's period/eccentricity/orientation and the companion mass (through the barycentric scale `a_1 = a * m_c/m_total`) ARE the parameterization -- Mroz et al. (2026)'s physical-parameter approach expressed through the component graph. MulensModel's native `xi_*` xallarap is NOT wired as a backend (unnecessary: the C25 machinery covers it), but its conventions ARE mapped and mutually verified at the light-curve level -- the closed-form mapping (bigomega = phi_pi + xi_Omega + 180, i = xi_i, omega_* = xi_omega, nu(t_0_xi) = xi_u - xi_omega) lives in conventions.md C25 and is pinned by `test_mm_xi_closed_form_mapping`, so a published xi_* solution drops into a config through six lines; `examples/ob170114` (Mroz et al. 2026 Table B.1) is the shipped worked case. Tests: `tests/test_xallarap.py` (the single source, against independent implementations and MulensModel) and `tests/test_nbody_source_motion.py` (the linked binary source, a static source beside a moving one, one orbit per source, the refusals).

## Where the observer is placed: at the reception instant (C4), with the physical frame velocity (C5)

Two time arguments enter a parallax model and they are not the same number. The trajectory term `(t - t_0)/t_E` takes the data's BJD_TDB tag as is: the lens-source motion is uniform in barycentric time. The observer's position enters at the instant it RECEIVED that light, `bjd - (r_obs . n_hat)/c`, up to 8.3 min earlier, during which the Earth moves 1e-4 AU. Every observer-position lookup in this component -- `MulensInstrument.get_observer_position` (data times and plot grids alike) and `MulensEvent.geocentric_frame` (the anchors at `t0_par`) -- therefore goes through `ephemeris.observer_position_at_reception`, which solves that fixed point with the observer's own position (a spacecraft's from its `.eph` file, whose time column is the state's coordinate time) and only then calls `get_observer_position`. The instrument hands the event the source's line of sight at stage 1 (`set_line_of_sight`); an event with no light curve resolves it from the source star at stage 3; before either, `geocentric_frame` raises rather than guess a direction. The frame velocity is the physical one, a `FRAME_VELOCITY_STEP_DAYS = 0.05` central difference about that instant; it cancels in `u(t)` but sets the reported `t_E` and `pi_E` direction, so it is a convention (C5). Both landed under review 1.10.13, found by the three-code comparison of review 8.14.13: VBMicrolensing already does this for HJD input; BAGLE 1.0.5 and EXOZIPPy each carried one face of the inconsistency. Tests: `tests/test_ephemeris_reception_instant.py`.

## Astrometric microlensing -- the centroid shift (`astrometryinstrument` <- `MulensEvent.get_centroid_shift`)

The convention -- referenced to the SOURCE's unlensed position, pointing AWAY from the lens, VBM's `astrox` being the same centroid measured from the lens, and the blend dilution/drag -- is conventions.md **C30**; the point-lens amplitude and the finite-source cancellation are recorded below ("The point-lens shift, exactly"); stages 1 and 2 landed in #324 and #327 (review 8.10.1). What lives where in the code:

- **One trajectory, two consumers.** `MulensEvent.get_trajectory(times, obs_pos, system, index)` returns the `(tau, beta)` pair `get_magnification` always built inline (same op sequence, bit-identical); `get_centroid_shift` rotates it onto `(N, E)` with C9's `tau_hat = mu_hat_rel,geo` and `beta_hat = tau_hat` rotated +90 degrees North through East, and scales by `-theta_E/(u^2+2)`. The direction comes from `mu_ra_rel_geo`/`mu_dec_rel_geo`, not from `pi_E`: the light curve fixes only `|u(t)|`, and a fit with `|pi_E|` pinned at zero still has a trajectory direction -- this term is what makes it observable.
- **The frame has one owner.** `MulensEvent.geocentric_frame()` holds the Skowron+2011 anchors (Earth's position and velocity at `t0_par`, C5) and `skowron_deviations(t, xyz_abs)` builds the C6 deviations; `MulensInstrument._abs_to_delta` delegates to it, and so does `AstrometryInstrument` for its own epochs and observer. `t0_par` is read from `t0_par[0]` at call time because `MulensInstrument.load_data` re-resolves it at stage 1 -- the frame must not be cached against the `__init__` snapshot.
- **The seam is `AstrometryInstrument._absolute_model`, last.** `_apply_lens` adds `w_s * delta_theta + w_b * (x_b - x_s)` onto the five-parameter (+ photocenter-orbit) track, `w_s = A/(A + g)`, `w_b = g/(A + g)`, `g = f_blend/f_source` of the `photometry:` light curve (only the RATIO enters; the astrometric band is that light curve's band). The shift is a sky angle and takes NO further `cos(dec)`: East on those lines is already projected. `_absolute_model` now takes optional `P_E`/`P_N`/`dev` tensors so `compile_plotters` can run the SAME builder on a plot grid with the grid's own ephemeris (`_lens_ephemeris`) -- the instrument.md "one builder" rule, extended to this component's absolute modes.
- **Activation is automatic, opt-out explicit.** `_resolve_lens_hook` (stage 3) turns the term on for every gaia/abs dataset whose `star_ndx` is a `source` body when a `mulensevent:` exists; `microlensing: false` opts out; `microlensing: true` where it cannot apply (rel mode, no event, not a source) is an error. `blend_dE`/`blend_dN` are declared only when some dataset is lensed and are INACTIVE elsewhere (the fluxfrac pattern). **A lensed dataset with no microlensing light curve RAISES** (`_resolve_lens_photometry`; JDE 2026-09-23: "we should raise when astrometry is fit without photometry. that can't happen"): the blend fraction has no other source and in a bulge field its term can dominate, and the geocentric frame's proper-motion correction (`_earth_vperp_en`) is anchored by the light curve's `t0_par` and Earth velocity, without which `mu_rel_geo`, and so the direction of the shift, would silently fall back to heliocentric.
- **Two paths, one entry point.** `MulensEvent.get_astrometric_terms(times, obs_pos, system, index, u1, u2, bandpass)` returns `(A, delta_N, delta_E)` from one evaluation and is what `_apply_lens` calls. A point-source single lens takes the symbolic pair above and stays differentiable. Finite source, binary, N-lens, or a forced `use_op` go through `VBMDirectMagOp(astrometry=True)` (stage 2): VBM's `astrox1/astrox2` are instance state filled by every magnification call (PSPLMag, ESPLMag2, BinaryMag0/2, MultiMag0/2), so the Op reads them after the call it already makes -- two more outputs, `(dtau, dbeta)` on the trajectory axes, not a second VBM call and not a mirror Op. `_vbm_op_call` is the ONE builder of a VBM Op call (param layout, flags, per-epoch series) for the photometric branches and the astrometric one alike, so the two are built from the same parameters and differ only in the flag. Three measured facts (op.py's class docstring; pinned in `tests/test_astrometric_microlensing_op.py`): the `astrox` origin is the lens centre of mass with the primary at `(-s q/(1+q), 0)`; a scalar-`u` call leaves `astrox2` stale, so the single-lens branch projects `astrox1` radially; and the flag moves the binary magnifications' last bit at some epochs, so the astrometric Op is a separate instance and photometry stays bit-identical. Always VBM regardless of `backend:`; gradient-free like the photometry.
- **Not split out (follow-ups, review 8.10.1):** the lens's own flux inside `f_b` sits at `x_s + theta_E dtheta` and would need the SED-predicted lens flux (`sed_constrains_blend` has it); a blend with its own proper motion; the relative-astrometry (`rel`) modes, whose reference stars have partially absorbed the signal. So an abs dataset of a source RESOLVED from its lens years later (HST, Roman) must not carry the light curve's blend drag, whose `f_b` contains the now-resolved lens: set `microlensing: false` on it (the centroid shift it drops is `theta_E/u`, tens of uas at `u ~ 10`), or better, measure the pair with `lensrel` (next section).

## The lens's position is derived from the source's (`mulensevent.lens_offset_e/n`, `astrometryinstrument` `lensrel`)

**The light curve already says where the lens is relative to the source at every instant** (review 8.10.4, JDE 2026-10-24: "We know the source and lens offset at t0. So can't we actually make the lens position a deterministic of the source and mulensing parameters?"). C9's lens-minus-source separation seen from the geocenter at `t0_par`, where the Skowron deviation vanishes by construction, is `theta_E [((t0_par - t_0)/t_E) tau_hat + u_0 beta_hat]`; seen from the observer each star is displaced by its own parallax times the parallax factor, so the BARYCENTRIC separation is that minus `pi_rel P_earth(t0_par)`; and the barycentric separation moves with the HELIOCENTRIC relative proper motion. Hence `physics.lens_offset_en`: the offset at the astrometric reference epoch is `theta_E dtheta_geo(t0_par) - pi_rel P_earth(t0_par) + mu_rel,helio (epoch - t0_par)`. It is exact, not a linearization: the residual against the trajectory at any epoch is `(mu_geo - mu_helio + pi_rel v_perp)(t - t0_par)`, zero by `calc_mu_*_rel_geo`. `tests/test_lens_track.py` measures the model's lens track minus source track against `theta_E` times the light curve's own trajectory at the observer to 1e-6 mas from 300 d before the event to 11 yr after it.

- **Where it applies: wherever the lens position is MEASURED.** `MulensEvent.lens_track_star` is true when the primary lens star is the target of a gaia/abs astrometry dataset; then `Star._radec_manifest_entry` flips that star's `ra`/`dec` element to the `from_mulens_track` expression (source's position + `lens_offset_e/n`, the `fitmurel` per-element pattern), mulensevent declares `lens_offset_e`/`lens_offset_n` (reported: the predicted separation at the epoch), and the SOURCE's `ra`/`dec` are released from the microlensing pin (a pinned catalog position would pin the lens track at ~0.1 arcsec precision). Elsewhere nothing changes: a lens position nothing reads is a bookkeeping pin, and deriving it would only add a soft-barrier potential to every microlensing fit (start logp of every shipped example is unchanged). A star that may NOT be the lens -- the hypothesis a resolved neighbor tests -- is simply not the lens body, and keeps a free position.
- **Never a link.** The old workaround, `star.Lens.ra: {initval: star.Source.ra, sigma: 0}`, was wrong twice: the two positions differ by `theta_E u_0` at `t_0` and by `mu_rel` times the elapsed time after it, and a linked coordinate reads as the defaults.yaml placeholder at stage 1 (review 1.1.10). A user number on a derived lens `ra` is a start on a derived element and is reported by `check_user_starts` if it disagrees.
- **Origins.** `t_0`, `u_0` and `alpha` refer to the lens CENTER OF MASS (C12), which is what moves rectilinearly; the luminous primary sits at `physics.host_offset_from_com` from it (C15's companion vector times `-q/(1+q)`), from the companion geometry at `t0_par` -- the `binary` expression, and `MulensEvent.host_offset` for the trajectory consumers. The orbit's RATES are deliberately not extrapolated to an imaging epoch years away (a Taylor orbit means nothing there). For a planet the host's reflex is `~q s theta_E` (uas); for a STAR companion the binary has orbited and a luminous companion shifts the photocenter, which warns. Two companions or more raise.
- **Xallarap raises for the abs derivation**: the source's `ra`/`dec` track is the source system's barycenter, and its orbital offset at the reference epoch is not in `lens_offset_en`. `lensrel` (below) needs no such term -- it is the trajectory itself, xallarap shift included.
- **Stage-1 readers.** A derived element's resolved initval is a placeholder, not a position, so the two readers that want a START position read the star it derives from (`Star.position_root`): the astrometry tangent point of the lens's dataset (`AstrometryInstrument.load_data`) and the galactic model's frozen sight line (anchored on a derived lens it reads the source and logs so; the two differ by mas, and the freeze is harmless to ~1 arcmin).

**`mode: lensrel` is the resolved-pair observable.** A pair fit to one image gives the lens's position minus the source's, `(dE, dN)` with errors and their correlation; the file is `time dE dN err_E err_N corr`. Two reasons it is not two abs datasets. A frame-registration error shared by both stars in one image cancels in the difference, while a pair of abs datasets absorbs it into the source proper motion and biases both absolute proper motions (0.5 mas per epoch moved them by exactly that over two epochs at zero chi2 cost, in the 2026-10 two-epoch simulation behind review 8.10.4) -- which matters because the galactic kinematic prior levers absolute proper motions onto `D_S`. And a blended pair's two positions are strongly anti-correlated, which abs rows cannot say. The model (`AstrometryInstrument._lensrel_model`) is `theta_E` times the trajectory at the observer, plus the host offset, minus the source's own centroid shift (the source is measured through its images; the lens is not lensed), with no blend dilution (the pair is resolved). The likelihood is the bivariate normal written as the East marginal times the North conditional (two observed nodes, exact); the jitter adds isotropically to both variances, floored at `-0.95` of the smallest data-covariance eigenvalue so the matrix stays positive definite. It requires the event's light curve, as a lensed abs dataset does (frame anchor, limb darkening of the centroid shift).

Tests: `tests/test_astrometric_microlensing.py` (closed form vs a direct image solve; the handedness test; the dilution/drag algebra; theta_E is consumed by the astrometric likelihood and not by an opted-out dataset; binary raises, finite source warns; a self-consistent synthetic recovery with finite gradient).

### The point-lens shift, exactly -- and why a resolved source breaks it

A point lens splits a point source into two images on the lens-source axis, at `y_+- = (u +- sqrt(u^2+4))/2` (units of theta_E, from the lens) with `A_+- = (u^2+2)/(2 u sqrt(u^2+4)) +- 1/2`. Summing `A_+- y_+-`, the flux-weighted centroid relative to the LENS is `theta_c = theta_E u (u^2+3)/(u^2+2)` (VBM's `astrox`), and relative to the source's UNLENSED position -- what an astrometric time series references -- the shift is `delta_theta = theta_E u/(u^2+2)` along the lens->source axis, peaking at `u = sqrt(2)` with `sqrt(2)/4 theta_E = 0.354 theta_E`. It is exact because the point-lens lens equation is a quadratic (the same reason Paczynski's `A(u)` is exact): a direct image solve agrees to 1e-14 - 1e-16 relative across `u = 1e-2` to 10, the residual growing only where `centroid - u` is a catastrophic cancellation in the DIRECT computation.

The point-source assumption is not safe on a resolved source. Averaging the exact per-element shift over a uniform disk of radius `rho`:

| `u_0` | `rho` | point source | finite (uniform) | frac. error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.100 | 0.01 | 0.0497512 | 0.0493780 | 7.5e-03 |
| 0.100 | 0.10 | 0.0497512 | 0.0001805 | 1.0e+00 |
| 0.100 | 0.50 | 0.0497512 | -0.0230882 | 1.5e+00 |
| 1.414 | 0.10 | 0.3535534 | 0.3525205 | 2.9e-03 |
| 1.414 | 0.50 | 0.3535534 | 0.3266657 | 7.6e-02 |
| 1.414 | 1.00 | 0.3535534 | 0.2295702 | 3.5e-01 |
| 3.000 | 1.00 | 0.2727273 | 0.2629292 | 3.6e-02 |

With the lens inside the source disk (`u_0 = 0.1`, `rho = 0.1`) the shift nearly VANISHES (99.6% cancellation: elements on opposite sides of the lens are deflected in opposite directions), and at `rho = 0.5` it REVERSES SIGN. So the closed form on a resolved source can get the DIRECTION wrong, which is why finite-source events dispatch to VBM's disk-integrated centroid. The two theta_E routes complement each other: finite-source events already have theta_E from `rho`, and the astrometric route earns its keep on the events that do not, which are exactly those where the point-source formula is valid.

## Three or more lens bodies: one relation set per companion

**Every companion has its own relations, and the total lens mass is a sum over every body** (review 8.6.13). `symbolic_physics.get_symbol_map` returns one symbol map per source (the event chain) and then ONE MAP PER COMPANION: companion `j` (lens element `j`; element 0 is the masked primary) maps `q_lens`, `companion_mass`, `primary_lens_mass` and -- unless it moves on a keplerian orbit -- its geometry (`alpha`'s pair, `s <-> log_s`). So `q_j * M_primary = M_j` and the geometry relations instantiate once per companion, for any number of lens bodies. The total mass `mulensevent.0.mlens_total = sum of every lens body's mass` has a config-dependent number of terms, so it cannot sit in the fixed `RELATIONS` list; it comes from `get_extra_relations`, the engine's hook for relations written directly in full-path symbols (`components/components.md`).

Why it matters, measured on examples/KMT-2021-BLG-1122L with t_E seeded at 30 d, the companions' `q` at 0.3 and 0.05, and no body-mass entries. Before, only lens element 1 was mapped and the mass sum existed for exactly two bodies, so `mlens_total` had no relation to the body masses: the engine satisfied the seeded t_E by moving `mlens_total` -- a derived parameter whose start never reaches the model -- and the model started at t_E = 124.7 d with both companions at the default 1 Msun (q = 3.16). That is review 1.1.12's "silently not delivered" seeds and review 2.6.34's "t_E not derivable at stage 1". After, all three starts are delivered and the body masses follow from them (`check_user_starts`: 0 misses). The 3+ body hint block in `MulensEvent.register_parameters` -- which seeded `mlens_total` and the extra companions' `q`/`log_s` from user body masses, and warned that a 3+ body lens REQUIRED explicit masses -- is gone with it, and so is its stage-3 mass read, which raised on per-seed (list) mass initvals.

`Lens._validate_q_start` now treats a NaN q start as fatal on every active element: every companion is solved, so a NaN means a failed solve, not the bookkeeping initval of an unsolved element (the review 1.6.5 exemption it replaces).

**Lens orbital motion is per companion too** (review 8.6.33). Each companion's `orbit:` names its own orbit, linear or keplerian, and a companion without one stays static beside moving ones. `Lens` keeps `companion_motion[j]` and `companion_orbit_idx[j]` per companion slot, and fills its per-element orbit maps (`lens_kep_orbit_map`, `lens_motion_orbit_map`) so element j + 1 reads companion j's own orbit. `MulensEvent._companion_geometry_series` returns one row per companion: C24's linear series from that companion's rates, the keplerian geometry from that companion's orbit, or the static constant. Each linearly moving companion gets its own bound-orbit potential (`lens.bound_orbit_<element>`; a single one keeps the name `lens.bound_orbit`). Each companion's orbit is primary <-> that companion. Two companions on ONE orbit (a companion group, e.g. a planet orbiting the secondary) would need positions composed through the orbit's body groups, so `Lens` refuses it -- phase 2 of 8.6.33, built when an event needs it. The `mulensmodel` backend stays binary-only.

## The binary mode finder: topology is found BEFORE the fit (`components/mulensing/gridsearch.py`)

**`s = 1.0` is not a neutral start, it is the s <-> 1/s symmetry point.** `lens.log_s`'s defaults.yaml `initval: 0.0` puts every unseeded binary fit exactly on the close/wide reflection axis, so the topology is decided at initialization by an initialization that contains no topology information. The sampler cannot repair that later: DC2018 event 128's close and wide solutions are **42,563 nats** apart, and a `T_max = 200` ladder's hottest rung sees 213 of them, which is uncrossable (T_max = 8500 would bring it to 5). It is also not a rare case -- on the two CLEAN central-caustic DC2018 events (069, 258) the best fit sits on the **mirror of the truth to better than 1.5%**, so a search reporting one best fit hands back the wrong topology with no sign that it did. Measured on the RMDC26 pilot: all four sampler arms sat at `s < 1` with 0-10 round trips and never visited the wide half.

**So the search is an ENUMERATION, run before the fit, and it is not an optimization of the fit's own posterior.** `grid_search(curves, pspl, spec)` grids (log s, log q) and re-minimizes (t_0, u_0, t_E, alpha) at every node from several alpha starts; `GridResult.local_minima(k)` returns K distinct basins and `seed_fragment` writes them as the params file the fit then reads. Three things about it that look like choices and are measurements:

- **The nuisances MUST be re-minimized at each node.** The first version of this gridded (s, q, alpha) with t_0/u_0/t_E frozen at the point-lens fit and ranked the truth **35th to 144th** among local minima. Event 163's point-lens fit is off by 2% in u_0 and 1.7% in t_E, and that alone is ~11,565 chi2 over its 38,568 points -- on a sharply peaked light curve the "nuisances" dominate and bury the binary signal.
- **The basin is far too small to stumble into.** Within dchi2 <= 100 of the global minimum the truth basin holds 2, 16 and 55 nodes of 2911 on events 163, 069 and 194 -- **0.07% to 1.9%** of the (s, q) plane. That is the argument for enumerating rather than sampling, and it applies to every binary event, not only the high-magnification ones.
- **rho is optimized OUTSIDE the simplex** (`rho_mode: separate`, the default). rho sets the smoothing of a caustic crossing, not its location or width, so it is largely independent of s and q; a 1-D Brent search at the 4-D optimum costs ~15 evaluations against the simplex's ~600, where putting log10 rho in the NM vector costs a measured **3.4x** (70 nodes/h against 237).

**A COARSER grid is cheaper, not more robust, and the difference matters.** Decimating a finished grid and asking where the coarse global minimum lands gives "same basin" at strides 3, 5 and 10 -- but "same basin" there means *within two grid steps AT THAT STRIDE*, a tolerance that grows with the stride, so large strides pass by construction. Read the same table the other way and the fine grid is strictly better: stride 1 is **exact** on all three events where every coarse stride is merely "same basin", and stride 2 **missed outright on 069**, an aliasing accident the fine grid cannot have. Whether a coarse node is inside the polish's convergence radius is the untested step (notes 2.4.14). Do not coarsen to save time without re-opening that question.

**Two start-population flags whose correct value is the opposite of the obvious one**, both set by `seed_fragment` and both worth knowing before hand-writing a grid seed:

- **`overdisperse: TRUE` for K basins.** The key answers one question (samplers.md, 8.3.3): are these K points K MODES that still want scattering, or K draws from one posterior that are already dispersed? Grid basins are the first. `false` means "use these exactly", so every chain starts on one of K points with **no jitter** and the start population's affine hull is the hull of those K points -- 5 basins against a 34-parameter model spans 3 directions where `n_params + 2 = 36` are needed to span the space at all, and the only escape is `DE_JITTER = 1e-4`, ~1e8 accepted steps per whitened sigma. `_common.warn_if_seed_population_degenerate` says exactly this at sampler start.
- **`seed_polish: true`, in the config's `sampler:` block and NOT in the params file.** `auto` resolves to **zero** steps for any multi-seed set without component hints (`polish.resolve_polish_steps`), which leaves every seed sitting exactly on its grid node -- and a node is the best point on a LATTICE, up to half a node-spacing from the basin's real minimum. `seed_polish` is a sampler key; writing it into the params file would not reach the polish and would be registered as a **leaf symbol** by `finalize_user_params`, an orphaned ledger row for a parameter that does not exist (CLAUDE.md, the `user_params` invariant). `gridsearch.sampler_overrides()` returns it for a generator that writes both files.

**What the grid may NOT seed: `t_E` and `rho`.** Both are DERIVED wherever a galactic prior is in play -- `t_E = theta_E/mu_rel_geo` from masses, distances and proper motions, `rho = theta_star/theta_E` from the source radius and distance -- so an `initval` on either is a silent no-op, which is why `SEED_LEAVES` is t_0/u_0/log_s/log_q/alpha and the measured t_E and rho go into the fragment's COMMENTS instead. Not theoretical: RMDC26 event 096's params file seeds both, and the run's own auditor reports `mulensevent.0.t_E: you set 8.42027, the model starts at 9.7949 (+16.33%)` and `source.0.rho: you set 0.00662299, the model starts at 0.000976985 (-85.25%)`. To convey a t_E, seed the LEAVES (the lens and source proper motions, with the masses and distances) -- the recipe is in "Seeding: the built-in peak finder" above, review 8.6.15.

**The window: `trim_t_E`, default +/-10 t_E.** Every node pays for every epoch, and a survey light curve spans years where the event spans weeks. Measured on RMDC26 event 096 (49,096 epochs over 1717 d, t_E = 8.42 d): +/-3 t_E keeps 3,678 (13.3x fewer), +/-5 t_E 5,618, +/-10 t_E 8,185 (6.0x) -- and +/-20 t_E keeps the **same** 8,185, because 10 t_E already reaches the ends of the observing season. 10 rather than 3 because the wings are what separate a binary from a point lens at a different t_E, and a tight window lets a wrong-t_E solution hide outside it; at 10 t_E the magnification is within 1.00002 of baseline, so the window is "the season", not a choice about the event.

**It is blind, and it is NOT the fit's chi2.** The start comes from `peakfind.find_pspl_seed` on the same curves; nothing reads an answer key. But every node is a **static, geocentric** binary -- no parallax, no lens orbital motion, no xallarap -- so chi2 values are comparable to each other and not to a fit's. `curves_from_system` takes the data through `System.prepare()` rather than re-reading the files, so the search sees the masks, the detrending and the magnitude->flux conversion the fit sees; `mulensinstrument.mag_to_flux` is that conversion's one owner for the same reason. `maxtime` returns a partial grid, and the task order is **shuffled** so a partial grid is a uniform subsample of the whole box: in row-major order a timed-out run would have evaluated a slab of low log_s and never looked at the wide half, reporting a confident global minimum on exactly the question it failed to ask. Tests: `tests/test_gridsearch.py`.


## Microlensing body roles (`components/mulensing/lens.py`)

Three rules, all enforced in `MulensEvent._validate_bodies` and all raising: the **primary** (first) lens body must be a `star`, **every** source body must be a `star`, and a body may not be both lens and source.

The why is a type erasure. `lens_map` / `source_map` carry only an **index**; the component type is discarded, and every primary-side and source-side dependency is hard-coded to the star component (`star.mass[lens_map]`, `star.distance`, `star.pm_ra`/`pm_dec`, `star.radius`, plus `build_likelihood`'s `d_l`). A `planet.0` primary therefore silently modeled `star.0`: measured on `examples/ob08092`, `lenses: ["planet.0"]` built a `theta_E` **bit-identical** to `lenses: ["star.0"]`, responded to that star's mass, was completely insensitive to the planet's, and reported a healthy logp and a lens mass that never entered the likelihood. Planet **companions** were never affected -- their mass deps carry the component type -- so only the primary slot is restricted. Same erasure on the source side, where the case is stronger still: a planet is not a self-luminous point source at bulge distances, so a non-star source is physically meaningless rather than merely unimplemented. `lens_map`'s dual role (the lensing **mass** and the kinematic **host** whose distance/proper motion feed the galactic prior) coincides only because the primary is always a star; keep that in mind before generalizing it.

Planet-as-lens was **not** implemented, deliberately. A planet body has no `distance` or proper motion of its own, and `galacticmodel.build_likelihood` reads `system.star` only -- so a free-floating planet declared as a `planet` would lose both the galactic density prior and the kinematic prior, leaving the lens distance unidentifiable. The supported recipe is therefore a `star` block with a low `star.<name>.logmass` **and** `mass_function: ffp` (next section). Both halves are needed: a star block alone still draws the stellar IMF, which is what the guard's error message now says.


## Choosing a coordinate set: the swaps are conditioning, not correctness (review 8.6.7)

The coordinate swaps -- `fitmurel` (sample the relative proper motion), `fitpirel` (`log_pi_rel`), `fitthetae` (`log_theta_E`), `fitu0te` (`u_0 * t_E`, per source), plus `log_s` and the `star_constrains_rho` severance -- change WHERE the sampler walks, never the model: each carries its exact change-of-variables Jacobian. Which set to use was measured, not argued. A/B on DC2018 event 128 (2026-08-27; every arm with the capped Hogg mixture of review 8.6.3, against a cap-only baseline):

| arm | rho | mixing of the latent block | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| cap-only baseline | 0.00713(20) | -- | healthy |
| `fitmurel` only | 0.00713(20) | logmass ESS 7306 | healthy (best) |
| `fitu0te` only | 0.00713(20) | unimodal, global | healthy |
| all swaps together | 0.00713(20) | latent ESS 1200-7300 | healthy |
| `fitpirel` only | 0.00713(20) | logmass ESS 300 | under-mixed |
| `fitthetae` only | -- | 30 of 52 chains frozen | stuck |

Three conclusions, each of which looks like a judgment call and is not:

1. **The Hogg cap alone recovers rho.** The swaps are conditioning, not correctness: no arm moved rho, and a fit that is wrong without a swap is wrong for another reason.
2. **The swaps interact non-additively, and a single swap can hurt.** Sampling `log_theta_E` alone turns the mass-parallax ridge into a `log_theta_E`-`log_mu_rel` ridge; only composing it with `fitmurel` relieves it. So: **use the full swap set together, or `fitmurel` alone. NEVER `fitpirel` alone or `fitthetae` alone.** A log-polar `mu_rel` coordinate was not needed and is not implemented.
3. **Healthy arms still disagree on the prior-dominated latent block** (lens and source masses and distances) by 1.0-3.2 posterior widths -- on event 128, D_l spans 1924-3698 pc and D_s 2081-7334 pc across healthy arms. A sweep that reports latent masses or distances must fix one parameterization and say which, or report the spread as a systematic. The observables (`t_E`, `rho`, `pi_E`, `theta_E` where the SED ties it) agree.

The severance question was settled the same way. Severing `star_constrains_rho` hands `mu_rel` to the galactic kinematic prior, and event 128's true `mu_rel` (1.814 mas/yr) is atypically slow against a prior that prefers ~11, so every severed arm put `theta_E` 6.6x high. Relinked, with the source `teff`/`radius` free and `feh ~ N(0, 0.5)`, the fit gives `mu_rel` 1.469 [1.158, 1.758] against the truth 1.814, `theta_E` 0.0733 against 0.0905, and `rho` unchanged at 0.00708. The 601-nat backward tie that had motivated severing was a stiff solar pin on the source star, not the tie itself. Keep the link; do not sever to escape a tension without first checking what is pinned.

## The event's derived observables are in the trace, and a point source still reports rho (review 2.6.14)

`t_E`, `theta_E`, `pi_rel`, `mu_rel_mag`, `pi_E_N`/`pi_E_E` and the lens masses are pure expressions of the sampled physical state. They are now `pm.Deterministic`s (the general "every reported derived parameter is a node" rule in `src/exozippy/components/parameter.md`), so the trace `run.py` writes before wrap-up carries them, in every coordinate configuration (default, `fitmurel`, `fitpirel`, `fitthetae`, `fitu0te`): a wrap-up that dies -- three OOM kills on one model -- now costs formatting, not physics. `MulensEvent.plot_corner` still reads `Parameter.posterior`, which is now the trace's copy.

**A point-source event reports `source.rho`** (JDE 2026-09-14: rho should appear "even if finite_source=False"). `rho = theta_star/theta_E` is determined by the source star's radius and distance and the event's `theta_E` whether or not the light curve resolves the source, and publishing it on both arms gives a point-source fit and a finite-source one a common column. It is declared as a REPORTED role (`output_expr_key: default`, `Source.register_parameters`): nothing may consume it, so it cannot reach the magnification, and a reported element takes only the constraints the USER wrote -- the defaults.yaml soft upper barrier on rho is a statement about finite-source magnification and never becomes a logp term on a model rho is not part of (start logp bit-identical on every shipped point-source example). It is `report_only` (kept out of the burn-in scan and the global corner) and stays out of the lens-geometry corner, which keeps "rho only for finite-source events". Where the source star is constrained only by its prior, the reported rho is a prior product like any other derived stellar quantity. Tests: `tests/test_derived_in_trace.py`.

## The event-rate prior: what it weights, and what it does to ln Z (`MulensEvent.build_likelihood`)

`mulensevent.event_rate_prior` is `log(mu_rel_geo * theta_E)` on the event-level scalars (element 0): the rate at which a lens sweeps a source, `Gamma ~ 2 theta_E mu_rel` per lens-source pair, which turns the Galactic population prior (densities, `d^2` volumes and velocities, `galacticmodel`, and the mass function) into the prior on parameters **of an observed event**. `theta_E` is the **total** lens mass's (C13).

**It is not normalized, and that is deliberate (2026-10).** Its normalizer is the mean rate under the population prior, `Gamma_bar = E_prior[mu_rel theta_E]`, an integral over both stars' positions and velocities and the lens mass. What matters for comparing architectures is how `Gamma_bar` differs between them, and that is known exactly:

- **1L1S vs 1L1S_fs**: the same factor on the same quantities -- identical.
- **1L2S** (a second source star whose distance and proper motion are hard-linked to the first's, `star.md`, "Linked stars: one position, one density term"): the term reads element 0 only, and `mu_rel` and `theta_E` are those of the one shared position and motion, so the second source adds **no** factor -- identical to 1L1S. (A second source at an independent distance would be a different physical event, and the rate of "either source is lensed" is not modeled.)
- **2L1S**: `theta_E = sqrt(kappa M_host (1 + q) pi_rel)`, so the per-sample weight differs from 1L1S by `sqrt(1 + q)` -- a function of `q`, not a constant. With the companion in `log_q` mode (the default for a lens body), `q`'s prior is independent of everything the 1L1S prior contains, so the normalizer FACTORIZES exactly: `Gamma_bar(2L1S) = Gamma_bar(1L1S) * E_q[sqrt(1 + q)]`. For `log_q ~ U(-6, 0)` (the DC2018 architecture set) `ln E_q[sqrt(1+q)] = 0.0322` nats; for the defaults.yaml `U(-9, 1)`, `0.1261`. In linear mass mode `q = m_p/M_host` is not independent of the host's mass prior, and the ratio is `E[sqrt(1 + m_p/M_host)]` under the joint prior -- still a constant.

**Ruling: the `sqrt(1 + q)` weight is physical and stays, and the evidence keeps the rate ratio.** A lens of total mass `M (1 + q)` has a larger Einstein ring and genuinely produces more events, so within 2L1S the weight correctly tilts `q` toward larger values. With the unnormalized weight, `Z_arch = Gamma_bar_arch * p(data | arch, event)`, so `ln Z(2L1S) - ln Z(1L1S)` already contains `ln E_q[sqrt(1+q)]`: the posterior odds are right when the prior odds `P(arch)` are **population** fractions (the fraction of lenses carrying such a companion -- what an occurrence rate is). Only if the prior odds are fractions of **events** must that constant be subtracted from `ln Z(2L1S)` by hand; never do both. The common factor `Gamma_bar(1L1S)` is the same in every architecture with the same lens/source priors and cancels in any difference; it is NOT computed, so an absolute `ln Z` carries it (as PR #465's "not covered" list records).

Other architecture-dependent prior terms an evidence comparison should know about (none from this component is a hidden non-constant): `source_behind_lens` is a per-SOURCE soft barrier, so a linked second source applies the same barrier on `D_s - D_l` twice -- negligible (its transition is 4.4 pc wide around a 10 pc offset); a second source star's own relation potentials (`torres`/`mann`) drop their `-log(sigma sqrt(2 pi))` constants (`relations.py`), a per-relation constant present only in 1L2S; and the companion's/second source's own bounded coordinates (`log_q`, `log_s`, `xalpha`/`yalpha`, `log_q_flux`, `rho`) are normalized only through PR #465's `evidence_log_norm`.
