# Bands, filters, limb darkening, and the SED

`src/exozippy/components/band/` carries filter identity and limb darkening;
`src/exozippy/components/sed/` carries the broadband photometry, the bolometric-correction
grid and the cross-component flux predictions.

Read this before touching limb darkening, filter resolution, the BC grid or an SED flux
hook. Related: `src/exozippy/components/instrument.md` (the instruments that reference a
band), `src/exozippy/components/mulensing/mulensing.md` (the `zeropoint` consumer),
`src/exozippy/components/parameter.md` (how the `loggsed` barrier reports itself).

## Bands, filters, and SED flux constraints

The Band component is the single carrier of filter identity and limb darkening: instruments (`transit`, `mulensinstrument`, optionally `astrometryinstrument`) reference a `band:` block by name; each band's `filter:` string is resolved through the shared alias table (`src/exozippy/filters/filternames.txt`, columns Keivan/MIST/Claret/SVO/VOID) into canonical names at load time. Transit LD (q1/q2/u1/u2) lives on Band, not on the transit component.

`ld_law:` is `quadratic` (Kipping q1/q2, u1/u2 derived) or `linear` (u1 sampled, **no `u2`** -- its second coefficient is exactly 0). An unrecognized spelling raises at load; **bands may declare different laws**, expressed through the per-element roles (`Band.LD_MODE_TABLE`, fed to `parameterization.mode_manifest`): `u1` is derived on the quadratic bands and sampled on the linear ones, while `q1`/`q2`/`u2` are INACTIVE on a linear band -- not parameters of it, so they are pinned (u2 at exactly 0, via `inactive_value`), carry no potential, and print no table row. A mixed system used to be a hard **error**, because `Parameter.build_pymc` derived a whole vector or none of it; before that, `any(law != "linear")` chose quadratic for everyone and silently gave a declared-linear band a free `u2`. The documented workaround (quadratic everywhere with the linear bands' `q2` pinned at 0.5) is no longer needed and its prior-in-q1-rather-than-u1 cost is gone. Which coordinate the unread-band autopin pins is therefore per band too (`Band.LD_SAMPLED_PARAMS`): a `sigma` on a derived element is a silent no-op, so pinning `u1` on a quadratic band would pin nothing. **Every consumer of `u2` must still guard with `"u2" in band.manifest`** and fall back to `pt.zeros_like(u1)` -- that case is now "every band is linear", where the parameter does not exist at all (`mode_manifest` omits a parameter no instance uses); `transit.py` (both sites) and `rm.py` do. `limbdark.quad_limb_darkened_flux` needs no linear branch: the Green's-basis coefficients are exact at `u2 = 0`.

**A band whose limb darkening nothing reads pins it itself** (`Band.ld_consumers` / `_pin_unread_limb_darkening`, logged at INFO once per band). The complete consumer set is `transit` (unconditional -- any transit referencing the band), `mulensinstrument` (only when `lens.finite_source` is on; it reads `u1`, and `u2` too where the backend can carry a quadratic profile -- see `components/mulensing/mulensing.md`, "The limb-darkening LAW reaches the magnification"), and an `rvinstrument` `rm:` block (its `rm_band`, or band 0 when unset -- `rm.resolve_rm_indices`); `astrometryinstrument`'s `band:` is **not** a consumer, it takes only the filter identity for the SED fluxfrac. The predicate is per band instance (one system can hold a transit band and a mulens-only band) and reads each consumer's raw `config`, not its parsed band map -- `MulensInstrument.band_map` is built in stage 3, so whether it exists yet depends on component ordering. The pin is `sigma = 0` on the **sampled** coordinates (q1/q2, or u1 under `ld_law: linear`) through the manifest `"overrides"` channel, which layers **under** the params file, so an explicit user entry still frees it. This is what makes declaring filter identity free: a `band:` block on a point-source microlensing fit used to add two RVs no likelihood term read (`examples/ob08092`: -5.23 nats at the start; now bit-for-bit identical to having no band block), and flipping `finite_source` is again a one-line edit rather than an edit plus remembering to add or remove two pins. Tests: `tests/test_band_autopin_ld.py`.

**This component reads only the stars it predicts a flux for, on its own word** (`SED.photometric_readers`, the one readership predicate; `seen_star_mask` and `data_free_inventory` are derived from it, and `Star.structure_consumers` / `Star.register_parameters` read them; the `teffsed`/`fbolsed` floor potentials are masked to the same stars). A star is read by: an SED filter row (`blend_matrix` column non-zero); a microlensing light curve that TIES its source flux -- a prediction in its band AND a user-stated zeropoint `mu` and `sigma` (`MulensInstrument.sed_tie_mask`; with no stated tie the zeropoint is only reported, so until review 2.9.11 every source counted as read whether anything read it or not); every star under `sed_constrains_blend: true` on a light curve with a prediction (the blend tie compares the non-sources' predicted flux through the source's zeropoint); the primary source and the `psf_stars` of an unresolved background tied to a light curve (`Background.sed_read_stars`, `components/background/background.md`); and -- conservatively, all stars -- a transit dilution or an absolute-astrometry `band:`. It reads raw configs where the parsed maps may not exist at stage 3, the same ruling as `Band.ld_consumers`. An unread star's `STAR_KNOBS` (`av`/`teffsed`/`radiussed`) are pinned by `Star` (and made inactive, with what derives from them, unless a params entry names one -- `star.md`, "Photometrically unseen stars"), and the BC grid's `loggsed` barrier is lifted on it (`Star._unread_loggsed_bounds`: the grid bound is a 2-part override on every star, and on an unread M-dwarf lens it pushed the lens MASS against a grid nothing evaluates for it -- logg passed NextGen's then 5.0 ceiling below ~0.2 Msun; the tables reach 5.5 since 2026-10-20, padded above 5000 K -- `models/NextGen/README.md`, "Padding"); its `radius`/`teff`/`feh` take the structural inactive tier unless a relation or a user prior reads them. Why it matters (the DC2018 lens-mass pull) is in `components/star/star.md`, "Photometrically unseen stars". Tests: `tests/test_unseen_star.py`.

**The data-free inventory, and the errscale pin** (review 2.9.11). `SED.data_free_inventory(system)` answers, from the topology and not from a hand-kept list of configurations, which SED knobs no data reads: `sed.errscale` when there is no photometric row, and the `STAR_KNOBS` of every unread star (`DataFreeInventory.data_free`). The same object counts `n_free`, the free SED parameters the rows must determine -- errscale plus, per star a row names, the `PHOTOMETRIC_DOF` coordinates the user has not pinned (`teffsed`, `feh`, `av`: the grid's three sampled axes; `radiussed`: one flux scale, distance being degenerate with it) -- and **errscale is pinned at 1.0 whenever there are fewer usable rows than free SED parameters** (JDE 2026-09-10: "if we only have two (or 1?) band and we're fitting an errscale, we don't have enough dof ... pin errscale to 1"). The pin is `{sigma: 0, initval: 1}` on the manifest `overrides` channel, so a params entry still wins; startup logs it with the two counts (INFO with no rows, WARNING with some), and the draft says so when there is photometry. With `filters: []` -- the DC2018 sweep shape -- errscale used to sample its U(0.001, 1000) prior with nothing reading it, which is what the "500x errscale inflation" once read off those fits was. A count with a prior still counts: the rule is "rows < free parameters", not an information count. Shipped SEDs with photometry are untouched -- start logp bit-identical on all eleven configs (hat3 x4, kelt4 x2, kelt17 x3, gj1214, wasp18): one star needs 5 rows and has 9-12; kelt4's three-star SED has 17 rows against 1 + 4 + 2 + 2 = 9, B's and C's av/feh being pinned in its params file. The row-less SEDs moved: `examples/KMT-2019-BLG-1806` (all three configs) +7.8287 nats, exactly errscale's logit-uniform prior (-6.9098) and its raw N(0,1) term (-0.9189) leaving, plus the unread lens's loggsed barrier (a 1e-304 term); `examples/KMT-2021-BLG-1122L` likewise loses `sed.errscale_raw`. Tests: `tests/test_sed_data_free.py`.

**When no grid can host a star, the SED link is SEVERED -- unless the SED's own photometry reads it** (review 2.9.15, the fallback leg of 2.9.13). `teffsed`, `feh` and `av` are sampled through a logit onto the grid box, so a start outside it has no raw coordinate and `Parameter.build_pymc` refuses it. JDE's ruling on 2.9.13 stands -- "raising is the right move when we try to include the SED but we have no grid for it" -- so a star an SED row (or a transit dilution, or an astrometric fluxfrac) reads still raises. A star read ONLY through a cross-component tie (the microlensing zeropoint and blend ties) is the DC2018 case: its start comes from the source-flux seeding, which puts a faint source on the cool end of the Mamajek dwarf locus (down to 2350 K at 0.078 Msun) under NextGen's 2600 K floor. There `SED.revise_after_starts` -- the `Component.revise_after_starts` hook, run after stage 4 because the start exists only then -- adds the star to `severed_stars`; `can_predict` then refuses it, so `MulensInstrument.sed_prediction_mask` drops the source's zeropoint tie on every light curve (its element inactive, the user's prior unused and reported as unmatched) and a severed non-source drops the blend tie. What that leaves unread is exactly the inventory's answer, so `System.prepare`'s second stage-3/4 pass pins those stars' `STAR_KNOBS` and lifts their loggsed barrier, and their radius/teff/feh fall to the inactive tier unless a relation, a track or a user prior reads them; the light curve's own constraints (rho, and through `star_constrains_rho` the source radius) are untouched. It is loud and in the record: a WARNING at prepare, a caption line in the startup table, a solve diagnostic (`config_manager.diagnostics`, which the GUI reads), the pinned knobs exported as fixed, and a sentence in the draft -- a sweep where some events severed is not one population unless it says so. The long-term answer is a grid that reaches the star (2.9.14). Tests: `tests/test_sed_no_grid_fallback.py`.

### Limb darkening is per (star, band), and the pairing is now explicit

Physically the coefficients belong to a (star, band) pair; in the code they live on the band **instance** alone, so two hosts sharing one band instance silently shared their limb darkening. The LOCKED design (2026-08-04) does **not** move to map-indexed LD vectors (`band.u1[ld_map]`) -- named blocks referencing a filter string are already legal, `band: [{name: I_A, filter: I}, {name: I_B, filter: I}]`, so a second instance is the spelling for a second star. What was missing was anything checking that the instance and its readers agree, and that is what `Band._resolve_ld_stars` (stage 3) now does.

- **One predicate answers both questions.** `Band.ld_consumers(system)` returns an `LDConsumer(label, band, star)` per registration; `_ld_consumer_indices` is `{c.band for c in ...}`. The two questions -- *which* bands are read (the autopin) and *whose* limb darkening each read is -- were on their way to being answered by two lists, which is how a new consumer gets remembered in one place and forgotten in the other. Adding a consumer means adding it to `ld_consumers`, once.
- **`star_ndx:` on the band block stays the single source of truth** (it is what `Transit._add_sed_deblend_prior` reads for the seddeblend host, its only consumer). What changed is that it is **validated** against the registrations when declared, and **derived** from them when not -- so the historical default of 0 no longer silently stands in for a source star that is really star 1.
- What each consumer registers: **transit** -> the host stars of the planets it models (`planet.star_map`, derived from the orbit groups); **mulensinstrument** -> the SOURCE star, `lens.source_map[0]`, and only under `finite_source` (a point source takes no limb darkening at all); **rvinstrument `rm:`** -> the primary star of the RM orbit.
- **A contradiction raises; an intrinsic ambiguity warns.** A consumer needing a star the band does not carry, or two consumers of one band needing different stars, raises and points at a second band block with the same filter -- advice the user can act on. A single transit light curve covering planets of several hosts only *warns*: one light curve models every planet, so its limb darkening is ambiguous no matter how many band blocks exist, and refusing would gate a configuration with no legal spelling. Set `star_ndx:` there to say which host is meant.
- Model-inert on the shipped set: start logp and the free-RV set are bit-identical on all five examples that exercise it, and the only movement anywhere is `band.star_indices` going 0 -> the source star on the three finite-source microlensing fits (`KMT-2019-BLG-1806` and `ogle0383` to 1 `'Source'`, `ob161003` to 2 `'SourceA'`), none of which has a transit to read it. That correction is the point: those bands' coefficients describe the source's atmosphere, and the value they carried named the lens.

It is what the Claret limb-darkening tie (below) predicts a band's coefficients from: whose (teff, logg, feh) they are. The resolution is a PURE query, `Band._ld_star_resolution`, because `Star.structure_consumers` asks it too and may run before Band's own stage 3; `_resolve_ld_stars` only logs its warnings and stores the answer. Every `LDConsumer` carries a `kind` (`Band.LD_CONSUMER_KINDS`), since which consumer reads a band now matters. Tests: `tests/test_band_ld_star.py`.

### The Claret limb-darkening tie (`band.star_constrains_ld`)

EXOFASTv2's Claret prior, in EXOZIPPy's terms (#452, Samuel Yee). This is what the code does and why each piece must stay as it is.

- **On by default, per band; `false` turns it off** (JDE ruling 2026-10-15, following EXOFASTv2, which applies the prior unless `/NOCLARET`). The key is a YAML boolean or absent (`bool("false")` is True, so anything else raises); absent and explicit `true` differ only where the tie CANNOT apply -- see "What raises" below: an unset band is then skipped with a log line saying why (WARNING for a light curve over several hosts with no `star_ndx:`; INFO for a band nothing that the tie serves reads), while an explicit `true` raises. A band that something reads but whose filter has NO TABLE raises either way (next bullets). The default reaches into the star: a tied star's Teff, [Fe/H] and radius become READ (`Star.structure_consumers`), so in a transit fit with no SED, no evolutionary model and no Teff prior the star's Teff is now a free parameter held only by its default and the tie (the warning below fires), and the star is held inside the tables' box, so a star that STARTS outside it (an M dwarf below 3500 K) raises at stage 3 until its bands say `star_constrains_ld: false`. That raise is the "model used outside its domain" exception to warn-don't-gate: the tables cannot describe that star. `exofast2exozippy` writes the choice out either way: `true` for a fit without `/noclaret`, `false` for `/noclaret` or (with the reason) a host that starts outside the tables.
- **The `mulensinstrument.zeropoint` pattern.** An opted-in band gets the DERIVED offsets `band.du1 = u1 - u1_Claret` and, on a quadratic band, `band.du2`, INACTIVE on untied bands. `u1_claret`/`u2_claret` are context nodes `Band.add_parameter` injects (`context_dep_names`, `aligned_context_deps`): the table at the band's star's `star.teff/logg/feh[star_map]`, interpolated with `bc_grid.RegularGridInterpolator` (`components/band/claret.py`), built once per model. The tie IS the derived parameter's own defaults.yaml Gaussian, `mu: 0, sigma: 0.05` -- `Parameter.build_pymc` applies it to every derived non-reported element with a finite sigma, unnormalized, so it is exactly EXOFASTv2's chi2 term -- which makes the width an ordinary params field (`band.TESS.du1: {sigma: 0.1}`, or `band.du1:` for all) and gives the table rows, restart files and GUI for free. **Do not "fix" the default sigma to match zeropoint's no-default ruling** (2.2.21): a zeropoint's center and width are facts about the user's calibration; this center is computed and 0.05 is a statement about the tables' accuracy, like the Torres scatter. No bounds on du: a derived element with bounds gets a barrier, and the physical region is already the support of the sampled q1/q2 (or u1).
- **No Jacobian is owed.** The Kipping map has |det| = 1, so uniform (q1, q2) is uniform (u1, u2) over the triangle and the Gaussian on (u1, u2) is EXOFASTv2's truncated Gaussian exactly (`test_the_kipping_map_has_unit_jacobian`). Some table centers lie outside the triangle (infrared quadratic, blue linear nodes); the prior is then truncated at the boundary, as in EXOFASTv2.
- **Once per band, not once per file** (ruling 2026-10-12, confirmed 2026-10-15). 0.05 is a model systematic floor -- how far the tables can be from the true limb darkening -- and more data cannot shrink it. EXOFASTv2 adds the term inside its loop over transit FILES, so a band with N light curves gets it N times (effective sigma 0.05/sqrt(N)); that is an EXOFASTv2 bug (jdeast/EXOFASTv2#9), deliberately not reproduced, and its limb-darkening posteriors on multi-file bands are correspondingly too narrow. Do not offer the sqrt(N) width as a "parity" option; the translator notes the difference without a recipe.
- **The tables are trimmed, never filled, and the star is held inside them.** Claret's tables have holes (about half of every ATLAS cube); the published copies are trimmed to the box complete in all 47: Teff 3500-10000 K, logg 2.0-5.0, [Fe/H] -2.0 to +1.0 -- the BC grid's and the MIST grid's ruling. Below [Fe/H] = -2.0 every row has missing dwarf cells, and below logg 2.0 the complete part shrinks to Teff <= 8000 K. `Band._bound_tied_stars` puts that box on each tied star through `ConfigManager.add_override` at INDEX paths (`star.0.teff`), so it does not overwrite the SED's broadcast `star.feh` entry and the two combine as max/min: hard bounds on the sampled teff and feh (EXOFASTv2's out-of-table rejection, with a gradient), a soft barrier on the derived logg, declared to the reports by `_declare_claret_tie` (`supersedes_bounds`, the `loggsed` precedent). `grid_bound_paths()` publishes them under each tied star's INDEX key (`star.0.feh`), not the label: a wall on a star no tied band reads is not the tables running out. `diagnostics.check_near_bounds` reads a label claim (the SED's `star.feh`) and an element claim together, and `grid_bounded_paths` merges two components' claims on one key instead of keeping the later one.
- **What raises, at stage 3, on an explicit `true` -- and is skipped with a log line when the key is unset** (`Band._claret_tie_plan`, a pure query so `Star.structure_consumers` and Band cannot disagree about which bands are tied): a band nothing reads the limb darkening of; a light curve over several hosts with no `star_ndx:` (it would predict one host's coefficients from another's Teff). **What raises either way:** a band something reads whose filter has no table under its `ld_source` -- a filter with no Claret table (`claret_band_lookup` is strict, unlike `resolve_filter_name`, which hands back an unknown label), a linear band with no linear table (TESS), a PHOENIX table with no column for the filter. It used to be skipped with a warning when unset, which left a modeling choice nobody made (JDE 2026-11-06: "it should not be silently skipped"); the error names the three ways out (`Band._no_table_remedies`): `ld_source: phoenix`, `claret_filter:` naming the nearest Claret band by effective wavelength (`claret.nearest_claret_band`, from the hard-coded `CLARET_LAMBDA_EFF` and the filter's SVO profile), or `star_constrains_ld: false`. **`claret_filter:`** reads another filter's Claret table for the band -- only the table: the band's `filter:` stays its real bandpass for the SED and the zeropoint tie, which is why it is a key of its own rather than a relabeled `filter:`. It warns at stage 3 and the modeling draft states the substitution; with `ld_source: phoenix`, or naming no table, it raises. Also raising either way: a star whose probed teff or feh START lies outside the box (it needs the probed start, so it is judged in `_check_and_seed_claret_starts`, not the plan). A logg start outside it only warns (soft barrier). **It warns** when nothing but the tie reads the star's Teff: then the prior's center follows a temperature only the light curve constrains.
- **Several filters can read one table, and Johnson U B V read Claret's U B V.** The alias table's Claret column is a many-to-one map (Bessell and Johnson V both read `V`; 2MASS Ks reads `K`). Claret & Bloemen's U B V are the Johnson-Cousins system, so `Generic/Johnson.U/B/V` read them; Johnson R, I and J stay `Unsupported`, because Claret's R and I are Cousins and its J is the 2MASS-like one, different bands rather than near neighbors. A filter with no Claret table (the Roman WFI set, Gaia, MOA-Red) gets no alias to a nearby table: the alias table states identity, and an approximation is the user's to state, through `claret_filter:` (above). `ld_source: phoenix` reads tables built through the filter's own profile, the better answer where it exists. A bare `U`/`B`/`V` still means Bessell, now through `AMBIGUOUS_FILTER_ALIASES` rather than row order.
- **The tables come from Zenodo** (record `claret_ld_tables` in `utilities/zenodo_assets.py`, concept 23384501; cite as `Yee:2026claret`). `models/Claret/claret_tables.py` fetches each table a fit reads on first use, the NextGen BC tables' pattern (`exozippy-fetch-claret-tables` pre-fetches all 47), and a present table that differs from its pinned md5 RAISES rather than being overwritten or used. `generate_claret_tables.py` rebuilds them from VizieR -- Claret's corrected release (17 Jun 2011 for J/A+A/529/A75), never EXOFASTv2's copies -- into `models/Claret/build/`, never the fetch directory, and compares every cell with an EXOFASTv2 checkout's `quadld/` save files. 44 of the 47 agree exactly. The other three quadratic tables (Sloan z' a and b swapped; Stromgren b and v copies of Johnson B and V; jdeast/EXOFASTv2#10) differ because EXOFASTv2's tables were built from the original April 2011 release and still carry its errors; the published tables use the corrected values, so on those three bands they differ from EXOFASTv2 by design. The generator's `EXOFAST_DEFECTS` pins the three differences so the comparison stays exact.
- **The tie is a property of the BAND, never of what reads it** (JDE ruling 2026-11-05). A band's limb darkening is one (filter, star) pair however it is consumed, so a transit, an RM block, a Doppler-tomography dataset and a finite-source microlensing light curve are tied identically, to the star `_ld_star_resolution` names (a microlensing band's is the primary SOURCE). The plan's only consumer question is whether ANYTHING reads the band. `LDConsumer` deliberately carries no consumer kind, so no rule about a band's limb darkening can be keyed on one. Finite-source microlensing was excluded until then (#452: "a source's Teff/logg are usually weakly constrained") -- but that is exactly the case the unconstrained-Teff warning below already reports, not a reason to drop the tables' information: a source with an SED, a color-magnitude-diagram Teff prior or an evolutionary model is constrained, and one without still gets the tables' (u1, u2) correlation with its logg and [Fe/H].
- **Readership and seeding.** `Star.structure_consumers` marks teff, feh and radius (logg = f(logmass, radius)) of each tied star as read, through `Band.claret_tie_stars` and the label `Band.CLARET_CONSUMER_LABEL` (declared once, on Band). The tie reads the MASS too (that logg), so a tied star is a mass reader (`star._mass_reader_star_indices`): a microlensing-only source's tier-2 `logmass` pin does not apply to it. Pinned, the tables' logg barrier would press on the radius -- on rho -- against an arbitrary fixed mass. The pin lifted, `examples/ob161045`'s giant source still STARTS at logg 1.68 (13.4 Rsun at its 0.316 Msun default mass), below the tables' 2.0 floor, and pays the soft barrier at the start (the warning above); the sampler can now raise the mass to leave it. Each tied band's sampled coordinates are seeded by `add_hint` at the table value for the star's `probe_start`, only when all three inputs are informed, so a user initval still wins; untied bands are never seeded (that would move every shipped start). A table center outside the physical region is projected inside [0.001, 0.999] for the start only, with a log line.

Tests: `tests/test_claret_ld_tie.py` (synthetic tables, `conftest.write_synthetic_claret_tables`), `tests/test_exofast2exozippy.py` (the translation). Examples: `examples/hat3/hat3_mist_claret.yaml`, `examples/toi3919/toi3919_claret.yaml`.

Everything that turns those (u1, u2) into an occultation flux goes through the one shared helper `components/limbdark.py:quad_limb_darkened_flux(b, r, u1, u2)` -- `transit.py` (likelihood and plotting) and `rm.py` (the Rossiter-McLaughlin blocked flux). Never open-code the dot product: exoplanet-core's `quad_solution_vector` returns `s` in starry's Green's basis, so the coefficients are `c0 = 1 - u1 - 1.5*u2`, `c1 = u1 + 2*u2`, `c2 = -0.25*u2` (Agol, Luger & Foreman-Mackey 2020), **not** the mu-power `[1 - u1 - u2, u1, u2]`. The naive basis is exact at `u2 = 0` and silently wrong otherwise -- rm.py carried it undetected (up to ~87% of the transit depth at u2 = 0.3, with flux > 1 near egress) precisely because every test that exercised it used u2 = 0. `transit/physics.py:calc_planet_visible` is the one deliberate exception: it occults the *planet* by the star, a uniform disk where `s0/pi` is the whole answer. Tests: `tests/test_limbdark.py` pins the helper against brute-force disk integration.

The SED component supports n stars: each `.sed` filter row's `photType: {pos: [...], neg: [...]}` (with `blend:` as an alias for `pos`; entries are star names or indices) builds two 0/1 star matrices (`pos_matrix`, `neg_matrix`); blended rows are flux sums, `neg` makes the row a differential magnitude (`-2.5*log10(F_pos/F_neg)`), and `neg_filter:` makes it a color between two filters ("Color rows", below). An empty `filters:` list is legal — the SED then only serves cross-component flux predictions (`predict_star_appmag`, `predict_blend_appmag`, `predict_flux_fraction`). BC tables for missing filters auto-generate from the model spectra (`components/sed/make_bc.py`, CLI `scripts/make_bc_tables.py`) -- but see **auto-generation is a development convenience, not the production path** below. The shipped tables are built from the full-resolution spectra by `models/NextGen/generate_NextGen_BC_Tables.py`; `models/NextGen/README.md` has the workflow and the table format.

### SED flux sources: something the SED evaluates like a star (`Component.sed_flux_sources`)

A component may give the SED a FLUX SOURCE that is not a star -- the red clump of a microlensing sight line (`components/redclump/redclump.md`) -- through the generic hook `sed_flux_sources(system)`: `SEDFluxSource(name, paths, photometric_dof)`, `paths` mapping each of `SED.SOURCE_INPUTS` (teffsed, loggsed, feh, av, radiussed, distance; plus rv_dust under `fit_rv_dust`) to a parameter path `"comp.<i>.param"`. The SED knows the provider by nothing else, exactly as it knows its band readers (`sed_flux_bands`), and a provider writes no SED code: "a flux source looks like a star to the BC path" is the whole contract. A path map rather than a tensor-returning hook so the SED owns every evaluation of the source: the graph, the numpy start helpers (`bc_np`, `appmag_np`), the figure's inputs and the grid bounds.

- **The source axis** of `pos_matrix` / `neg_matrix` and `_predicted_appmag_node` is the stars (indices 0..nstars-1, unchanged) followed by the providers, `source_names`. A photType NAME resolves against the stars first, then the providers; an INDEX only ever means a star (one spelling, and no index ambiguity); a provider named like a star, or two providers with one name, RAISES at stage 1 naming the owners. A missing photType is still the blend of the stars only.
- **ONE bolometric-correction evaluation** on the stacked coordinates (`_source_vectors` concatenates each input's provider nodes after the stars'; `bc_node` is the one table/spectral evaluation), so the cost stays flat in rows and every `predict_*` consumer that indexes [star, column] is untouched. With no provider the concatenation is skipped and the graph is exactly the stars-only one (start logp bit-identical). A provider Parameter is materialized lazily for THIS build (`source_input_node`, the `_ensure_star_nodes` rule).
- **Readership stays a STAR question**: rows' provider columns are reported by `provider_readers` (name -> labels), so an `SEDReader` is a star index and `seen_star_mask`, `Star.structure_consumers` and the unseen-star pins are unchanged. A provider a row reads adds its `photometric_dof` to the errscale count.
- **Grid support**: `register_parameters` (stage 3) puts the grid's extent on each provider's teffsed/feh/av/loggsed paths through `add_override` and publishes them in `grid_bound_paths`, so a provider start outside the grid has no raw coordinate and the build refuses it (a provider is never severed). `load_data` slices the grid over the providers' bounds too.
- **Directly-read filters**: `sed_flux_filters(system)` (filter labels, default empty) are appended to the grid's columns after the band filters (`provider_filters`), and the grid box is re-peeked over them so a source is never bounded onto nodes one of its columns lacks. `bc_node(teff, logg, feh, av, rv)` is the public graph evaluation such a reader uses (the clump's (V-I)_0 and its band extinctions) -- the SAME interpolator or spectral node, never a second grid.
- **The figure** draws each source's spectrum from `_compiled_source_inputs` (the forward model's own nodes, never the draw dict by hand); the blended "Total" is over the stars only; a provider's legend label is its bare name.

Tests: `tests/test_redclump.py` (a test-double twin of a star predicts it bit for bit; the name and index rules).

### Differential color rows: four fluxes in two filters (`color_filter:`)

A row with `color_filter: b` is the difference of two same-filter differentials (review 8.9.11):

    m_row = [m_pos(a) - m_neg(a)] - [m_pos(b) - m_neg(b)],   a = the row's `name`

the Delta(V-I) = (V-I)_S - (V-I)_RC of a microlensing source from the red clump -- four fluxes in two filters, which a color row (one star set per side, -2.5 log10 F_pos(a)/F_neg(b)) cannot spell: pos=Source(V), neg=Clump(I) is V_S - I_RC. Writing it as two same-filter rows instead throws away the precision it is measured with (Delta V would inherit Delta I's error).

```yaml
  - name: "Generic/Johnson.V"   # filter a
    mag: -0.30                   # Delta(V-I), source minus clump
    err: 0.01
    color_filter: "Generic/Cousins.I"   # filter b
    photType: {pos: [Source], neg: [RedClump]}
```

pos and neg are both required, and may not share a star (the same-filter rule); `color_filter` and `neg_filter` together raise (a row is one kind), and so does `neg_magsys`; naming the row's own filter (in any alias) raises -- that is identically zero. Every magnitude-system offset cancels exactly (each filter is on both sides), so none is applied, but the second filter's record is still resolved against its column (`_convert_to_bc_system`). The second filter is a grid column of its own, appended after the color rows' (`color_col`), and `_combined_appmag_node` subtracts the second differential on those rows only, assembled with `pt.set_subtensor` on a static index set; a file with no such row builds the color-row graph (start logp bit-identical). The figure plots no photometry point for it (no flux at any one wavelength), only its residual between its two filters. Tests: `tests/test_sed_differential_color.py`.

### Color rows: a measured color between two filters (`neg_filter:`)

A row that names a second filter for its neg side is a COLOR: `-2.5 log10(F_pos(name) / F_neg(neg_filter))`, each side's stars summed in its own filter (review 8.9.10). It is the third kind of row beside the absolute row and the same-filter differential, and the one spelling for a cross-filter comparison:

```yaml
filters:
  # V - I of the source from the light-curve regression (blend- and
  # calibration-free): one number with its own error.
  - name: "Generic/Bessell.V"     # the pos side's filter
    mag: 1.832                    # m(V) - m(I), the measured color
    err: 0.012
    magsys: Vega                  # pos side's system (optional)
    neg_filter: "Generic/Cousins.I"
    neg_magsys: Vega              # neg side's system (optional)
    photType:
      pos: [Source]
      neg: [Source]
```

**When to use one.** For a color MEASURED as a color, whose error is smaller than the quadrature sum of the two magnitudes' errors: (a) a microlensing source color from the light-curve regression (the V/I flux ratio while the source is magnified, ~0.01 mag), which is blend-free and calibration-free -- two absolute rows would each carry their band's independent zeropoint error, and the color would inherit both in quadrature; (b) a calibrated clump color (V-I)_RC naming the red clump on both sides (a source-minus-clump Delta(V-I) is four fluxes: a differential color row, above); (c) a color from one image pair. Two absolute rows with independent errors throw that precision away.

**Do not add a color beside both of its magnitudes when they are catalog magnitudes with independent errors.** The color is then the same information counted twice, and the fit is overconfident in exactly that color. A color row plus one of the magnitudes is the right shape when the color was measured more precisely than the second magnitude; a covariance between rows is the general answer (review 8.9.9) and is not built yet.

The rules, each enforced at load with an error naming the file and row:

- **Each side is in its own filter and its own magnitude system.** `magsys:` is the pos side's, `neg_magsys:` the neg side's; unstated means that filter's native system, as for any row. A color reported as m_a - m_b moves onto the BC columns' Vega system by the pos side's m_AB - m_Vega MINUS the neg side's (each only where that side is AB): an AB g minus Vega J is fitted at `color - [m_AB - m_Vega](g)`, a Vega J minus AB g at `color + [m_AB - m_Vega](g)`, so a color reported as the difference of two reported magnitudes is fitted as the difference of the two fitted ones (`_convert_to_bc_system`, the same per-column record and drift check as an absolute row). A same-filter differential row is unchanged: its system cancels, and `neg_magsys:` on it raises.
- **pos and neg may name the same star** -- the color of one star is the common case. On a same-filter row that would be identically zero and still raises.
- **`neg_filter:` needs a `neg` star list** (a row with no photType is the blend of all stars on the pos side only, so it raises too), must be a filter name, and must be a DIFFERENT BC column from the row's own: `neg_filter` naming the same filter under another alias is a same-filter differential row, whose one spelling has no `neg_filter`.
- **A row takes only the keys `name`, `mag`, `err`, `magsys`, `photType`, `neg_filter`, `neg_magsys`, `color_filter`** (`SED.ROW_KEYS`); any other key raises, because a misspelled `neg_filter` would otherwise turn a color into a same-filter differential in silence.

**How it is built.** The second filter is a grid column of its own, appended after the rows' own filters (`row_filters`; `neg_col` is each row's neg-side column, its own on every other row), so the spectral (`fit_rv_dust`) path, the magnitude-system record and the `predict_*` API see it like any column. `_combined_appmag_node` sums the neg stars' flux in `neg_col`; a file with no color row builds the same-filter graph it always did, so every shipped SED start logp is bit-identical. A star named on either side of any row is read (`photometric_readers`). In the figure a color row is plotted like a differential row -- each side's point at its OWN filter's wavelength, through its own zero point, anchored on the model's flux for the other side in the OTHER filter -- and its residual sits between the two filters (the geometric mean of their effective wavelengths); the data-only GUI preview, which plots magnitudes against wavelength, leaves color rows out. The AB-conversion sentence in the draft names each AB side's filter. Tests: `tests/test_sed_color_rows.py`.

**Every photometry row has a magnitude system, and an AB row is fitted on Vega** (review 1.9.1, 2026-10). Every NextGen BC column is Vega-referenced (its table's `mag_system`; SVO's Vega F_lambda zeropoint is what the BC divides by), while SDSS, Pan-STARRS, GALEX, Euclid and Roman publish in AB. Until this was fixed `magsys` was parsed into `self.magsys` and nothing read it, so an AB row was fitted as Vega -- wrong by m_AB - m_Vega in its band (SDSS u +0.90, PS1 z +0.51, GALEX FUV +2.09 mag) against errors of a few hundredths, and PR #349's new SLOAN/PAN-STARRS/GALEX tables made that silent.

- **One internal spelling** (`components/sed/magsys.py`): `"Vega"` and `"AB"` (`VEGA`, `AB`). `parse_magsys` accepts only those exact spellings at the boundary -- CASE-SENSITIVE (JDE 2026-10-01: "We want to require case matching. g is different than G."), so `ab` or `VEGA` RAISES with a "did you mean 'AB'?" hint (suggesting, never accepting) -- and an unknown one (ST included -- nothing records an m_ST - m_Vega) raises naming the file, row and filter. Past `_process_SED_yaml` everything compares the two strings exactly.
- **A row that states no system is in its filter's NATIVE system** (JDE ruling 2026-10-01), not "always Vega". The native system comes from the same per-column record as the offset (below), which declares one for every shipped column; a filter with none recorded (`""`) RAISES when a row in it states none. That is Gemini Zorro (contrast photometry, where the system cancels) and, UNRESOLVED, Kepler Kp (JDE 2026-10-01: "I'm not sure about the Roman/Kepler system. Raise until we figure it out." -- KIC Kp is built from SDSS-like g r i, while MIST carries it as Vega). A row in those filters that states its system works as usual; mkticsed writes no Kepler rows, and would refuse one (`native_system` raises). Roman WFI was unresolved too until an email from Alison Duck (2026-10-01) settled it: Roman photometry will be in AB magnitudes, so every WFI column is recorded AB-native and an unstated Roman row is fitted as AB. (MIST's `filter_magsys.txt` lists its WFIRST_* columns as Vega, under different column names, so the generator's MIST cross-check does not compare them; the email is the authority.) No shipped .sed file states a system or has an AB-native row, so no shipped example changed meaning.
- **mkticsed writes `magsys:` explicitly on EVERY row, Vega included** (JDE ruling 2026-10-01: "Without it, someone might wrongly think we didn't think about it and change it"), live and commented-out rows alike. The system it writes is read from that same record (`magsys.native_system`) -- one source, so the file and the fitter cannot disagree: APASS g'r'i' (UCAC4) and GALEX rows are AB, everything else Vega. It used to tag every row Vega and suppress the tag when Vega, i.e. it wrote AB catalog magnitudes AS Vega.
- **The offset is the generator's, never MIST's.** `filters/filter_magsys.txt`'s `mag(Vega/AB)` column comes from MIST's Vega spectrum and filter curves; our BCs come from SVO's Vega zeropoints and the detector-type weighting. `models/NextGen/generate_NextGen_BC_Tables.py:magsys_table` computes m_AB - m_Vega per column inside `BolometricCorrection`'s own filter-set data (`filter_ab_minus_vega`): the AB reference spectrum (3631 Jy, Oke & Gunn 1983) through the same processed profile and weighting, over the same zeropoint flux the BC uses -- one integral per filter, no spectra needed. `write_magsys_table` writes `NextGen.magsys.csv` beside `NextGen.grid.yaml` (column, svo_id, native_system, zeropoint_Fl_Vega, flux_weighting, ab_minus_vega), and `generate_bc_tables` rewrites it whenever it writes tables. Against MIST the two differ by up to 0.031 mag (GALEX FUV), 0.029 (SDSS u), 0.025 (GALEX NUV) and <= 0.011 elsewhere; against SVO's own AB zeropoints by < 0.004. SVO's VOTable `MagSys` field is not a usable native-system source: it reads "Vega" for SDSS, PS1 and GALEX alike.
- **The record cannot drift from its column.** `SED._convert_to_bc_system` checks each row's record against the column's `filter_meta` (svo_id, flux weighting, Vega zeropoint) and the table's `mag_system`, and raises naming the column on any mismatch: a column regenerated with a new zeropoint cannot keep a stale offset.
- **Applied at ONE place.** `_convert_to_bc_system` (load_data, right after the grid names each row's column) sets `self.mag = self.mag_reported - self.ab_minus_vega`; the likelihood, `plot()`, `plot_data()` and the residual panel all read `self.mag`, so the figure shows what is fitted, and the caption says the plotted system is Vega. Only the centre moves; the error does not. A differential row (photType `neg`) is not shifted -- -2.5 log10(F_pos/F_neg) in one band is the same number on either system -- but its system is still resolved. `plot.py`'s `filtersys_df` (filter_magsys.txt loaded, zero readers) is gone. A converted row declares a `data` prose sentence citing Oke & Gunn (1983); an all-Vega SED declares nothing, so its draft is unchanged.
- **What does NOT read it:** transit dilution and the astrometric fluxfrac are flux RATIOS of one column (`predict_flux_fraction`, `predict_star_appmag` ratios), where any offset cancels, and limb darkening never reads an SED magnitude. The mulensing `zeropoint` IS absolute (`predict_blend_appmag` returns Vega), and since issue #313 it reads this record too: each light curve has a `magsys:` (unstated = the band filter's native system), resolved by `SED.filter_magsys` -- the public face of `_column_magsys`, the same per-column resolution and drift check `_convert_to_bc_system` runs for a row -- and an AB light curve's prediction gains the record's `ab_minus_vega` before it meets the zeropoint (the DC2018 challenge's magnitudes are AB: +0.499 in F087, +1.037 in F146, which this record reproduces). The consumer side is `components/mulensing/mulensing.md`.
- Start logp is bit-identical on every shipped SED example (all-Vega; `x - 0.0 == x`). Tests: `tests/test_sed_magsys.py`, `tests/test_mkticsed.py`.

**BC tables grow incrementally, and a fit reads only its own slice of them.** A NaN cell in a `{FACILITY}.bc.parquet` table means "not computed yet". `bc_grid.write_bc_table` merges cell by cell, so it never overwrites a value with a NaN, and it adds new grid nodes only when called with `allow_new_nodes=True`. `bc_grid.bc_nodes_to_compute` is the generators' shared work list, so extending the grid yaml along any axis, or adding filters to a facility, computes only the missing cells (`models/NextGen/README.md`). A table can therefore be ragged: 2MASS extended to A_V = 15 while GAIA is still at 6. Readers only ever use the **complete** part of the columns they ask for (`bc_grid.complete_axes`). That is why `_inject_grid_bounds` passes the fit's own filters (`_requested_filter_labels`: the `.sed` rows plus the band filters) to `peek_grid_axes`. Without them, a 2MASS-only fit could not reach the extended A_V values, and a fit that also uses Gaia would be bounded onto nodes where Gaia has no value. `load_data` passes the resolved `teffsed`/`feh`/`av` bounds to `build_bc_grid`. It reads only the requested filter columns and only the rows inside the bounds (plus the grid point bracketing each one), and replaces the old path of reading everything and then calling `slice_bc`. On the shipped tables the result is bit-identical to that path. If the bounds reach past what a requested filter covers, it raises rather than extrapolate. Tests: `tests/test_bc_incremental.py`, and Section 4 of `tests/test_nextgen_bc_pipeline.py`.

**The BC grid's support is enforced on all four of its axes, and the fourth one is the interesting case.** `SED._inject_grid_bounds` reads the grid's (teff, logg, feh, av) extents at `__init__` and puts them on star Parameters through `ConfigManager.add_override`. `teffsed`, `feh` and `av` are **sampled**, so two finite bounds put them on the logit transform and the grid extent is their exact support -- they cannot leave the grid at all. `loggsed` is **derived** (`calc_logg_from_logmass` on `logmass` and `radiussed`), so the same bound becomes the house soft barrier `Parameter.build_pymc` adds for a derived element, with the steepness **measured** at startup by `whitening.measure_barrier_scales` and pinnable per element with `bound_scale:`.

- **Every SED star parameter is a relaxation-engine symbol** (JDE 2026-10-14): `sed/symbolic_physics.py` maps `teffsed`, `radiussed`, `loggsed`, `luminositysed`, `fbolsed`, `fbol` and `av` (plus the `teff`/`radius`/`logmass`/`luminosity`/`distance` they derive from), with relations mirroring their defaults.yaml expressions. So a user may seed any of them, and a stage 1-3 solver reads any of them with `probe_start` -- `Transit._hint_dilute_from_sed` reads `loggsed` itself rather than re-deriving it.
- `star.loggsed` exists as a Parameter *for that reason*. It was an inline expression inside `SED._predicted_appmag_node` until 2026-08, so there was nothing for the override channel to bound -- and **bounding `logmass` and `radiussed` separately does not bound their combination**, which is what `loggsed` is. `_predicted_appmag_node` and `compile_plotters` now read `star.loggsed.value`; the quantity the barrier restrains and the quantity the interpolator is handed must be the same node.
- **The barrier is a restoring force, not a wall, because the interpolator never fails.** `bc_grid.RegularGridInterpolator` is built with `fill_value=None`, so it computes `out_of_bounds` and then **discards** it: an off-grid point is **linearly extrapolated off the edge cell** -- not NaN, not `-inf`. What was missing was never a rejection but a gradient pointing back, and a `-inf` wall would have been strictly worse (NUTS has nothing to follow). Off-grid logp and dlogp are finite in both the C and the JAX backends; verified by sampling from a deliberately off-grid start with `nuts_sampler="numpyro"`, which pulls the chain back onto the grid.
- **On-grid fits are untouched**: five of the seven shipped SED configs keep a bit-identical start logp and the two kelt4 ones move by 1-2 ulp (1.5e-11 out of 8e4) from graph reassociation.
- **`examples/gj1214` started OFF the grid, and shipped a deliberately softened barrier because of it** (removed in #508, once the star was inside the grid: start logp +2.2e-4). Its M dwarf sits at `loggsed = 5.0223`, against what was a NextGen logg ceiling of 5.0 until 2026-10-20 (the tables now reach 5.5, the plane above 5000 K padded from 5.0; `models/NextGen/README.md`), so that fit was scored on extrapolated bolometric corrections from the day it shipped until then. NextGen is the only BC grid whose tables ship, so there is no drop-in replacement today, and **a modestly extrapolated correction beats refusing to fit** as long as the caveat is explicit. Its params file therefore carried `star.A.loggsed: {bound_scale: 25.0}` -- transition width 0.25 dex, i.e. **half a NextGen logg cell**, slope 17.6 nats/dex -- which costs the start 0.91 nats instead of the measured barrier's 434, while still charging 4.41 nats half a cell past the edge and 8.80 a full cell past. The half-cell anchor is the point: linear extrapolation past the edge reuses the last cell's slope and its error grows roughly quadratically, so half a cell out is where it becomes comparable to the in-grid interpolation error and a full cell out is where it clearly exceeds it. Do not pick a scale that zeroes the penalty -- that is silent extrapolation again.
- **The off-grid notice keys on the VALUE, never on the barrier being active**, so softening cannot silence it; `SED._declare_grid_support` warns per star and `_barrier_advice` switches the second half of the message between "measured, so effectively clamped to the edge -- here is `bound_scale:`" and "DELIBERATELY WIDENED, admitted at N nats", quoting the penalty it actually computes from the potential's own formula. A warning and not a raise precisely because such fits have been running. **A softened barrier now survives a restart cycle** (review 2.3.13, 2026-09): `mkparam` had `bound_scale` in its constraint loop all along, but that loop only runs for the parameters it EMITS, and a derived parameter like `loggsed` is never one of them -- so the gj1214 entry was dropped as an initval-only stale guess and had to be re-added by hand. The fix is in `mkparam`'s end-of-function pass-through (`_CONSTRAINT_FIELDS`), which is a derived parameter's only path into a restart file; it copies the user's entry VERBATIM and never reads the trace, so `loggsed` gets its `bound_scale` back without a start value the model would silently override.
- The barrier **declares itself** to the reporting layer (`supersedes_bounds=True`), because `_own_prior_str` reports a derived parameter carrying two finite bounds as `U(lo, hi)` -- exactly the prior a barrier is not. See "Reporting component-added priors" in `src/exozippy/components/parameter.md`, including why it passes a non-default `support_phrase`.

Tests: `tests/test_sed_grid_barrier.py`.

**The SPECTRA grid behind the figure is a different grid from the BC grid, and it is not a full rectangle.** `models/NextGen/BCs/NextGen.spectra.csv` spans logg -0.5 to 6.0 where the BC tables stop at 5.5 (5.0 before 2026-10-20), and holds 12796 of its 23716 rectangle nodes. `NextGenPlot._interp_spectra` brackets a draw blindly with `_findNearestGridPoints`, so two things follow. Off the whole spectra grid it **extrapolates** off the bracketing cell (`interpn(..., bounds_error=False, fill_value=None)`) and warns per axis -- the same ruling as the BC interpolator, and necessary because scipy's default `bounds_error=True` raised inside `SED.plot`, which `run.py` calls **unguarded** before sampling. A bracketing node the grid does not contain **raises**: leaving its flux at the initialized 0.0 behind a `warnings.warn` (what this did until 2026-08) interpolates the published figure against a fabricated zero. Inside the BC grid's own box -- teff [2600, 10000], logg [0, 5], feh [-4, 0.5], where the three sampled axes are hard-bounded -- the shipped grid is complete, so neither fires; above logg 5.0, which `examples/gj1214`'s start already exceeds, 385 of the 660 (teff, feh) nodes on the logg = 5.5 plane are absent. Tests: `tests/test_sed_spectra_plot.py`.

**The `teffsed`/`fbolsed` floor potentials keep their `-log(sigma)`** (`SED._fractional_floor_logp`). Their sigma is a *fraction of the star's own* teff/fbol, i.e. a function of a sampled parameter, which is precisely the case where the house convention keeps the normalization -- `components/relations.py:_add_penalty`, where mann keeps it for a fractional sigma and torres drops it only because its scatter is a constant in dex. Dropped, the term leaves a `d/dx log(x)` tilt worth exactly one nat of free likelihood per e-fold, pushing teff and fbol up for no physical reason. The 2pi is dropped, matching `_add_penalty`. The term is unit-dependent and so mostly a large constant (`fbol ~ 5e-10` internally): it moved `examples/gj1214`'s start logp by +20.874 and `examples/kelt4`'s by +62.256. Tests: `tests/test_sed_floor_normalization.py`.

EXOFASTv2 has the same defect, so this is a BUG FIX rather than a divergence, and it should not be advertised as a parity break: EXOFASTv2's sigma here is a fraction too, and it drops the normalization anyway. Dropping additive constants is right when sigma is constant -- a term that does not vary cannot move a posterior -- and wrong when sigma is a fraction of a sampled quantity, which is the case in both codes. Nor is a note of that kind wanted here in any case: the whole SED -> bolometric-correction path already departs from EXOFASTv2, so flagging one term as THE parity break would imply the rest is closer than it is.

**`sed/symbolic_physics.py` is conditional on the `sed:` block.** It sets `comp_key = "star"` so its two defaulting relations (`teffsed = teff`, `radiussed = radius`) instantiate once per star -- but `Star.register_parameters` declares those parameters only inside its `if in_system("sed")` branch, so registering the relations regardless put two phantom leaf symbols per star, each with a defaults.yaml start and a ledger row, into every non-SED config. The mechanism is one optional module-level hook, `relations_apply(system_config)`, checked by `ConfigManager`'s `symbolic_physics.py` walk; absent, relations always apply (the right default for a file keyed on its own block). It is the symbolic-side analogue of the per-child `JITTER_RELATIONS` registration: which configs a relation applies to is a fact only the relation's own file knows. Tests: `tests/test_sed_relations_conditional.py`.

Filter profiles and stellar-model data are **package-level**, not SED-component-level, because the MIST evolutionary grids need them too: `src/exozippy/filters/` (profiles, `filternames.txt`, `filter_magsys.txt`) and `src/exozippy/models/` (`extinction_law.ascii`, plus one subdirectory per model family -- `NextGen/BCs/` (one `{FACILITY}.bc.parquet` table per facility), `MIST/MISTv1.2/BCs/`, `MIST/MISTv2.5/EEPs/`). `bc_grid.DEFAULT_FILTER_ROOT` / `DEFAULT_MODEL_ROOT` are the two anchors; the SED's `model_root:` config key overrides the latter. The gitignored NextGen spectra CSVs cache under `models/NextGen/BCs/` alongside the tables they generate.

**The NextGen BC tables are published on Zenodo, not shipped (JDE ruling 2026-10-01).** PR #349 took the fourteen `{FACILITY}.bc.parquet` tables to ~96 MB tracked under `models/NextGen/BCs/`, and the wheel to 134 MB -- over PyPI's 100 MB per-file default (rc2 was 31.5 MB). They are now git-ignored there and fetched on first use, exactly like the spectra and the MIST EEP grid, through `utilities/zenodo.fetch_assets` and the machine cache behind it. `models/NextGen/bc_tables.py` owns the fetch; the pins -- the record id of the published VERSION (23074951, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23074951; concept record 23074950 groups every version) and each table's size and md5 -- live with every other Zenodo pin in `utilities/zenodo_assets.py`, record `nextgen_bc_tables` (review 4.9.2). All three EXOZIPPy data records (these tables, the MISTv2.5 grids 21893308, the R = 150 NextGen spectra 20547997) are browsable in the Zenodo community https://zenodo.org/communities/exozippy -- a link for people; the code pins record ids and md5s, never the community. The SED prose cites the tables as `Eastman:2026bc` (`latex/references.bib`). Publishing a new version of the set -- new filters, a longer Av axis, the HPC full-resolution rebuild (review 2.9.13) -- means uploading it as a new version of the same Zenodo concept record, updating that one registry entry and running `tests/test_zenodo_assets.py` (see `docs/testing.md`); every cache is keyed by md5, so the old copies are simply never matched again. The rules, each of which is there for a reason:
- **Fetched per facility, by the readers.** `bc_grid.ensure_bc_tables` is called by `locate_bc_column` and `find_bc_table` (one facility -- `build_bc_grid`, `peek_grid_axes` and `SED._collect_band_filters` go through the first) and by `peek_grid_axes` (the requested facilities, or every published table when it globs the directory), so a 2MASS + Gaia fit fetches two tables, not fourteen. A facility with no published table falls through to the reader's own "not calculated" error, as before.
- **Only into the DEFAULT root.** An explicit `model_root:` is the caller's own tree, used as given and never fetched into -- the rule `mist_grid._resolve_grid_file` applies to the MIST grids.
- **A table that differs from the pin RAISES, it is not re-fetched -- unless it is a PREVIOUS published version.** `fetch_assets` re-downloads a wrong-size destination because the only way it gets that way is a truncated download; here the generators (`generate_NextGen_BC_Tables.py`, `make_bc.py`) MERGE into these very files, so a mismatch may be hours of generation work. The one mismatch that is not a local edit is a checkout that fetched the record's earlier version before the pin moved: the registry entry lists each table's md5s in superseded versions (`ZenodoRecord.superseded`, filled when the record is re-versioned -- 2026-10-20's logg 5.5 version was the first), and `bc_tables._check_present` fetches the pinned table over such a file instead of telling every user to move theirs aside (the old file is a hard link into the machine cache, so unlinking it leaves the cache alone). The record also carries the publish script's `MANIFEST.txt`; it is pinned, since the pins describe the whole record, but it is not a table and is never fetched. A present table is checked against size and md5 once per process and a mismatch names the file and both ways out (publish it and re-pin, or move it aside). Silently using it would score a fit on a table nobody can reproduce from the record. The generators pass `allow_local_changes=True` and fetch absent tables BEFORE planning -- otherwise a fresh checkout would write a facility's table from scratch holding only the columns asked for, dropping every other published column.
- **Offline is explicit.** An absent table that cannot be fetched raises `RuntimeError` naming the file, the record and how to pre-fetch: `exozippy-fetch-bc-tables` (`bc_tables.main`; `bc_tables.fetch_all()` from Python) fetches the whole set into the package tree and the machine cache, after which every checkout on that machine works with no network. It is a RuntimeError and deliberately NOT a FileNotFoundError, because `SED._inject_grid_bounds` and `SED._collect_band_filters` read FileNotFoundError as "this model has no tables" (no column) and carry on -- a failed download would have become a fit that quietly lost its grid bounds. There is no fallback to any other copy.
- **The legacy per-[Fe/H] text tables (`BCs/{FACILITY}/feh*_afe+0.0.{FACILITY}`, ~120 MB) were deleted, not moved**, with `convert_legacy_BC_tables.py`: nothing in the package read them since the parquet conversion, and the three `examples/DC2018` scripts that still parsed them (photon-weighted pre-#335 values, so already drifted from what the fit uses) now read the published parquet through `dc18_common.nextgen_bc_slice`.
- **Guards against a re-add.** The tables are git-ignored by name (`.gitignore`, which also keeps them out of the wheel: poetry builds from what git does not ignore), and `tests/test_tracked_file_sizes.py` fails on ANY tracked file over 1 MB under `src/exozippy/models`, whatever it is called.
- **Tests and CI.** `tests/conftest.py` fetches the whole set once, in the controller, before any test (`_prewarm_zenodo_records`, which fetches every registered Zenodo record through its owner's `fetch_all()`; this module's is `bc_tables.fetch_all`) -- the per-test fixture switches the machine cache off, so without it a fresh worktree would download inside the first SED test, and a Zenodo outage would fail the pre-push suite (review 2.13.4). CI caches the machine download cache with actions/cache, keyed on a hash of `utilities/zenodo_assets.py` (`docs/testing.md`, "Model data from Zenodo, and the CI download cache"). Tests: `tests/test_bc_tables_zenodo.py` -- including one real download of the smallest table from the pinned record (skipped only when Zenodo is unreachable).

**The band average is weighted by the filter's SVO `DetectorType`, not by one rule for all filters.** SVO computes a filter's Vega zeropoint energy-weighted (int F S dlam) for an energy counter (`DetectorType` 0: Gaia, TESS, WISE, Bessell, Cousins) and photon-weighted (int F lam S dlam) for a photon counter (`DetectorType` 1: 2MASS, NIRC2), so the synthetic flux must be averaged the same way before it is divided by that zeropoint. The pre-parquet tables photon-weighted every filter. Against the MIST BC tables ([Fe/H] = 0, Av = 0, 3500 - 9000 K) that puts WISE_W3 off by 0.18 mag, Gaia_G by 0.07 - 0.16 mag and TESS by 0.08 - 0.10 mag, where the detector-weighted values agree with MIST to 0.01 - 0.04 mag. `models/NextGen/bolometric_correction.py` applies the rule (`weighting="detector"`); each column's `filter_meta["flux_weighting"]` records what was used. `make_bc.py` still photon-weights every filter.

**`model_root:` must reach every consumer, and two of them used to ignore it.** `SED._ensure_model_data` passed `DEFAULT_MODEL_ROOT` to `make_bc.ensure_model_data`, which takes a root -- so a configured root downloaded 259 MB of spectra into a directory nothing reads and then downloaded them again. And the model's plot class was imported through the fixed dotted namespace `exozippy.models.<model>.BCs.plot`, which can only resolve inside the installed package: `sed.load_model_plot_module` keeps the dotted import for a path inside the package (so module identity matches a normal import) and spec-loads anything else, caching it in `sys.modules`. `sed.plot_class_from` then selects the one **subclass of `components.sed.plot.Plot`** defined in the module, raising on zero or several -- the old ast-parse-and-take-the-first-`ClassDef` silently instantiated a helper class defined above it. `plot.Plot` reads the spectra, the wavelength grid and the grid yaml under `self.model_root`; its fall back to the packaged NextGen spectra survives but is an explicit `is_file()` test plus a warning saying the *figure's* curve is a NextGen spectrum while the fit's BCs were not, in place of a bare `except:` + `print()`. Tests: `tests/test_sed_model_root.py`.

**On-the-fly BC generation from the R = 150 spectra is DISABLED (2026-09-28, JDE); synthesis from the FULL-resolution spectra replaced it (2026-11).** `build_bc_grid` used to call `make_bc.generate_missing_facility` for a filter with no shipped column and synthesize one from the R = 150 NextGen spectra that were only ever made for plotting -- a column ~2 percent wrong, larger than most surveys' photometric errors. Once PR #335 rebuilt the shipped tables from the full-resolution spectra (and the SED's `errscale` improved markedly for it), a silently synthesized R = 150 column became misleading and dangerous, so that hook was removed and a missing filter refused. The full-resolution atmospheres are ~250 GB and no user should download those to add one filter (JDE 2026-09-17) -- but their step-1 product, the spectra resampled to R = 20000 (3.3 GB as float32, Zenodo record `nextgen_spectra_r20000`), is published since #495, and BCs recomputed from it match the published tables to 8e-10 mag. So a column the published tables lack is now SYNTHESIZED from it, for a band filter whose flux something reads, by the same step-2 code that made the published columns (`models/NextGen/bc_synthesis.py`; "Which band filters need a BC column" below). `build_bc_grid` itself still synthesizes nothing: it reads published and synthesized columns alike (`bc_grid.locate_bc_column`) and refuses one in neither (`_refuse_missing`, which names the request path). `make_bc.py` survives only as a MANUAL development tool (`scripts/make_bc_tables.py`); its columns say in their metadata which pipeline made them. Tests: `tests/test_bc_grid_refuses_missing.py`, `tests/test_bc_synthesis.py`.

The better answer, unexplored and possibly not permitted: a SERVICE hosted next to the models
that computes and serves a requested BC table without the caller ever holding the underlying
grid. That would also dissolve the large-`av` problem -- extending the Av axis would stop being
a repository-wide regeneration and become a query -- and the Rv axis with it. Whether Harvard
IT supports hosting such a thing is an open question and not a software one; recorded here so
the option is not rediscovered as novel.

### Which band filters need a BC column (2026-11)

**Only a band whose SED flux something READS must have a BC column** (`SED._collect_band_filters`). Every `band:` block's filter used to go into the grid build, so a band used only for its filter identity or limb darkening failed the fit when the published tables lacked its column -- a microlensing config whose MOA band had no stated zeropoint stopped working the day #495 shipped a MOA table holding only `MOA-II.R`. Now, per distinct band filter:

- **a column exists** (published, or synthesized earlier into the per-user cache): it goes in, read or not, so predicted magnitudes, a light curve's REPORTED zeropoint and the outputs keep working;
- **no column, and its flux is read**: it goes in, and its column is synthesized before the grid build (below). Dropping it instead would silently lose the constraint the band exists to carry -- the mulensing zeropoint tie, the transit dilution, the astrometry fluxfrac (review 2.9.6);
- **no column, and nothing reads its flux**: left out, with one INFO line saying so. A light curve in such a band then has no zeropoint element, and mulensinstrument says so at INFO, not WARNING: no constraint is lost;
- **a label with no SVO identity at all** -- neither in the alias table nor SVO-shaped (`examples/gj1214`'s `MIRILRS`): skipped with a WARNING, as before, since there is no bandpass to compute a column from.

**Who reads a band is DECLARED by the reader, through a generic hook** -- `Component.sed_flux_bands(system)`, a set of band names, default empty (`components/components.md`, "The declared extension API"). The SED asks every component (`SED.consumed_sed_bands`) and knows none of them by name. The SED builds its grid at stage 1, before any other component's stage 1 may have run, so every answer comes from construction-time state: raw config, `__init__` bookkeeping and the params file. The readers today:

- `mulensinstrument` -- a light curve whose zeropoint the user STATED (`mu` and `sigma`, numbers or links; the zeropoint tie, `sed_tie_mask` without the prediction mask the grid itself decides), or one with `sed_constrains_blend: true`. A light curve with neither only reports its zeropoint, which needs no column.
- `background` -- every band of an instance whose flux is tied to a light curve (`light_curves:`; the derived flux subtracts its psf_stars' predicted flux there).
- `transit` -- the band of every file with `seddeblend` true or a star list.
- `astrometryinstrument` -- every dataset's `band:`, conservatively (the gaia/abs SED fluxfrac, the psf_stars split and a rel dataset's nested-orbit weighting all read it, and which applies is settled only at stage 3). Over-declaring costs a synthesis; under-declaring would drop a constraint.

`mann`'s Ks tie reads the SED only where the `.sed` file already carries 2MASS Ks (`has_filter`), so it declares nothing. A new reader of `predict_*_appmag` / `predict_flux_fraction*` in a band must override the hook, or a band only it reads would be left out of the grid; its own `has_filter` check would then refuse or skip, as for any band without a column.

**Synthesis** (`models/NextGen/bc_synthesis.py`, called by `SED._synthesize_missing_columns` before `build_bc_grid`). For each read band filter with no column it runs step 2 of `models/NextGen/README.md` -- `generate_bc_tables`, with its padding of the grid nodes no spectrum exists for -- for that one filter, on the published R = 20000 spectra, which it downloads on first use (the record is ON DEMAND in `utilities/zenodo_assets.py`, so no prewarm or CI cache fetches it; it lands in `<machine cache>/nextgen_spectra_r20000`, hard-linked from the md5-keyed download cache). Which nodes to pad is read from the bundle itself (a complete step-1 product: a node it lacks is one SVO has no spectrum for), so no SVO index is fetched. The column -- with the published pipeline's own `GENERATOR`/`SPECTRA_TAG` provenance and padding record -- is merged into a per-user cache that mirrors the model tree, `<machine cache>/bc_synthesized/NextGen/BCs/{FACILITY}.bc.parquet` (`bc_grid.synthesized_model_root`), and its native system and m_AB - m_Vega offset, computed exactly as `write_magsys_table` computes a published column's, into that root's `NextGen.magsys.csv`. The rules:

- **Never into the package tree.** The published tables are pinned by md5 (`bc_tables.py`); a fit that merged a column into one would then refuse it. So synthesis needs the machine cache (`EXOZIPPY_CACHE_DIR` / `$XDG_CACHE_HOME/exozippy`) and raises naming the switch when it is off. It applies only to the NextGen tables in the DEFAULT model root; an explicit `model_root:` is the caller's own tree, and a read filter missing there is refused by the grid build with the request path.
- **Read seamlessly, published first.** `locate_bc_column` finds a column in the published table, then in the cache; every reader (`build_bc_grid`, `peek_grid_axes`, the SED's magnitude-system lookup, which reads the record beside whichever table holds the column) goes through it. A published column wins if a later table version ships the filter. A cached column counts only while CURRENT (`cached_column_is_current`: this pipeline's provenance, every node of the grid yaml), so a grid extended since, or a newer pipeline, re-synthesizes it rather than reading a stale one.
- **The magnitude system is STATED, never guessed.** A synthesized filter is by definition not in `NATIVE_SYSTEM`, and its record needs a native system for every AB light curve or row in it, so the band must say `mag_system: Vega` or `mag_system: AB` (case-sensitive, `magsys.parse_magsys`); without it `_synthesis_native_system` raises naming the band and the key. On a column that already has a record, a stated `mag_system:` must match it (`_check_band_mag_systems` raises otherwise) -- the key declares the native system of a synthesized column and nothing else.
- **Concurrency.** A file lock per (facility, filter) serializes two fits that need the same column: the second waits (an INFO line every few minutes), then finds the column current and reads it. The merges into the shared facility table and the shared magsys record hold locks of their own; both are written beside and replaced, so a reader never sees half a file.
- **Loud, and in the paper.** The synthesis logs a WARNING naming the filter and the cache path (the fit now reads a correction outside the published, centrally checked tables, and it cost a download and minutes of compute -- worth seeing in every log that made one); a later fit reading the cached column says so at INFO. Either way `SED.synthesized_filters` lists it and the draft says, citing both records, that those corrections were computed for the fit from the R = 20000 spectra by the published tables' procedure (`_add_synthesized_prose`).

Tests: `tests/test_bc_synthesis.py` (a real step-2 run on a fake bundle round-tripping through the cache; the lock; the INFO skip; the `mag_system` raise; published and cached columns in one grid; the prewarm skipping the record), `tests/test_sed_band_filters.py`.

**Filter profiles are read from the package and written to the machine cache.** `filters/filter.py` used to write its downloaded `.filter` pickles into the installed package directory -- a `PermissionError` on a read-only site-packages and source-tree litter in a dev checkout, and its `os.makedirs` was unconditional, so even READING a shipped profile created a facility directory there. Reads now try the shipped package directory first (25 profiles ship) and `filter_cache_root()` -- the `$XDG_CACHE_HOME/exozippy/filters` sibling of `utilities/zenodo.py`'s download cache, honouring the same `EXOZIPPY_CACHE_DIR` switch -- second; a download goes to the cache, falling back to the package directory when the cache is switched off (which is what "off" meant before it existed) and to a temp directory when neither is writable. A `.filter` file stores `Filter._file_state()`: no `requests.Session` and no `filterDirectory` (it is restored from where the file was found on read), so a shipped profile carries nothing of the machine that wrote it. The profiles shipped before that rule (all but `GAIA/GAIA3.*`, added for mkticsed's DR3 rows) still carry a developer path and a pickled session; reading them is unaffected, and regenerating them is a separate change. The SVO zeropoints those downloads scrape are validated before they are believed: HTTP status, table count, that the rows read are LABELLED as zeropoints, that the unit cell names the expected quantity, and that each value is `--` or a finite positive float. Every failure raises, and raising is the point -- the scrape happens inside `_set_attrs`, before `_create_filter_file`, so refusing is refusing to cache a wrong number forever. Tests: `tests/test_filter_cache.py`, `tests/test_filter_svo_zeropoints.py`. `bc_grid._load_alias_table` and `plot._read_spectra_csv` are both cached on `(path, mtime, size)` -- the 250 MB spectra table was re-read and re-json-parsed (5.5 s) on every `Plot` construction; tests `tests/test_filter_alias_cache.py`, `tests/test_sed_spectra_cache.py`.

Cross-component hooks when a `sed:` block exists: `mulensinstrument` ties each light curve's `f_source` to the SED-predicted source mag through a per-lightcurve `zeropoint` -- only where the user states its prior (mu AND sigma; no default since review 2.2.21) and in the light curve's `magsys:` (#313); `sed_constrains_blend: true` opts f_blend in; `transit` constrains each seddeblend file's `dilute` parameter with a Gaussian centered on the SED-predicted flux fraction of the other stars in the band (width from `seddeblend_floor`, EXOFASTv2's 2% per-star flux error) and starts it there (`predict_flux_fraction_at`, a numeric twin of `predict_flux_fraction` for stage 3) -- the SED constrains the dilution, it does not set it (see "Transit dilution" in `components/instrument.md`); `astrometryinstrument` derives its photocenter `fluxfrac` from the SED when given `band:` + `companion_star_ndx:`.

`zeropoint` is an ordinary **derived** `Parameter` (`mulensinstrument.zeropoint`, one element per light curve, `force_node: True`), not a hand-built node: `physics.calc_zeropoint` is its expression and `Parameter.build_pymc`'s derived-with-sigma branch supplies the Gaussian. **Derived, never sampled** -- `zp = m_SED + 2.5*log10(f_source)` is determined exactly by f_source and the SED, and no data constrains it separately, so sampling it would add a dimension identified only by its own prior. Until 2026-08 it was not a Parameter at all: `_build_sed_flux_constraint` resolved the config block itself and read only `mu`/`sigma`, so a user's `initval` (and their `unit:`) were silently inert. The **node layout** changed with it and the content did not: the per-instrument `mulensinstrument.<name>.zeropoint` Deterministics became the one vector `mulensinstrument.zeropoint`, and the per-instrument `.zeropoint_prior` potentials became the one `gaussian_prior.mulensinstrument.zeropoint`. Summing N scalar potentials as one vector instead moves a start logp by ~1 ulp (measured: exactly 1 on KMT-2019-BLG-1806), and nothing else. Two deps are injected as **context nodes** by `MulensInstrument.add_parameter` (`context_dep_names`, the `Orbit` group-mass idiom): `m_source_pred`, the SED forward model on the zeropoint's magnitude system, which cannot be spelled as a manifest dep, and `flux_zp`, the magnitude of unit flux in each light curve's file (`MulensInstrument.flux_system_zp`: 0 for a magnitude or flux file, the recovered `zp_ref` for `data_format: dia`, whose flux is in units of its reference), so `zp = m_SED + 2.5*log10(f_source) - flux_zp` and a dia file and the equivalent magnitude file tie identically (review 2.2.22, `components/mulensing/mulensing.md`). A light curve with no `band:` or whose filter is missing from the BC grid has nothing to tie to, and its zeropoint element is INACTIVE (manifest `mask` + `inactive_value`; `m_source_pred` is an `aligned_context_deps` entry, so the expression is sliced to the elements with a prediction). Until review 2.2.21 such an element reported the prior center through a `pt.switch` on a 0/1 `sed_constrained` mask (with `zp_center`, two more context nodes); with no default center that would have reported a number nobody stated. The Gaussian applies only where the user stated a sigma, element by element (`build_pymc`'s mask needs a finite sigma), which is what makes the tie the user's. `sigma: 0` still raises (in `_zeropoint_context`) rather than falling through to `build_pymc`'s generic "sigma=0 has no effect on a derived parameter" warning. Because the SED forward model is read through `predict_*` rather than declared as a dep, `SED._predicted_appmag_node` lazily materializes the star Parameters it needs (`SED._ensure_star_nodes`) -- the zeropoint expression is built at stage 6 and can get there first.


### The PHOENIX limb-darkening tables (`band.ld_source: phoenix`)

The tie has a second table source since 2026-10-20: tables EXOZIPPy generates itself from the PHOENIX-ACES specific intensities (Husser et al. 2013) -- `models/PHOENIX/` (the generator, which streams the archive node by node and never rehosts it; `README.md` there has the physics) and `components/band/ldgrid.py` (the band side: lookup through the filter alias table, the complete-box loader, the numpy and PyTensor interpolations). `ld_source: claret` stays the default, so every shipped fit is unchanged. What differs from Claret, and why:

- **Same filters, weighting and extinction law as the bolometric corrections.** The band-integrated profile `I_band(mu; A_V)` is formed through `bolometric_correction._filter_set_data`: SVO's profiles on the common R = 20000 grid, the `DetectorType` weighting, `extinction_law.ascii` at R_V = 3.1 -- so the limb darkening and the SED flux of one star come from one description of the band. Every filter the BC tables ship inside 500-26000 A has a column (WISE and Johnson M do not); a band with no column raises, naming the remedies, exactly like a filter with no Claret table.
- **A_V is a fourth axis.** Reddening tilts the weighting inside a band toward the red, where limb darkening is weaker; at A_V ~ 1 that is ~0.01 in u and ignorable, on bulge sight lines and in Roman's wide bands it reaches the tie's 0.05 width (notes 2026-08-04). The table is read at the star's `av` when the star has one -- an `sed:` block in the topology -- and bounds it to the table's A_V range; without one it is read at A_V = 0 and the draft says so.
- **The box is the table's complete box, not a constant.** `LDTable.box` comes from `bc_grid.complete_axes` over the filter's own columns, so a facility whose table is still ragged is bounded on what it has; the archive reaches 2300 K and logg 6, so the 3500 K floor and 5.0 ceiling that kept every M dwarf out of the Claret tie are gone. Interior holes of the archive (5000 K at several metallicities) are filled by interpolating the band intensities along Teff, recorded in `meta["padding"]`.
- **The fit is quasi-spherical and flux-conserving** (`generate_PHOENIX_LD_Tables.fit_profile`; Claret, Hauschildt & Witte 2012 for the limb criterion), so the coefficients are NOT Claret's least-squares ones: measured over 30 (band, node) pairs at A_V = 0 (`scripts/compare_ld_sources.py`; `models/PHOENIX/README.md`), u1 + u2 agrees with Claret to -0.012 +/- 0.025 and the linear u to -0.025 +/- 0.043, inside the tie's 0.05 width, while u1 and u2 separately differ by ~0.08 with opposite signs -- the flux-conserving-versus-least-squares split; a fit may not mix sources on one band, and the modeling draft cites `Husser:2013` and `Claret:2012` for a PHOENIX-tied band, `Claret:2011`/`Claret:2017` for a Claret-tied one.
- **The band side is one code path with a per-table axis order.** `Band._table_axes` / `_table_box` / `_interpolate_numpy` / `_interpolate_node` dispatch on the table object (`ClaretTable`, three axes; `LDTable`, four), so `_bound_tied_stars`, the start check and seed, the context nodes and the prose all serve either; a star read by two tables gets the intersection of their boxes. Tests: `tests/test_phoenix_ld.py` (the fit on a synthetic law and a synthetic cliff, the hole filling, the loader on a linear synthetic table, and the tie end to end at A_V = 0).

## The sight-line dust profile (`sed: {distance_constrains_av: ...}`; review 8.9.8)

**What it does.** Every object the SED reads -- each read star and each SED flux source (the red clump) -- has ONE extinction law along ONE sight line, so its A_V is a single monotone function of distance evaluated at its own distance, not a free number: `star.av = A(d) (1 + av_frac)`. The physics is in `dust_profile.py`, the SED side (plan, parameters, graph builders, seeding, figure, prose) in `sight_line_dust.py`. Why it matters: toward the bulge A_I is 1.6-6 mag, so where a LENS sits along the sight line moves its I-band flux by a factor 4-250; a free lens A_V let the SED polish of KB210457/OB120690 run the lens to A_V ~ 10, past the source's. JDE 2026-10-26 overruled the 08-27 "nothing more sophisticated" scope cut (review 8.9.8); the rulings of 2026-11-09 are why each piece below is shaped as it is.

**When it is on** (`SED.dust_plan`, the ONE answer Star, the flux sources and the SED all ask; from the topology, never from a start). ON by default when the objects the SED reads sit at two or more DIFFERENT distances: a read star counts once per distance group (an orbit group's members are their anchor's, `Star.orbit_anchors`; a params-file hard link `star.<k>.distance: {initval: star.<j>.distance, sigma: 0}` -- a microlensing second source -- joins k to j), each flux source once. `sed: {distance_constrains_av: false}` (the tie toggle, one spelling) returns every A_V to a free parameter and is PR 2's model exactly; `true` turns it on for a single object too (a map total as the anchor). There is NO bulge/off-bulge switch: the prior shape is the exponential dust layer everywhere; only the ANCHOR differs. The sight line is the galactic model's when there is one (`GalacticModel.sight_line`), else the read stars' stated positions, which must agree to 60 arcsec (it raises otherwise: not one sight line). With no stated position (a transit blend with no ra/dec) there is no Galactic latitude and so no layer: the profile is OFF with an INFO line, and `distance_constrains_av: true` raises naming the missing position. It raises on the Galactic plane itself (|sin b| < 1e-4: the layer has no vertical extent).

**The shape.** The smooth layer (scale height `dust_scale_height`, default 164 pc -- Nataf et al. 2013 eq. 21, genulens's own dust; the microlensing standard is the same family, Bennett et al. 2015 eq. 19) puts the fraction s(d) = 1 - exp(-d sin|b|/H) of the column in front of d. The K bins are EQUAL INTERVALS IN s -- each holds the same share of the layer's dust -- so they are dense where the dust is, and with every shape coordinate at its prior center the profile IS the layer exactly, not a piecewise-linear approximation of it: `C(d) = sum_k 10**z_k clip(K s(d) - k, 0, 1)`, `sed.dust_z` ~ N(0, 0.8) dex per bin (Green et al. 2014's log-normal increments about a smooth model; JDE's 0.7-1 dex), bounds +/-6 dex as validity limits. Monotone by construction, clumpy (any bin can hold a cloud, so dust between a lens and a source is representable), continuous and smooth in the sampled distances -- NOT a pairwise ordering of distances, which would be a branch the where-trap rule forbids.

**How many bins** (ruling 14). Default K = the number of SED objects at their own distances, clipped to [3, 6]; `sed: {dust_bins: K}` overrides it (a future "all stars in a cone" use raises it). Why: n objects have n extinctions, one of which the anchor fixes; K bins carry K - 1 free shape coordinates (their common shift cancels in the anchored ratio), so K = n is the most the data can resolve and finer bins only add prior-dominated degeneracy. The floor 3 lets a lens-source pair put most of the dust between them (with K = 2 on Baade's window a 3 kpc lens could never see less than 45% of an 8 kpc source's A_V). `dust_bins: 1` is the smooth layer itself (exactly Bennett's ratio form; `examples/MOA-2008-BLG-379` uses it with `dust_scale_height: 100`).

**The anchor: one sampled total, the bins only shape** (ruling 12). `A(d) = A_anchor C(d) / C(d_anchor)`. `sed.dust_anchor` is sampled directly (DEFAULT_WEAK, so a user's start on any star seeds it): the well-measured total is one axis, not a curved ridge through K log-increments. `d_anchor` is the red clump's SAMPLED distance when a clump is in the fit (an `SEDFluxSource` with `extinction_anchor=True`; at most one); else, with a galactic model, the Galactic bar's MEAN distance on the sight line (`GalacticModel.bar_distance_moments`, the distance a clump block's prior is centered on), a FIXED number, not sampled; else infinity (`C(inf) = sum 10**z_k`: the whole column), with a DEBUG line. The bar, not the column, because a red-clump extinction map (Surot et al. 2020, Zelakiewicz et al. 2025) states the extinction TO THE CLUMP, at the bar, and a source behind the bar sees 3-5% more at |b| ~ 3-4 deg (JDE 2026-11-10). The clump's own `redclump.av = A(d_clump) (1 + redclump.av_frac)`, `av_frac` ~ N(0, 0.09) (Nataf et al. 2013's field-scale granularity: the centroid measures the pencil beam to 9%), which SUPERSEDES PR 2's interim `av_ratio` tie (kept only with the profile off; `clump_constrains_source_av: true` under the profile raises).

**The anchor's band** (`sed: {dust_anchor_band: <filter>}`). Absent: the anchor IS A_V (no conversion; every shipped example). With a filter (a NIR map's 2MASS Ks), `sed.dust_anchor` is the extinction in THAT band, integrated through the passband on a reference red-clump spectrum (Teff 4700 K, logg 2.5, [Fe/H] 0 -- the giants the maps are built from) at the fit's law, so a map value is a prior natively with no hand conversion and, under `fit_rv_dust`, a tight A_Ks no longer makes A_V and R_V a curved ridge. `sed.av_anchor` (derived, reported) is A_V at the anchor: the band-integrated A_band(A_V) inverted on the SED's OWN grid (`bc_node`, table or spectral path) by three fixed-point steps from the A_V = 1 ratio, converging to < 1e-5 (the ratio A_band/A_V varies by a few percent over A_V 1-8). The filter must have a BC column (it is requested through `sed_flux_filters`). Why A_V is the default and not A_I: it keeps every shipped example bit-identical, it is the grid's own axis, and every per-star `av` the BC path consumes is A_V.

**Each object's own coordinate.** `star.av_frac` ~ N(0, 0.03) per read star (Sale 2012's per-star term; JDE's 0.02-0.05), bounds [-0.9, 10] as validity limits (A_V > 0). THE one spelling of the width is its sigma, `star.<name>.av_frac: {sigma: 0.05}`. Under the profile `av_frac` joins the `STAR_KNOBS` (pinned and inactive on unread stars, as av), counts in `PHOTOMETRIC_DOF` in place of av, and the errscale count adds the anchor once (and under the split the near R_V and the boundary); the z_k are prior-only shape and are not counted. An orbit group links `av_frac` instead of `av` (`Star.orbit_linked_params`: a derived member would raise). A derived av has no lower grid barrier (A(d) > 0 by construction; a barrier at 0 would tax a nearby lens), and keeps the grid's upper one, as `loggsed` does.

**A user's statements on a derived av** (the role flip, parameter.md). A `{mu, sigma}` prior is kept as a Gaussian on the derived value, bounds as soft barriers; `sigma: 0` RAISES (pinning a derived element does nothing: pin `sed.dust_anchor` and `av_frac`); ANY link on a read star's av RAISES (the profile already relates them; the remedy names `dust_bins: 1`). An `initval` (else a `mu`) on a read star's av seeds the anchor: `SED._seed_dust_anchor` divides it by the layer shape at the probed start distances (the selected source's first) and pushes a DERIVED_USER hint -- never a `user_params` write -- and `reread_after_starts` redoes it once if the solve moved those distances, so `check_user_starts` is clean.

**R_V mixing: the near/far split** (`sed: {fit_rv_dust_near: true}`, ruling 13). Only under `fit_rv_dust` (the tables have no R_V; it raises otherwise). CCM is linear in 1/R_V (A_lambda/A_V = a + b/R_V), so dust from two populations sums EXACTLY to one CCM curve at `1/R_eff(d) = f(d)/R_near + (1 - f(d))/R_far`, `f(d) = C(min(d, d_b))/C(d)` the share of the dust in front of d that lies in front of the boundary (min of two clips: a finite gradient; f in (0, 1], no floor). `sed.rv_dust` is the far (inner-Galaxy, clump-constrained) R_V, `sed.rv_dust_near` the disk dust's, `sed.dust_boundary` the boundary, SAMPLED on U(3, 6) kpc. Every read star's `star.rv_dust` and the clump's `redclump.rv_dust` become `from_dust_profile` and are table rows (ruling 7: per-object R_V rows only where they differ). ON by default when an object is KNOWN to be nearer than the far group -- a read star outside the distance group of a selected source (`Component.selected_sources`: a microlensing lens with light) -- and OFF otherwise: a lone source sees only the integrated column, where one R_V describes it. Why ship it rather than wait: one R_V for the whole column asserts with zero uncertainty that the lens's foreground dust has the bulge law; at fixed A_V, A_I/A_V is 0.53 at R_V 1.96 against 0.63 at 3.3, ~0.2 mag in I for a lens behind A_V ~ 2 (JDE 2026-11-09: "is our lack of decision a decision itself, and a worse one?").

The near prior is the diffuse ISM's, Schlafly et al. (2016, ApJ 821, 78): their R'(V) proxy (eq. 8, `1.2 E(g_P1 - W2)/E(g_P1 - r_P1) - 1.18`, calibrated on Fitzpatrick 1999) is a Gaussian of mean 3.32 and standard deviation 0.18 over their APOGEE sight lines (sec. 5.4, Fig. 15). Their R' is NOT this family's R_V (they note CCM's prediction of the ratio differs from F99's by up to 0.5), so it was mapped by computing the SAME proxy through our spectral path on their reference giant (4500 K, logg 2.5, [Fe/H] 0) at A_V = 2 (their Fig. 14's): R' = 3.32 is R_V = 2.88, and 3.32 -/+ 0.18 is 2.70 / 3.07, so `rv_dust_near` ~ N(2.88, 0.19) on [1.5, 6] (R_V = 2.92 at A_V = 1, 2.81 at A_V = 4: weakly A_V-dependent). The caveat stands and is in the draft: the mixing makes the law consistent along the line of sight within the CCM family, not right (Nataf et al. 2016's >= 2 degrees of freedom; one CCM R_V cannot match the bulge's optical and NIR ratios at once).

**Gradient safety.** No `pt.where` anywhere; ramps are `pt.clip` (finite gradient both sides, a slope jump at each edge like any piecewise-linear interpolator); `C(d) >= 10**z_0 K s(d) > 0` for d > 0 and every distance's lower bound is strictly positive, so there is no division by zero and no floor (if one is ever added it must be strictly positive, the ECC_FLOOR rule). C and JAX logp/dlogp are finite and agree on every edge, at d = 0.001 pc, far past the layer and at z = +/-6 (`tests/test_dust_profile.py`).

**Outputs.** `sed.dust_z` is suppressed from the LaTeX table (`print_to_table: false`) and kept in `results.csv`; `sed.dust_anchor` (and `av_anchor`), each object's derived av and its av_frac are rows. The figure `<prefix>_dust_profile.pdf` (a `Chart` from `dust_profile_charts`, rendered by plotrender, compiled off the SAME `profile_av_node` the forward model reads) shows the posterior median and 68% range of A_V(d) with each SED object at its median distance and A_V and their 68% ranges; under the split `<prefix>_dust_rv.pdf` shows R_V,eff(d). The draft gets one sentence for the profile and one for the split with its caveat.

Tests: `tests/test_dust_profile.py`; the synthetic lens-behind-a-cloud recovery is `tests/test_dust_profile_recovery.py` (slow).

## The spectral mode: a fitted R_V (`sed: {fit_rv_dust: true}`)

**What it does.** The default path interpolates tabulated BCs in (Teff, logg, [Fe/H], A_V) at the tables' one extinction law. `fit_rv_dust: true` computes the BCs from the spectrum at every evaluation instead (`spectral_bc.py`): the R = 3000 runtime grid (`models/NextGen/runtime_spectra.py`, `models/NextGen/README.md`, "The runtime spectral grid") interpolated trilinearly in log10 F over (teffsed, loggsed, feh), reddened at each bin centre with the Cardelli, Clayton & Mathis (1989) law with O'Donnell's (1994) optical coefficients, A_lambda/A_V = a + b/R_V (`extinction.ccm89_ab`), at the star's own `av` and `rv_dust`, and integrated through each filter with the tables' formula, profiles, DetectorType weighting and Vega zeropoints (`bolometric_correction._filter_set_data`). Everything downstream of the BC -- the magnitudes, blends, the mulensing zeropoint, dilution, fluxfrac -- is unchanged, and `fit_rv_dust: false` (the default) is bit-identical to the table path. A YAML boolean or absent; anything else raises.

**What stays on the tables.** Every filter still needs a BC-table column in this mode, published or synthesized (above): the column names it, carries its magnitude-system record (`_convert_to_bc_system`) and supplies the grid bounds (`_inject_grid_bounds`), so a filter the published tables lack goes through the same synthesis as in table mode before the spectral path is built. `SpectralBC` is built on the same filter list and must produce the same column order (it raises otherwise). The A_V bound stays the tables' 0-20 though the spectral path has no A_V axis.

**One sight line, one R_V: `sed.rv_dust`** (review 8.9.8; JDE 2026-11-09). Declared by the SED only under `fit_rv_dust`: ONE sampled parameter, uniform on [1.5, 6.0] with no default Gaussian, start 3.1 (`sed/defaults.yaml`). Not `rv`: that is the systemic radial velocity. A star's `star.rv_dust` (declared by Star under `fit_rv_dust`, `sed_fits_rv_dust`) is DERIVED from it (`from_sightline`, `sed.rv_dust` broadcast through the aligned context node `sightline_rv_dust` -- a cross-component scalar in a mixed-role expression must be one, parameter.md) on every star the SED reads, and INACTIVE on the rest; `structural_prior: true` closes its `mu`/`sigma` (a prior on N derived copies of one number would count it N times; the remedy names `sed.rv_dust`), an initval stays open and seeds the sight line through the engine relation `star.<i>.rv_dust = sed.0.rv_dust` (`sed/symbolic_physics.get_extra_relations`, only under `fit_rv_dust`), and it prints no table row (`print_to_table: false`) while it repeats `sed.rv_dust`. It stays a star parameter because the dust profile of review 8.9.8 (PR 3) makes it genuinely per star, "the R_V of the dust in front of this star", with an optional near/far split. Why the SED owns it: the SED owns the extinction LAW (this mode, `extinction.py`, `spectral_bc.py`), it is a singleton, and it exists whenever any `av` does; links among stars would make the user's prior spelling depend on which star owns the value, and a lens and a source on one sight line used to float independent R_V. So rv_dust is NOT a per-star SED knob any more: `STAR_KNOBS` and `PHOTOMETRIC_DOF` are the table mode's (an unread star's rv_dust is inactive by the manifest mask, not the knob pin), the errscale count adds ONE for `sed.rv_dust` when any row reads any source (`data_free_inventory`; one star needs 6 rows, as before), and it left `Star.ORBIT_LINKED_PARAMS` (`_link_orbit_members` raises on a derived member element); `_STAR_NODE_DEPS` and `FLUX_FRACTION_INPUTS` keep it (the forward model and the dilution start solver read each star's value, which the engine supplies). An SED flux source reads its own R_V parameter (the red clump's `redclump.rv_dust`, derived from `sed.rv_dust`, or under the dust profile's split from the mix in front of it). The figure reddens with the same law at each source's R_V (`plot.Plot._normalize_optical_depth`).

**The bounds, and what "R_V" means here.** In this band-integrated CCM89 + O'Donnell family the bulge's mean A_I/E(V-I) = 1.217 (Nataf et al. 2013) is R_V ~ 1.98 (A_I/E(V-I) at the clump, Teff 4700 K, logg 2.5: 1.743 at R_V 3.1, 1.496 at 2.5, 1.229 at 2.0, 1.106 at 1.8). The old [2, 6] put the bulge at the wall; a + b/R_V stays positive at every filter wavelength down to R_V ~ 1.0 (0.22 at 1 micron and R_V 1.5), so [1.5, 6] is safe, though below CCM's fitted 2.6 it is extrapolation either way. **"R_V ~ 2.5 (Nataf)" is R_V in Nataf's own mapping, not this family's**: as a prior on `sed.rv_dust` it would bias A_I/E(V-I) high by ~23% (1.49 vs 1.22). And one CCM R_V cannot match both the optical and the NIR clump colors (at R_V 2.0, E(J-Ks)/E(V-I) = 0.297 where Nataf's relation wants 0.343): with V, I, J and Ks clump rows the residual lands in errscale and the stars' Teff (the NIR slope is review 8.9.2). With a red clump in the fit, no sight-line R_V prior from a map built from clump colors may be added (it counts the clump twice).

**It is not the tables at R_V = 3.1.** The shipped law (`extinction_law.ascii`) and CCM89 at 3.1 differ by up to ~0.1 mag in a band at A_V = 8 (the table runs ~6% high in J, H, Ks), so switching `fit_rv_dust` on moves a reddened star even where R_V ends at 3.1. That is the law changing, not the machinery: with the shipped law plugged in (`SpectralBC(..., law="shipped")`, reachable from no config) the spectral path reproduces the tables at their nodes to 0.69 mmag or better on all 93 filters (`scripts/validate_runtime_grid.py`).

**Why it is worth it, and what it costs** (feasibility study 2026-11-07, `models/NextGen/README.md`). Between R_V = 2.5 and 3.1 the BCs move by a median 30, 90 and 230 mmag at A_V = 1, 3 and 8 (Gaia G 0.34 mag at A_V = 8): on a reddened sight line the law is the dominant systematic. The tables' own A_V interpolation is also off by up to 24 mmag in the A_V 2-4 cell, which this path does not have. Speed, one star, logp + gradient: ~120-130 us for the tables, flat in the number of bands (op dispatch, not arithmetic), against ~370-450 us here at ~4000 bins -- set by the bins the filters touch, not by the band count. Neither path improves the interpolation in Teff/logg/[Fe/H]; grid spacing is the floor of both.

**What the DC2018 work showed.** The challenge reddened each band monochromatically at its
effective wavelength with a CCM-like R_V = 3.1 law (its A_Z087/A_W149 = 1.92 for all 293 events
is CCM at 0.87 vs 1.46 um); our tables integrate the reddened spectrum through the bandpass,
which for a band as wide as W149 shifts the effective wavelength redward for a cool reddened
star and lowers the extinction per unit A_V. Read off the key's own lenses (Teff, logg, R, D
and four magnitudes all given, no free parameter -- `examples/DC2018/dc18_source_chain.py
lens-law`), the sim's A_W149/A_F087 is 0.51 against our 0.45, i.e. 0.65 mag of grey W149
under-extinction on event 194 at the colour-anchored `av`, and theta_star comes out 0.71-0.76x
truth once the galactic prior is right. **Integration is the physics; the monochromatic
treatment is the simulation's shortcut**, and it is not a better bulge law -- the real bulge
wants R_V ~ 2.5 (Nataf+2013, in Nataf's mapping; ~2.0 in this family, above) and a STEEPER NIR power law (alpha ~ 2.0-2.3, Nishiyama+2009)
than CCM's ~1.6, which is the opposite direction from the sim's greyness. Decision (JDE
2026-09-24): no heroics to match the challenge's convention; list it as a caveat, widen the
zeropoint prior appropriately, move on.

**Not done.** The NIR slope as a second law parameter (the bulge wants a steeper power law than CCM's 1.61, Nishiyama et al. 2009), a choice of law family (Fitzpatrick 1999, Gordon et al. 2023), the A_V bound beyond the tables' 20, and a switch reproducing the DC2018 challenge's monochromatic convention for scoring.
